## Introduction

When people describe a house as haunted, one of the most common experiences is hearing scratching, scurrying or light [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}) seemingly coming from inside the walls. The sounds can be unsettling because they suggest a hidden presence moving through spaces that should be empty. In many cases, however, these noises have a biological rather than paranormal source: animals using wall cavities, lofts, chimneys and ceiling voids as shelter. Wildlife agencies, pest-control specialists and building inspectors consistently identify rodents, squirrels, bats, birds and other small animals as frequent causes of mysterious wall sounds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wildlifeillinois.org/solve-wildlife-problems/scratching-noises/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wildlifeillinois.org">[Wildlife Illinois]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wildlifeillinois.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wildlifeillinois.org.</span></span></span>

Within the broader subject of [haunted sounds]({{ 'haunted-sounds/' | relative_url }}), animal activity is important because it can produce noises that seem remarkably human. A squirrel crossing a loft may sound like footsteps overhead. Rats moving through wall cavities can create rhythmic scratching that resembles deliberate tapping. Birds trapped inside a wall may produce sudden thumps and scrapes that feel difficult to explain. Understanding how these animals move and how buildings transmit sound helps explain why an ordinary pest problem can become a convincing haunting story.

## What Is Scratching Inside the Wall?

The most likely explanation depends on the sound's timing, location and character.

Common animals associated with wall and attic noises include:

* **Mice and rats**: Often produce rapid scratching, scurrying and gnawing sounds, particularly after dark when they are most active.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wildlifeillinois.org/solve-wildlife-problems/scratching-noises/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wildlifeillinois.org">[Wildlife Illinois+2northernpest.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wildlifeillinois.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wildlifeillinois.org.</span></span></span>
* **Squirrels**: Can create surprisingly loud running noises, bumps and scrambling sounds. In attics, their movement is often mistaken for a person walking overhead. Daytime activity is a useful clue because squirrels are generally active during daylight hours.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wildlifeillinois.org/solve-wildlife-problems/scratching-noises/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wildlifeillinois.org">[Wildlife Illinois+2Waynes Pest Control]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wildlifeillinois.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wildlifeillinois.org.</span></span></span>
* **Bats**: Usually generate softer rustling, scraping or chirping sounds. ## Why Wall Cavities Make Sounds Feel Paranormal

One reason animal noises become associated with [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) is that buildings are poor sound locators. Wall cavities, ceiling voids and ventilation spaces can channel and amplify sound in unexpected ways.

A scratching noise occurring in one corner of an attic may appear to originate from a bedroom wall several metres away. Hollow stud walls can act like drums, reinforcing particular frequencies and making small movements sound larger and closer than they really are.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/groups/PestHelp/posts/2273369582938196/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What is the sound coming from the wall cavities in your...The stud cavity produces a low boom, just like a big drum. In typical...</span></span></span>

This creates several effects that encourage misinterpretation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Sounds appear to move independently of the animal.
* Noise seems to come from inside solid walls.
* Listeners struggle to identify a consistent location.
* Small animals seem much larger than they are.
* Intermittent activity resembles intentional knocking or pacing.

</div>

These acoustic effects help explain why witnesses often describe an unseen presence moving around a house even when the source is a single animal following the same route each night.

### Why the Sounds Are Often Heard at Night

Many of the species that enter buildings are nocturnal or become more active after sunset. Rats, mice and bats typically move during the quietest hours of the night.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wildlifeillinois.org/solve-wildlife-problems/scratching-noises/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wildlifeillinois.org">[Wildlife Illinois+2wmsmn.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wildlifeillinois.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wildlifeillinois.org.</span></span></span>

The timing matters psychologically as well as biologically. At night:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Background traffic and household noise decrease.
* People are more likely to be listening in silence.
* Sleep disruption increases attention to unusual sounds.
* Darkness removes visual information that might identify the source.

</div>

As a result, noises that would barely register during the day can feel significant and mysterious after midnight.

## Clues That Separate Pests from Settling Noises

Not every sound in a wall indicates an animal. Buildings naturally expand, contract and shift. Distinguishing between wildlife and structural noises often comes down to patterns.

Animal-related sounds tend to show:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Repeated scratching or scurrying.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.instagram.com/p/DTa4B_FDc-v/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: instagram.com">[instagram.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">instagram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rapid pattering, soft scratching, chewing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Often inside walls...- Light scratching or scurrying - Fast, repetitive movement - Mostly at night - Common attic sounds: rapid patterin...</span></span></span>
* Movement that changes location over time.
* Chewing or gnawing noises.
* Activity concentrated at particular hours.
* Associated evidence such as droppings, nests, smells or exterior entry points.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.terminix.com/wildlife/signs-animals-in-home/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: terminix.com">[Terminix+2Rentokil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">terminix.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Signs of Wildlife Activity in Your Walls or AtticsUnusual scratching noises and new holes in your home&#x27;s exterior can all be sign...</span></span></span>

</div>

By contrast, settling noises are more likely to be:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Single pops or creaks.
* Linked to temperature changes.
* Triggered by heating systems or weather.
* Relatively random rather than sustained movement.

</div>

[Observers]({{ 'observers/' | relative_url }}) often report that haunted sounds occur in recognisable patterns. Interestingly, animal behaviour can produce exactly that impression. A rat following the same route through a wall every evening or a squirrel returning to a nest at a predictable hour can create what feels like a repeated supernatural routine.

## Why Animal Sounds Become Haunting Stories

Animal activity sits at an interesting intersection between folklore and ordinary building life. The sounds are real, but the source is hidden. That combination allows imagination to fill in the gaps.

A listener hears scratching from inside a wall where nobody should be. The noise stops when investigated. It returns later from another location. Because wall cavities conceal the cause, the experience feels personal and elusive. In older houses especially, recurring animal activity can become woven into local ghost stories, reinforcing the belief that the building is occupied by something unseen.

For investigators of alleged hauntings, scratching and scurrying noises are therefore among the most important sounds to evaluate first. They are genuine disturbances, often alarming and occasionally destructive, but they are also among the most common examples of how an apparently supernatural sound can emerge from ordinary wildlife sharing the hidden spaces of a house.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wildlifeillinois.org/solve-wildlife-problems/scratching-noises/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wildlifeillinois.org">[Wildlife Illinois+2Terminix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wildlifeillinois.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wildlifeillinois.org.</span></span></span>
