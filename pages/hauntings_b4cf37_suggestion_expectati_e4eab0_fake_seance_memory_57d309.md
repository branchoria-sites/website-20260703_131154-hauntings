## Introduction

One of the most striking findings in the psychology of [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) comes not from a haunted house but from a fake séance. In a series of controlled experiments, researchers showed that people could come away remembering a table moving or levitating even when it had remained completely still. The studies are important because they isolate a key ingredient often present in haunting reports: suggestion. Rather than asking whether ghosts are real, the experiments tested how [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}), belief and ambiguous observation shape memory after an unusual event. The results suggest that under the right conditions, people may sincerely remember movement that never occurred, a finding with direct relevance to reports from allegedly haunted rooms and séance settings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14511544/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance roomby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 112 — In Experiment 1, participants took p...</span></span></span>

## What the Fake Séance Tested

The best-known study was conducted by psychologist Richard Wiseman and colleagues. Participants attended what appeared to be a genuine séance. The room was darkened, a medium-like figure guided the session, and participants were encouraged to focus on the possibility of paranormal activity. Some objects in the room were secretly made to move through simple tricks, creating a convincing [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}) of unusual events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://richardwiseman.wordpress.com/research/psychology-of-the-seance/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.wordpress.com">[Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Seance - Richard Wiseman - WordPress.comApril 11, 2020 — by A Nyman — Some of the objects did indeed move (via tricker...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 11, 2020</span></span></span>

The crucial test involved a small table. During the séance, the performer suggested that the table was levitating. In reality, it never moved. Infrared monitoring confirmed that the table remained stationary throughout the session. Yet when participants were later questioned, roughly one-third reported that the table had moved or levitated. They were not merely guessing; many appeared genuinely convinced that they had witnessed movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14511544/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance roomby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 112 — In Experiment 1, participants took p...</span></span></span>

This finding matters because the false memory was not produced by elaborate hypnosis or repeated [questioning]({{ 'questioning/' | relative_url }}). A relatively simple verbal suggestion, delivered in a setting already framed as paranormal, was enough to alter later recollections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14511544/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance roomby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 112 — In Experiment 1, participants took p...</span></span></span>

## Why Believers Were More Vulnerable

The séance experiments found a relationship between paranormal belief and susceptibility to the suggestion. Participants who already held stronger paranormal beliefs were more likely to report movement that had not occurred. The researchers argued that believers may enter such situations with expectations that make suggested events easier to incorporate into memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14511544/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance roomby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 112 — In Experiment 1, participants took p...</span></span></span>

Importantly, this does not mean believers were being dishonest. The evidence points toward a difference in interpretation and memory formation rather than deliberate fabrication. When people encounter ambiguous information, prior beliefs help determine what seems plausible. In a séance context, the idea of a table levitating may fit comfortably within an existing framework of expectations, making later recollections more vulnerable to distortion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14511544/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance roomby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 112 — In Experiment 1, participants took p...</span></span></span>

Later research using an apparently paranormal key-bending demonstration produced a similar pattern. Participants watched a fake psychic bend a key. Some heard a suggestion that the key continued bending after it had been placed on a table, although it had not. Those exposed to the suggestion were significantly more likely to remember seeing continued movement. In some versions of the experiment, paranormal believers were especially likely to report the non-existent bending.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01289/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontiersin.org">[Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontiersin.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Frontiers Magic and memory: using conjuring to explore the effects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FrontiersMagic and memory: using conjuring to explore the effects...November 13, 2014 — by K Wilson · 2014 · Cited by 44 — This study us...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 13, 2014</span></span></span>

A notable feature of these studies is that confidence often failed to distinguish accurate memories from inaccurate ones. Participants who reported seeing the illusory movement could express very high confidence in what they remembered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/268876688_Magic_and_memory_using_conjuring_to_explore_the_effects_of_suggestion_social_influence_and_paranormal_belief_on_eyewitness_testimony_for_an_ostensibly_paranormal_event" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Magic and memory: using conjuring to explore the effects...Half the participants heard the fake psychic suggest that the key...</span></span></span>

## How Suggestion Became a Memory

The séance findings are best understood as a combination of perception, expectation and memory reconstruction rather than a simple failure of observation.

Several factors worked together:

* **Ambiguity:** The darkened séance environment made precise observation difficult.
* **Authority cues:** The medium explicitly suggested that movement was occurring.
* **Expectation:** Participants were attending an event presented as paranormal.
* **Memory reconstruction:** When recalling the event later, people integrated suggestions and expectations into their recollections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14511544/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance roomby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 112 — In Experiment 1, participants took p...</span></span></span>

The result was not merely a mistaken interpretation of something that happened. In many cases, participants remembered a specific movement that never occurred at all. This places the finding within the broader psychological literature on false memories, where people can develop vivid recollections of events that were suggested rather than observed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-McDermott-1995_JEPLMC.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psychnet.wustl.edu">[Psychnet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psychnet.wustl.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">False memories-either remembering events that never happened,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roediger-McDermott-1995_JEPLMC.pdfby HL Roediger III · 1995 · Cited by 6204 — The results reveal a powerful illusion of memory: P...</span></span></span>

## How Séance Findings Relate to Haunted Rooms

Although a laboratory-style séance differs from a reported haunting, the underlying psychological ingredients overlap closely. Both situations often involve uncertainty, emotionally charged expectations and a narrative that encourages particular interpretations of ambiguous events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14511544/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance roomby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 112 — In Experiment 1, participants took p...</span></span></span>

In a haunted room, a visitor may hear that objects move on their own, that a spirit is active, or that previous witnesses have seen strange activity. The séance experiments show that such framing can influence not only what people notice in the moment but also what they later remember. A vague sound, a shifting shadow or a fleeting impression may become more definite in memory as the story surrounding the location fills in missing details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14511544/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance roomby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 112 — In Experiment 1, participants took p...</span></span></span>

The key lesson is not that all haunting reports are false. Rather, the séance studies demonstrate a specific risk when evaluating eyewitness testimony: sincere witnesses can sometimes remember dramatic movement that never occurred if expectation and suggestion are strong enough.
