## Introduction

Ghost photographs have been faked almost as long as photography has existed. In the world of [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}), some of the most famous “evidence” images were not accidental camera quirks but deliberately constructed pictures designed to show the dead standing beside the living. From nineteenth-century spirit photographers using darkroom techniques to modern smartphone apps that can add translucent figures with a few taps, the history of ghost imagery is also a history of image manipulation. ## What older spirit photography teaches today

The classic era of spirit photography emerged in the 1860s, when photography was still a relatively new medium and many people believed cameras might reveal things invisible to the naked eye. The most famous practitioner was William H. Mumler of Boston and New York, who claimed that photographs could capture deceased relatives standing beside living sitters. His business flourished during and after the American Civil War, when many families were grieving lost loved ones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/spirit-photography-civil-war-william-mumler" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">spirit photography civil war william mumler</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">HISTORY reviews and updates its content regularly to ensure it is...Read more...</span></span></span>

Mumler's images typically showed a solid portrait accompanied by a faint, ghostly figure. To modern eyes, the effect resembles double exposure or compositing. Although he was prosecuted for fraud in 1869, authorities could not conclusively demonstrate the exact method used, and he was acquitted. Nevertheless, photographers, sceptics and investigators widely suspected photographic manipulation. Even showman P. T. Barnum demonstrated in court how convincing fake spirit photographs could be produced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/history-spirit-photography-future-deepfake-videos-180979010/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">fake by brothers George and William Slee. A second major chapter involved the British spirit photographer William Hope and the Crew Circle in the early twentieth century. Hope produced numerous photographs allegedly showing spirit forms. ## How editing and apps add apparitions

Modern fake ghosts rarely require darkroom expertise. Digital editing software allows a user to create effects that nineteenth-century photographers could only approximate.

Common methods include:

* **Layer compositing**, where a semi-transparent person is added over an existing photograph.
* **Opacity reduction**, making a figure appear translucent or ethereal.
* **Blur and glow effects**, which mimic motion, mist or supposedly supernatural energy.
* **AI-assisted image generation**, which can create entirely synthetic ghostly figures.
* **Template-based ghost apps**, which insert pre-made spirits, monsters or [shadow figures]({{ 'shadow-figures/' | relative_url }}) into photographs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_GB&amp;id=com.usefultools.ghostinphotofreeeditor" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: play.google.com">[Google Play+2App Store]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">play.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Play Ghost in Photo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google PlayGhost in Photo - Photo editor – Apps on...&quot;Ghost in Photo - Photo editor&quot; is an easy to use photo editor. ### Why digital ghosts often look convincing

Modern fake ghosts succeed for many of the same reasons spirit photographs succeeded more than a century ago.

First, they are usually embedded in emotionally meaningful contexts: a family home, a historic building, a graveyard or a place associated with a death. The setting encourages viewers to interpret ambiguity as significance.

Second, many edited ghosts deliberately imitate genuine photographic imperfections. Added blur, grain, low-light noise and partial transparency mimic the appearance of accidental anomalies, making the manipulation feel less artificial.

## Why original files and sequences matter

Because deliberate fabrication is so easy, investigators increasingly focus on the surrounding evidence rather than the visual appearance of a single image.

An original image file may contain metadata showing:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The camera or phone used.
* Date and time information.
* Editing history indicators.
* Image dimensions and processing details.
