## Introduction

Cold spots are among the most frequently reported signs in haunting investigations. A witness enters a room, feels a sudden chill, and a thermal camera or infrared thermometer appears to confirm that part of the space is colder than its surroundings. The difficulty is that temperature-measuring devices often detect something different from what people assume they are measuring. [Thermal cameras]({{ 'thermal-cameras/' | relative_url }}) primarily measure infrared radiation from surfaces, not the temperature of the air itself, while infrared thermometers usually report the temperature of a specific surface area rather than the [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}) around it. As a result, an apparent “cold spot” may reflect building conditions, airflow, surface materials, or measurement errors rather than an unexplained phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4168422/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Dwyer Omega]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Infrared Thermography for Temperature Measurement and...by R Usamentiaga · 2014 · Cited by 1490 — This paper presents a review of inf...</span></span></span>

Within ghost hunting, thermal tools can be useful for documenting environmental conditions. Their value lies less in proving a haunting and more in helping investigators determine whether a reported temperature anomaly has an ordinary physical explanation.

## Temperature Drops Versus Surface Readings

One of the most common misunderstandings in paranormal investigations is treating a thermal image as a direct picture of air temperature. Thermal cameras detect infrared energy emitted, reflected, or transmitted by surfaces and convert that information into an image. They are exceptionally good at showing temperature differences across walls, floors, ceilings, furniture, and other objects, but they do not directly display the temperature of empty air in a room.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mx.omega.com/prodinfo_eng/thermal_imagers.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mx.omega.com">[mx.omega.com+2labomat.eu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mx.omega.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thermal ImagersA thermal imager is a non-contact temperature measurement device. This distinction matters because many reported cold spots involve sensations rather than surfaces. A person may feel colder while standing near a doorway, stairwell, chimney, or poorly insulated wall. A thermal camera pointed at the surrounding surfaces may indeed reveal cooler areas, but that does not automatically mean a mysterious source created them. Building thermography is routinely used to identify insulation failures, moisture problems, air leakage, and energy loss because these issues create visible temperature differences on surfaces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iso.org">[ISO+2AaltoDOC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iso.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISO 6781-1:2023(en), Performance of buildingsInfrared building thermography provides a tool to qualitatively identify the presence of...</span></span></span>

Infrared thermometers introduce another source of confusion. Although often described casually as “temperature guns”, they generally measure the surface temperature of the object being targeted. A reading taken from a cold wall, window frame, or stone floor may be interpreted as evidence that the surrounding air has suddenly cooled, even when the instrument has measured only the material surface.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tempsens.com/blog/thermal-imagers-vs-infrared-thermometers-understanding-the-differences/?srsltid=AfmBOopxylC7jc61G8Z9z75OijjsrIH7-R5aPUT0z06OkvKokm-4dQVT" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tempsens.com">[Tempsens+2argustecn.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tempsens.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Thermal Imagers vs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Infrared ThermometersNov 28, 2023 — Infrared thermometers, also Infrared Pyrometers, are handheld devices used for measuring the surface...</span></span></span>

For a haunting claim, the important question is not whether a colder surface exists. The question is whether there was an unusual temperature change that cannot be explained by known environmental factors.

## Why Thermal Cameras Can Mislead Investigators

Thermal imaging is powerful, but it is not foolproof. Professional thermographers spend considerable effort accounting for factors that can distort apparent temperatures.

A major issue is emissivity, the degree to which a surface emits infrared radiation. Different materials emit heat differently. Matte painted walls, wood, fabric, and stone often provide reliable thermal readings, while shiny metals and reflective surfaces may produce misleading temperatures. A reflective object can appear unexpectedly cold or warm because the camera is partially recording reflected infrared energy from elsewhere in the environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flir.com/en-gb/discover/professional-tools/how-does-emissivity-affect-thermal-imaging/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flir.com">[FLIR+3FLIR+3FLIR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">how does emissivity affect thermal imaging</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Does Emissivity Affect Thermal Imaging?1 Nov 2021 — High-emissivity surfaces like electrical tape can be used to accurately measu...</span></span></span>

Another problem is thermal reflection. In visible light, people understand that mirrors reflect images. In thermal imaging, reflective materials can also create misleading patterns. Glass, polished metal, glossy surfaces, and some windows can reflect infrared radiation from nearby objects, producing apparent shapes, temperature anomalies, or “figures” that are not physically present where they appear in the image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thesnellgroup.com/featured-tips/building-inspections-windows-glass-isnt-reflective-or-is-it" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thesnellgroup.com">[thesnellgroup.com+2mx.omega.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thesnellgroup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is however highly specular, so whatever reflections we do detect are intensely sharp due...Read more...</span></span></span>

Professional guidance for thermal imaging repeatedly emphasises the need to account for reflected temperature and surface characteristics before drawing conclusions from a thermogram. Incorrect settings or assumptions can produce significant measurement errors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flir.com/discover/professional-tools/top-5-mistakes-that-new-thermographers-make/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flir.com">[FLIR+2flirmedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flir.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">top 5 mistakes that new thermographers make</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Top 5 Mistakes That New Thermographers Make30 Sept 2020 — Speaking of temperature measurements, setting the right emissivity and refl...</span></span></span>

In a ghost-hunting context, a thermal anomaly becomes much less impressive once investigators ask a simple question: is the camera showing the object's true temperature, or a combination of emission and reflection?

## Drafts, Insulation, and Thermal Reflection Problems

Many reported cold spots have explanations that thermal imaging can actually help reveal rather than confirm as paranormal.

**Air leakage and drafts.** Small gaps around doors, windows, floorboards, vents, and service penetrations can introduce cooler air into a room. Occupants may experience these localised flows as sudden cold patches. Thermal imaging is widely used in building inspections specifically to locate such leaks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iso.org">[ISO+2AaltoDOC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iso.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISO 6781-1:2023(en), Performance of buildingsInfrared building thermography provides a tool to qualitatively identify the presence of...</span></span></span>

**Insulation defects.** Missing or degraded insulation creates colder wall and ceiling surfaces. A person standing nearby may perceive the area as unusually cold even when room air temperatures remain relatively stable. Thermal cameras frequently identify these defects because they produce distinctive patterns on building surfaces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iso.org">[ISO+2AaltoDOC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iso.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISO 6781-1:2023(en), Performance of buildingsInfrared building thermography provides a tool to qualitatively identify the presence of...</span></span></span>

**Thermal bridges.** Structural elements such as metal supports, concrete lintels, and framing members can conduct heat differently from surrounding materials. These features often appear as colder streaks or patches on thermal images and can be mistaken for unexplained anomalies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iso.org">[ISO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iso.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ISO 6781-1:2023(en), Performance of buildingsInfrared building thermography provides a tool to qualitatively identify the presence of...</span></span></span>

**Reflective artefacts.** Windows and glossy surfaces deserve special caution. A thermal camera may show an apparent cold area that is actually a reflection of a cooler object elsewhere. Glass is particularly problematic because it does not behave like an ordinary transparent window in thermal imaging. Investigators sometimes mistake reflected thermal patterns for genuine temperature anomalies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thesnellgroup.com/featured-tips/building-inspections-windows-glass-isnt-reflective-or-is-it" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thesnellgroup.com">[thesnellgroup.com+2FLIR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thesnellgroup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is however highly specular, so whatever reflections we do detect are intensely sharp due...Read more...</span></span></span>

These explanations are not hypothetical edge cases. They are common enough that building inspectors, energy auditors, and industrial thermographers routinely check for them before interpreting temperature differences.

## How to Document a Cold Spot Without Overclaiming

A thermal anomaly becomes more meaningful when investigators document it systematically rather than relying on a single dramatic image.

A stronger approach includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Measure both air and surface temperatures.** Use appropriate instruments and note which quantity each device actually measures.
2. **Record environmental conditions.** Document weather, heating systems, open windows, ventilation, and occupancy.
3. **Map the area.** Determine whether the anomaly stays fixed in one location or follows an airflow pattern.
4. **Check nearby structures.** Examine walls, floors, ceilings, windows, ducts, and doors for known sources of temperature variation.
5. **Repeat measurements.** A genuine environmental anomaly should be observable more than once under similar conditions.
6. **Capture ordinary explanations.** If a draft, insulation defect, or reflective surface is present, document it alongside the thermal image rather than excluding it.

</div>

This process mirrors professional thermography practice, where thermal images are rarely accepted as stand-alone proof of a condition. Instead, they serve as indicators that require additional [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}) and context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4168422/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2ISO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Infrared Thermography for Temperature Measurement and...by R Usamentiaga · 2014 · Cited by 1490 — This paper presents a review of inf...</span></span></span>

## What a Cold Spot Would Need to Show

For a cold spot to move beyond anecdote and become persuasive evidence in a haunting investigation, it would need to demonstrate more than a temperature difference on a thermal display.

Investigators would need to show that the anomaly is real, repeatable, accurately measured, and not attributable to airflow, insulation, material properties, reflected infrared radiation, weather conditions, or equipment settings. The challenge is that thermal imaging technology was developed precisely because ordinary buildings contain countless temperature variations that can be detected and analysed. The same sensitivity that makes thermal cameras useful also makes them vulnerable to misinterpretation.

A thermal camera can reveal that something is colder. It cannot, by itself, reveal why. That gap between observation and explanation is where many cold-spot haunting claims become less convincing under careful examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4168422/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[FLIR+3PMC+3ISO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Infrared Thermography for Temperature Measurement and...by R Usamentiaga · 2014 · Cited by 1490 — This paper presents a review of inf...</span></span></span>
