## Introduction

Harry Price’s reputation as a leading investigator of hauntings did not rest solely on dramatic visits to allegedly haunted buildings. A large part of his authority came from institutions that looked scholarly and scientific: a research laboratory, a specialised library, published catalogues, and links to the University of London. In an era when [psychical research]({{ 'research-files/' | relative_url }}) struggled for academic legitimacy, these structures helped Price present himself not merely as a ghost hunter but as the organiser of a serious research programme. By creating places where evidence could be collected, tested, archived and displayed, he gave paranormal [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}) an appearance of permanence and expertise that many rivals lacked.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/objects-and-stories/science-investigating-paranormal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk">[National Science and Media Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">science investigating paranormal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Price defined psychical...Read more...</span></span></span>

For the public, journalists and many supporters, the laboratory and library suggested that [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) and other paranormal claims were being studied under disciplined conditions rather than through anecdote alone. Whether critics accepted that claim is another matter, but the institutional framework itself became one of Price’s most powerful tools.

## The National Laboratory of Psychical Research

The strongest symbol of Price’s scientific ambitions was the National Laboratory of Psychical Research (NLPR), established in the mid-1920s in London. Price described its mission as investigating alleged psychic phenomena through dispassionate inquiry and scientific methods. The laboratory included dedicated rooms, recording equipment and experimental apparatus intended to demonstrate that paranormal claims could be subjected to controlled testing. By adopting the language and appearance of laboratory science, Price attempted to move investigation into a different category. The laboratory became a place where mediums could be tested, witnesses examined and alleged phenomena recorded under conditions that appeared more rigorous than those available in private drawing rooms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/objects-and-stories/science-investigating-paranormal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk">[National Science and Media Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">science investigating paranormal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Price defined psychical...Read more...</span></span></span>

### More than a building

The laboratory’s importance was symbolic as well as practical. Price understood that institutions generate authority. A named laboratory with honorary officers, publications and technical equipment conveyed seriousness to journalists and readers. Press coverage frequently emphasised these scientific trappings, helping Price become one of Britain’s best-known paranormal investigators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.harrypricewebsite.co.uk/Biography/price-psychic-career01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: harrypricewebsite.co.uk">[Harry Price Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">harrypricewebsite.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harry Price WebsiteA Brief Survey of His Career in Psychical ResearchOn the 1st of January 1926, the new National Laboratory of Psychical...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1926</span></span></span>

Critics questioned whether the laboratory always met the standards implied by its scientific presentation. Disputes with the Society for Psychical Research revealed disagreements about methods, controls and interpretation. Yet even critics acknowledged that Price had created a visible institutional centre for psychical investigation. The debate itself demonstrated how effectively he had positioned the subject within the language of organised research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/national-laboratory-psychical-researchnlpr" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">national laboratory psychical researchnlpr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Laboratory of Psychical Research(NLPR)Research facility established by psychical researcher Harry Price in 1925 at 13 Roland Gar...</span></span></span>

## The Harry Price Library of Magical Literature

If the laboratory supplied scientific authority, Price’s library supplied scholarly authority. Over decades he assembled a vast collection covering psychical research, spiritualism, conjuring, deception, witchcraft, occult traditions and related subjects. The collection eventually comprised nearly 13,000 books, pamphlets and periodical titles, including rare works dating back centuries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.london.ac.uk/about/services/senate-house-library/collections/printed-special-collections/harry-price-library-magical-literature" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: london.ac.uk">[University of London]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">london.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">harry price library magical literature</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of LondonHarry Price Library of Magical LiteratureAbout 13000 items from the 15th to the 21st centuries relating to magic in i...</span></span></span>

The breadth of the collection was significant. Price argued that anyone investigating paranormal claims needed to understand not only alleged supernatural phenomena but also the history of fraud, illusion and conjuring. His catalogues reflected this approach, bringing together works on psychical research alongside texts on deception, stage magic and scientific investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wellcomecollection.org/works/xcfm354n" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wellcomecollection.org">[Wellcome Collection]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wellcomecollection.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wellcome CollectionShort-title catalogue: of works on psychical research...Short-title catalogue: of works on psychical research, spi...</span></span></span>

Unlike a private collector who simply accumulated curiosities, Price actively promoted the library as a research resource. It supported the work of his laboratory and later became linked to university institutions. ### A collector as an expert

The library also helped Price cultivate a particular public image. Expertise in psychical research was difficult to define because the field lacked stable academic departments and recognised professional qualifications. Possessing one of the largest specialist collections in Britain offered an alternative route to authority.

Price published catalogues, organised exhibitions and frequently highlighted rare volumes in his possession. These activities presented him as a custodian of knowledge rather than merely an investigator of sensational cases. The collection suggested deep historical familiarity with paranormal claims and their investigation, reinforcing his standing whenever he commented on hauntings or mediumship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wellcomecollection.org/works/xcfm354n" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wellcomecollection.org">[Wellcome Collection+2Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wellcomecollection.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wellcome CollectionShort-title catalogue: of works on psychical research...Short-title catalogue: of works on psychical research, spi...</span></span></span>

## How institutions shaped public trust

Price’s authority rested on a combination of performance and infrastructure. Haunted houses attracted attention, but laboratories, catalogues and university associations translated that attention into credibility. ## Why Price Looked Like an Expert

Many investigators visited haunted locations during the early twentieth century. Far fewer built institutions around their work. Price’s distinctive achievement was to surround paranormal investigation with the visible markers of expertise that modern audiences associated with knowledge: laboratories, archives, catalogues, publications and university connections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/objects-and-stories/science-investigating-paranormal" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk">[National Science and Media Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">science investigating paranormal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Price defined psychical...Read more...</span></span></span>

Those institutions did not settle debates about ghosts, hauntings or mediumship. Critics continued to challenge Price’s methods and conclusions, and his reputation remains controversial. Yet the authority he acquired came largely from the structures he created. When readers encountered [Harry Price]({{ 'harry-price/' | relative_url }}), they encountered not just an investigator but the director of a laboratory, the owner of a major research library and a figure connected to respected educational institutions. That combination helped explain why, for much of the interwar period, he appeared to many [observers]({{ 'observers/' | relative_url }}) as one of the foremost experts on the paranormal in Britain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Price" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[en.wikipedia.org+2Time]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Harry Price</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harry Price</span></span></span>
