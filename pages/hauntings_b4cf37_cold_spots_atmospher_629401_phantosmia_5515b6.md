## Introduction

Reports of unexplained smells are common in haunting narratives. Witnesses describe perfume in an empty corridor, cigar smoke in a sealed room, flowers with no visible source, or the sudden odour of decay that vanishes as quickly as it appeared. One possibility that is often overlooked is that the smell may not be present in the environment at all. A recognised medical phenomenon called *phantosmia*—sometimes called an olfactory hallucination or phantom smell—involves perceiving an odour when no corresponding smell exists in the air. Medical authorities define phantosmia as the detection of an odour without an external source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apps.nhslothian.scot/files/sites/2/Phantosmia-advice-for-primary-care-2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apps.nhslothian.scot">[apps.nhslothian.scot+2Right Decisions]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apps.nhslothian.scot</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is also called &#x27;olfactory hallucination&#x27;. Hyposmia/anosmia means reduced/loss of sense of smell.Read more...</span></span></span>

In the context of haunting reports, phantosmia matters because it offers a [mechanism]({{ 'mechanism/' | relative_url }}) that can produce a vivid, apparently supernatural experience without requiring any detectable environmental cause. It does not explain every reported ghostly odour, but it provides an evidence-based alternative that investigators should consider whenever a smell is experienced by only one person, recurs across different locations, or appears alongside other neurological or sensory symptoms.

## What Phantom Smells Can Feel Like

People experiencing phantosmia often report highly specific odours. Medical studies and clinical reviews describe phantom smells ranging from smoke, burning materials and gas to rotten, musty, metallic, sweet or chemical odours. Burnt smells are among the most frequently reported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585128/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[NCBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">StatPearls - NCBI Bookshelfby B Gillette · 2023 · Cited by 6 — A burnt smell is the most commonly reported smell associated with phantosm...</span></span></span>

This can overlap strikingly with the kinds of smells that appear in haunting folklore. A witness who suddenly smells tobacco in a historic building may naturally connect the experience to stories about a deceased former occupant. Someone who detects decay in an allegedly haunted cellar may interpret it as evidence of a supernatural presence. Yet from the witness's perspective, a phantom smell can feel entirely real. The experience is not imagined in the everyday sense of deliberate make-believe; the brain genuinely presents the sensation as a smell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/phantosmia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ScienceDirect Phantosmia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phantosmia - an overview &#124; ScienceDirect TopicsPhantosmia is defined as the perception of odor in the absence of an odor sti...</span></span></span>

Another feature that can make phantom smells persuasive is their persistence. Some people experience brief episodes lasting seconds or minutes, while others report recurring odours over days, weeks or longer. Because smell is closely connected to memory and emotion, an unexplained odour can feel unusually meaningful and memorable compared with a fleeting sound or visual ambiguity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9702807/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PO is a qualitative smell disorder seizures, depression...</span></span></span>

## Medical Causes Worth Considering

Phantosmia is not a single disease but a symptom with many possible causes. Most are far more common than any paranormal explanation.

### Nose and Sinus Conditions

Many cases are linked to conditions affecting the nose and smell pathways. Upper respiratory infections, sinus infections, allergies, nasal polyps and post-viral smell disturbances can all contribute to phantom odours. Following the COVID-19 pandemic, clinicians also documented increased reports of altered smell perception, including phantosmia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/24799-phantosmia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: my.clevelandclinic.org">[nidirect+3Cleveland Clinic+3nhs.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">my.clevelandclinic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">24799 phantosmia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sinus infections. Upper respiratory infections. Allergies. Nasal polyps. Migraines. Taking certain...</span></span></span>

This matters for haunting reports because a person may experience the same phantom smell in multiple locations. If someone attributes the smell to a particular house, they may overlook the possibility that the source is a continuing sensory disorder rather than the building itself.

### Migraine and Headache Disorders

Although uncommon, phantom smells can occur in association with migraine. Some people experience odours shortly before or during migraine attacks, functioning somewhat like other sensory warning signs. These smells may occur even when no external odour exists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9702807/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Healthline]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PO is a qualitative smell disorder seizures, depression...</span></span></span>

A witness who encounters a phantom smell while already feeling light-headed, sensitive to light or unwell may therefore be experiencing a neurological event rather than detecting something in the environment.

### Epilepsy and Temporal Lobe Activity

One of the most discussed neurological associations involves temporal lobe epilepsy. Certain seizures can produce olfactory auras—brief smell sensations that occur before or during seizure activity. These experiences are relatively uncommon but well documented in medical literature. Researchers have linked them particularly to structures within the temporal lobe and nearby regions involved in smell processing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12558584/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2epilepsybehavior.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In this study, mesial temporal sclerosis is the most common etiology rather than tumors...</span></span></span>

Historically, unusual smells, sudden feelings of presence, intense emotions and altered perceptions have sometimes been interpreted as supernatural encounters. Because temporal lobe activity can affect several of these experiences simultaneously, neurologists have long considered it an important natural explanation for some reports that acquire paranormal interpretations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3263120/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psychosis and mood disturbance are other likely features of this clinical problem.Read more...</span></span></span>

### Other Neurological and Medical Causes

Medical references also associate phantosmia with head injuries, certain medications, Parkinson's disease, brain tumours and other neurological conditions. Most people with phantom smells do not have a serious neurological disorder, but persistent or unexplained symptoms deserve medical attention because they can occasionally signal an underlying health problem.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/temporal-lobe-seizure/expert-answers/phantosmia/faq-20058131" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mayoclinic.org">[Mayo Clinic+2Beacon Health System]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mayoclinic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">faq 20058131</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It also can be caused by aging, trauma, temporal lobe seizures, inflamed sinuses...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why Phantom Smells Fit Some Haunting Reports Better Than Others

Phantosmia is particularly relevant when an odour experience has characteristics that are difficult to reconcile with an environmental source.

Several patterns raise the possibility of a personal sensory event:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Only one person notices the smell while others nearby detect nothing.
* The smell appears in different buildings or locations.
* The odour persists after leaving the supposedly haunted area.
* Episodes recur over weeks or months.
* The smell is accompanied by headaches, dizziness, visual disturbances, unusual emotions or other neurological symptoms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apps.nhslothian.scot/files/sites/2/Phantosmia-advice-for-primary-care-2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apps.nhslothian.scot">[apps.nhslothian.scot+2Cleveland Clinic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apps.nhslothian.scot</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It is also called &#x27;olfactory hallucination&#x27;. Hyposmia/anosmia means reduced/loss of sense of smell.Read more...</span></span></span>

</div>

These features do not prove phantosmia, but they shift attention away from the room and toward the person experiencing the smell.

By contrast, if multiple independent [observers]({{ 'observers/' | relative_url }}) detect the same odour simultaneously, and a measurable source can later be identified, an environmental explanation becomes more likely. In such cases the question is usually not phantosmia but whether the smell came from hidden building conditions such as dampness, ventilation pathways, contamination or intermittent odour sources.

## How to Separate Room Odours from Personal Symptoms

The distinction between a real room odour and a phantom smell is not always obvious in the moment. Practical observation can help.

First, determine whether others can detect the smell. If several people notice the same odour at the same time and place, the likelihood of an environmental source increases.

Second, note whether the smell follows the person. A phantom smell may remain noticeable outdoors, in a vehicle or at home after leaving the location where it was first noticed. Environmental odours generally do not behave this way.

Third, look for repetition patterns. If the same smell repeatedly appears across unrelated settings, a personal sensory cause becomes more plausible than a location-specific haunting.

Finally, pay attention to accompanying symptoms. Recurrent phantom smells occurring alongside migraine symptoms, seizure-like episodes, changes in smell perception, recent illness, sinus problems or neurological symptoms deserve medical evaluation rather than paranormal interpretation. ## What This Means for Haunting Investigations

Phantosmia demonstrates that not every reported odour in a haunted location must actually exist in the surrounding air. A witness can experience a smell that feels vivid, convincing and emotionally powerful even when no external source is present. That does not mean the witness is dishonest or mistaken about having the experience. It means the experience may originate within the sensory system rather than the environment.

For investigators, phantom smells are a reminder that unexplained odours should be evaluated from two directions at once: the building and the person. Environmental causes remain important, but so do recognised medical mechanisms that can generate the perception of smoke, perfume, flowers, decay or other scents without any corresponding odour source. In some haunting reports, the most important clue is not what is supposedly in the room, but what is happening in the brain of the person who smells it.
