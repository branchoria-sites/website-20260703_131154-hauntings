## Introduction

Berry Pomeroy Castle is one of the clearest examples in Britain of a ghost story that appears to have travelled in the opposite direction from what people usually assume. Rather than a long-established local haunting inspiring fiction, evidence suggests that popular fiction and later romantic storytelling helped create some of the castle’s most famous ghosts. Over time, those invented narratives became attached to the ruins so firmly that many visitors came to regard them as ancient local traditions. English Heritage, which manages the site, explicitly notes that the castle’s famous White Lady and Blue Lady legends were shaped by Georgian and Victorian writers working from mistaken ideas about the castle’s past, and that literary inventions gradually became accepted as genuine [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/berry-pomeroy-castle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english-heritage.org.uk">[English Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english-heritage.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">English HeritageVisit Berry Pomeroy CastleIts awe-inspiring ruins became a magnet for sightseers and artists, and inspired blood-curdling...</span></span></span>

Within the wider world of [White Lady legends]({{ 'white-ladies/' | relative_url }}), Berry Pomeroy matters because it provides a rare case where the growth of the folklore can be traced. The castle shows how a ruined landscape, a taste for Gothic fiction and generations of [retelling]({{ 'retelling/' | relative_url }}) can transform imaginative stories into something that feels like inherited memory.

## How the ruined castle became picturesque

The transformation began long before the ghosts became famous. Berry Pomeroy Castle was abandoned in the late seventeenth century and gradually fell into ruin. By the nineteenth century, however, the broken towers and overgrown walls had acquired a different value. Romantic artists, tourists and antiquarians admired ruins as picturesque places that evoked mystery, loss and the passage of time. Berry Pomeroy became a popular destination precisely because it looked like the setting of a Gothic novel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berry_Pomeroy_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berry Pomeroy Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berry Pomeroy Castle</span></span></span>

That visual [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}) mattered. A standing castle with a well-documented history invites historical questions. A ruined castle hidden in wooded valleys encourages imaginative ones. Visitors could easily picture imprisoned noblewomen, secret passages, family betrayals and [tragic deaths]({{ 'tragic-deaths/' | relative_url }}) even when historical evidence was lacking. The setting itself encouraged stories that felt emotionally plausible.

English Heritage notes that the ruins attracted sightseers and artists and became associated with increasingly dramatic ghost tales. The castle's reputation as a haunted place grew alongside its reputation as a picturesque ruin rather than emerging directly from medieval records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/berry-pomeroy-castle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english-heritage.org.uk">[English Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english-heritage.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">English HeritageVisit Berry Pomeroy CastleIts awe-inspiring ruins became a magnet for sightseers and artists, and inspired blood-curdling...</span></span></span>

## How fiction supplied the ghosts

The most famous legends concern two female [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}): the White Lady and the Blue Lady. Today they are often presented as traditional castle ghosts, but their stories show strong signs of literary construction.

The White Lady is commonly identified as Margaret or Matilda Pomeroy, supposedly imprisoned and starved by a jealous sister. The Blue Lady is linked to a darker tale involving a noblewoman, an illicit pregnancy and the death of a child. Both stories possess the characteristics that Gothic fiction readers expected: innocence, cruelty, hidden crimes, family secrets and dramatic punishment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berry_Pomeroy_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2solosophie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berry Pomeroy Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berry Pomeroy Castle</span></span></span>

A particularly important milestone was the publication of *The Castle of Berry Pomeroy* in 1806, a Gothic novel attributed to Edward Montague. Later researchers have pointed to the novel as a major influence on the castle's supernatural reputation. The book drew on the conventions of Gothic romance and helped connect Berry Pomeroy with narratives of jealousy, imprisonment and restless spirits. Subsequent writers repeated and expanded these themes until they became difficult to separate from local tradition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berry_Pomeroy_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berry Pomeroy Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berry Pomeroy Castle</span></span></span>

What makes the case unusual is that the literary influence is visible. In many haunted locations, it is impossible to determine whether stories originated in folklore or fiction. At Berry Pomeroy, the chronology strongly suggests that published Gothic narratives played a significant role in shaping what later generations understood as authentic castle legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://superstitionsam.com/2022/05/21/the-power-of-folklore-the-ghosts-of-berry-pomeroy-castle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: superstitionsam.com">[Superstition Sam]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">superstitionsam.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the power of folklore the ghosts of berry pomeroy castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Superstition SamThe Power of Folklore: The &#x27;Ghosts&#x27; of Berry Pomeroy Castle21 May 2022 — Created the Most Haunted Site in Britain the sit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2022</span></span></span>

## Why Victorian audiences embraced the legends

The nineteenth century was an ideal environment for such stories to flourish. Britain experienced a huge appetite for Gothic literature, antiquarian history and ghost tales. Ruined castles became stages on which readers could imagine medieval tragedies and supernatural encounters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_story" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ghost story</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ghost story</span></span></span>

As stories circulated in guidebooks, local accounts and popular retellings, they acquired an aura of authenticity. Details were repeated often enough that they began to sound historical even when documentation was weak. A visitor arriving in the late Victorian period might encounter the White Lady story not as recent fiction but as something supposedly handed down through generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berry_Pomeroy_Castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Berry Pomeroy Castle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Berry Pomeroy Castle</span></span></span>

This process illustrates an important feature of folklore. Repetition can be more influential than origin. Once enough people tell a story, the question of who invented it becomes less important than the fact that communities have accepted it.

## Why invented legends can feel like inherited memory

<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://rosemarygriggs.co.uk/blog/28/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rosemarygriggs.co.uk">[Berry Pomeroy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rosemarygriggs.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pomeroy, the White Lady, Blue Lady, a Pomeroy who is said to have...</span></span></span> 's ghosts survive because they fit powerful folklore patterns.

The White Lady resembles countless female apparitions associated with castles and manor houses across Europe: a tragic woman linked to betrayal, imprisonment or [grief]({{ 'grief/' | relative_url }}). The Blue Lady serves a related function, embodying hidden crime, guilt and danger. Neither figure requires visitors to know detailed history. The stories are immediately understandable because they draw on familiar cultural themes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Lady" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">White Lady</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">White Lady</span></span></span>

The castle also demonstrates how physical places help preserve stories. When visitors stand in a ruined tower or descend a dark stairway, the setting supplies emotional reinforcement. The architecture appears to confirm the narrative. People often remember the experience of the place and the story together, making the legend feel older and more authentic than it may actually be.

Over generations, the distinction between documented history and accumulated storytelling becomes blurred. New witnesses report unusual feelings or experiences, and those experiences are interpreted through the existing legend. The folklore effectively creates a framework for understanding the location. As a result, a story that may have begun as literary invention becomes part of the site's cultural identity.

Berry Pomeroy Castle is valuable not because it proves or disproves ghosts, but because it reveals how ghost traditions are made. The site shows that a famous haunting can emerge from an interaction between landscape, literature and memory rather than from a single historical event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/berry-pomeroy-castle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english-heritage.org.uk">[English Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english-heritage.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">English HeritageVisit Berry Pomeroy CastleIts awe-inspiring ruins became a magnet for sightseers and artists, and inspired blood-curdling...</span></span></span>
