## Introduction

Haunted places remain culturally credible for a simple reason: large minorities of the public continue to believe that ghosts may exist, while many more people remain open to unexplained experiences even if they do not fully endorse supernatural explanations. Surveys do not demonstrate that any location is genuinely haunted, but they do reveal the social environment in which haunted-house stories, [ghost tours]({{ 'ghost-tours/' | relative_url }}), historic legends and local rumours continue to thrive. When substantial numbers of people think ghosts are possible, reports from old hotels, castles, houses and battlefields are less likely to be dismissed outright and more likely to be discussed, remembered and retold. ## What Recent Ghost-Belief Polls Show

One of the clearest findings from modern polling is that belief in ghosts is neither universal nor marginal. In a 2025 YouGov survey, 38% of Britons said ghosts definitely or probably exist, while 56% said they probably or definitely do not. That leaves a substantial share of the population willing to treat ghost claims as plausible rather than impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/52619-ghosts-magic-do-britons-believe-in-the-supernatural" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yougov.com">[YouGov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yougov.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">52619 ghosts magic do britons believe in the supernatural</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ghosts? The picture is similar in the United States. Gallup's 2025 polling found that 39% of American adults believe in ghosts. Although scepticism remains the majority position, ghost belief remains one of the most widely held paranormal beliefs measured by major polling organisations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.gallup.com/poll/692738/paranormal-phenomena-met-skepticism.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.gallup.com">[Gallup.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.gallup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">paranormal phenomena met skepticism.aspx</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phenomena Met With Skepticism in U.S.23 Jul 2025 — Slightly fewer, 39%, express a belief in ghosts, while between 24% and 29% say they be...</span></span></span>

Historical comparisons are especially important for understanding haunted-place credibility. The issue is not whether belief is rising dramatically, but whether it persists. Gallup found in 2005 that 37% of Americans believed in haunted houses and 32% believed that spirits of the dead could return in certain places or situations. Two decades later, levels of belief remain broadly comparable rather than collapsing under scientific modernisation or technological change.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.gallup.com/poll/16915/three-four-americans-believe-paranormal.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.gallup.com">[Gallup.com+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.gallup.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">in Four Americans Believe in ParanormalJun 16, 2005 — About three in four Americans profess at least one paranormal belief, according to...</span></span></span>

This continuity matters because haunted places depend less on proof than on plausibility. If roughly one-third to two-fifths of a population already accepts the possibility of ghosts, then stories associated with specific locations begin from a position of cultural familiarity rather than outright disbelief.

## Believers, Sceptics and the Undecided

Survey results often reveal a more complicated landscape than a simple divide between believers and sceptics.

* People who believe ghosts probably or definitely exist.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://english.aawsat.com/varieties/5204069-third-people-england-believe-ghosts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english.aawsat.com">[english.aawsat.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english.aawsat.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">5204069 third people england believe ghosts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Third of People in England Believe in GhostsNov 2, 2568 BE — In total, 39% of survey respondents said they believed there was life after...</span></span></span>

* People who are uncertain or open to the possibility without committing to belief.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/52619-ghosts-magic-do-britons-believe-in-the-supernatural" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yougov.com">[YouGov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yougov.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">52619 ghosts magic do britons believe in the supernatural</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ghosts? That middle group is particularly important for haunted-place legends. Fully committed believers are not the only audience that keeps ghost stories alive. Curious non-believers, cautious agnostics and people who simply enjoy unexplained mysteries also participate in the circulation of haunted-place narratives.

The National Folklore Survey for England highlights this distinction. The survey found that just over one-third of respondents believed in ghosts or spirits of deceased people, yet awareness of folklore and local supernatural traditions extended much more widely than direct belief alone. Researchers found strong evidence that ghost stories remain embedded in local culture even among people who do not necessarily accept them as factual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalfolkloresurvey.co.uk/news/first-findings-from-the-nfs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalfolkloresurvey.co.uk">[National Folklore Survey for England]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalfolkloresurvey.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">first findings from the nfs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Folklore Survey for EnglandFirst findings from the NFS28 Oct 2025 — When it comes down to beliefs in the unexplained and unexpec...</span></span></span>

This helps explain a recurring feature of haunted locations: many visitors do not arrive expecting definitive proof. Instead, they arrive willing to entertain the possibility that unusual events could be meaningful. Such openness gives haunted places a wider audience than belief figures alone might suggest.

## Why Local Legends Often Outlast Evidence

Most haunted locations depend on cumulative reputation rather than a single dramatic incident. Survey evidence suggests that belief functions as a social filter through which ambiguous experiences are interpreted.

If someone hears [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}) in an old inn, notices a sudden cold patch in a historic house or experiences an unexplained feeling of unease in a reputedly haunted castle, the interpretation may depend heavily on prior expectations. Polls show that many people already regard ghosts as possible, making supernatural explanations culturally available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/52619-ghosts-magic-do-britons-believe-in-the-supernatural" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yougov.com">[YouGov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yougov.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">52619 ghosts magic do britons believe in the supernatural</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ghosts? This does not mean belief creates experiences. Rather, it affects how experiences are categorised and remembered. An unusual sound may become a ghost story in one context and a building maintenance issue in another. Haunted places gain credibility when multiple visitors interpret different incidents through the same established narrative.

## How Public Openness Keeps Legends Alive

The strongest lesson from [belief surveys]({{ 'belief-surveys/' | relative_url }}) is not that most people are convinced by [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}). It is that enough people remain open to them.

The National Folklore Survey found notable links between age, regional culture and engagement with paranormal traditions, while researchers also observed continuing public interest in ghost stories, seasonal folklore and supernatural themes.
