## Introduction

Thermal cameras are often presented as one of the most convincing tools in modern ghost hunting. A thermal image can reveal a cold patch in a corridor, an unusual shape against a wall, or a sudden temperature difference that is invisible to the naked eye. Yet the key limitation is simple: a thermal camera can show that a temperature anomaly exists, but it cannot determine what caused it. Infrared imaging is widely used by engineers, energy auditors and building inspectors because it is excellent at finding heat loss, air leakage, moisture intrusion and insulation defects. The same features that make thermal cameras useful for investigating supposedly haunted locations also make them highly sensitive to ordinary building conditions. The Department of Energy's Energy.gov+2flir.com<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/thermographic-inspections" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: energy.gov">[energy.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">energy.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Department of Energy&#x27;s Energy.govhttps://www.energy.gov/energysaver/thermographic-inspections</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

For reports of [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) that involve cold spots or unusual atmospheric conditions, thermal imaging can provide valuable documentation. However, the image itself is not evidence of a ghost. It is evidence that a surface or area appears warmer or cooler than its surroundings and requires further [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lightpath.com/insights/exploring-the-capabilities-of-an-infrared-thermal-imaging-camera" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lightpath.com">[lightpath.com+2MESTEK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lightpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lightpath.com.</span></span></span>

## What Thermal Images Actually Measure

Thermal cameras do not see spirits, energy fields or hidden entities. They detect infrared radiation emitted by surfaces and convert temperature differences into a visual image. Every object above absolute zero emits infrared energy, and the camera translates that information into colours or shades representing relative temperature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lightpath.com/insights/exploring-the-capabilities-of-an-infrared-thermal-imaging-camera" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lightpath.com">[lightpath.com+2raythink-tech.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lightpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lightpath.com.</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because a thermal image shows surface temperature patterns, not causes. A cold area on a wall might indicate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Air entering through a gap.
* Missing insulation.
* Moisture inside building materials.
* A hidden pipe.
* Different construction materials behind the surface.
* Recent exposure to sunlight or shade.

</div>

The camera records the result of these conditions rather than identifying which one is responsible. Building scientists routinely describe unusual thermal patterns as clues requiring additional investigation, not as self-explanatory findings. Building Science+2The Department of Energy's Energy.gov<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://buildingscience.com/documents/published-articles/pa-1902-how-look-house-building-scientist-part-2-heat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: buildingscience.com">[buildingscience.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">buildingscience.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pa 1902 how look house building scientist part 2 heat</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

Another common misconception is that thermal cameras can see through walls. They cannot. Infrared cameras measure temperatures on exposed surfaces. When aimed at a wall, they record the wall's surface temperature rather than directly revealing what is hidden behind it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mestekinstruments.com/what-is-an-infrared-thermal-imaging-camera/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mestekinstruments.com">[MESTEK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mestekinstruments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on mestekinstruments.com.</span></span></span>

## Why Haunted Buildings Often Produce Thermal Anomalies

Many locations associated with hauntings are older structures with complex heating, ventilation and maintenance histories. These buildings are often exactly the kind of environments where thermal imaging reveals genuine temperature irregularities.

Government energy-efficiency guidance and professional thermography standards identify several common causes of thermal anomalies in buildings:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Air leakage around windows, doors, chimneys and service penetrations.
* Missing or degraded insulation.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://basc.pnnl.gov/diy-guides/look-missing-insulation-using-thermal-imaging" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: basc.pnnl.gov">[basc.pnnl.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">basc.pnnl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* Moisture intrusion and damp materials.
* HVAC system imbalances.
* Thermal bridges, where heat moves more easily through structural elements.
* Roof and wall defects. flir.com+3The Department of Energy's Energy.gov+3The Department of Energy's Energy.gov<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/thermographic-inspections" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: energy.gov">[energy.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">energy.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Department of Energy&#x27;s Energy.govhttps://www.energy.gov/energysaver/thermographic-inspections</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

</div>

A person standing in the path of a cold air current may experience a dramatic chill even though the room's average temperature has changed very little. On a thermal image, that airflow can appear as a distinct cool pattern spreading across a surface. Investigators unfamiliar with building physics may interpret the same pattern as an unexplained cold spot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.maesin.co.uk/resources/detecting-the-unseen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: maesin.co.uk">[maesin.co.uk+2Horizon Homes]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">maesin.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on maesin.co.uk.</span></span></span>

The challenge is that genuine anomalies exist. Thermal cameras are not creating illusions. The question is whether the anomaly represents a paranormal event or a conventional environmental process.

## Building Defects That Look Like Anomalies

Many thermal images shared in paranormal investigations resemble conditions that building inspectors encounter routinely.

### Air Leaks and Draught Paths

Infrared imaging is widely used during energy audits because moving air leaves recognisable thermal signatures. Cold outside air entering through gaps often creates streaks, plumes or localised cold regions that can seem surprisingly well defined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://horizonmaine.com/guides/thermal-imaging-home-energy/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: horizonmaine.com">[Horizon Homes+2nachi.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">horizonmaine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on horizonmaine.com.</span></span></span>

In a dark hallway or stairwell, such a pattern may appear mysterious, especially if it coincides with local ghost stories. Yet the same image may be entirely consistent with ordinary air infiltration.

### Moisture and Dampness

Water changes how materials absorb and release heat. Damp plaster, masonry or timber frequently appears cooler than surrounding dry materials. Thermal imaging is commonly used to identify hidden moisture because these temperature differences can be detected without opening walls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thermoelite.ca/how-thermal-cameras-detect-hidden-problems-in-homes-and-buildings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thermoelite.ca">[Thermo Elite Inc+2Cueto Engineering]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thermoelite.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thermoelite.ca.</span></span></span>

A cold patch repeatedly observed in the same location may therefore indicate a moisture problem rather than an intermittent paranormal phenomenon.

### Missing Insulation

Infrared surveys regularly reveal insulation voids and thermal bridges in older buildings. These defects can produce sharply defined cool regions that remain stable over long periods.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://basc.pnnl.gov/diy-guides/look-missing-insulation-using-thermal-imaging" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: basc.pnnl.gov">[basc.pnnl.gov+2flir.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">basc.pnnl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on pnnl.gov.</span></span></span>

From a paranormal perspective, a recurring cold spot may seem significant because it appears consistently. From a building-science perspective, consistency is exactly what would be expected from a structural defect.

## Why Thermal Images Can Be Misleading

Thermal imaging is powerful, but interpreting images correctly requires caution.

Surface [reflections]({{ 'reflections/' | relative_url }}) can create apparent hot or cold areas that do not correspond directly to the object's actual temperature. Environmental conditions, viewing angle and material properties can all influence what the camera records. Professional thermographers receive specific training because thermal images can be easy to misread.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iirt.sg/the-challenges-and-limitations-of-infrared-thermography/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iirt.sg">[iirt.sg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iirt.sg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on iirt.sg.</span></span></span>

The equipment itself also has limitations. Lower-cost thermal cameras may have reduced accuracy and resolution, making small temperature differences harder to interpret reliably. Environmental factors such as sunlight, wind and changing ambient conditions can further affect readings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12130" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

A particularly important limitation for paranormal claims is that unusual shapes in thermal images do not automatically correspond to physical objects. Temperature patterns can form complex outlines that the human brain naturally interprets as faces, figures or human forms. Without independent confirmation from other evidence, a shape alone carries little evidential weight.

## How to Document a Cold Spot Responsibly

Thermal imaging can contribute useful information to an investigation when it is treated as a measurement tool rather than a ghost detector.

A responsible approach includes:

1. **Record environmental conditions.** Note outdoor temperature, weather, heating-system status and ventilation conditions.
2. **Capture repeat measurements.** A single image may be misleading. Repeated observations help establish whether the anomaly is stable, changing or transient.
3. **Compare with direct temperature readings.** Use calibrated thermometers alongside thermal images.
4. **Inspect for physical causes.** Check windows, doors, vents, pipe runs, insulation defects and signs of dampness.
5. **Document location precisely.** Record distances, camera settings and viewing angles so results can be replicated.

## Can Thermal Cameras Really Find Ghosts?

Thermal cameras can unquestionably detect real temperature differences. They can reveal cold spots, heat loss, airflow patterns, moisture intrusion and other environmental features that people may experience as part of a haunted [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}). What they cannot do is identify the source of those anomalies as supernatural. [The Department of Energy's Energy.gov+2flir.com](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/thermographic-inspections)

For that reason, thermal images occupy an unusual position in ghost investigations. They are often among the most objective forms of documentation available, yet they remain fundamentally ambiguous. A thermal image can demonstrate that something is colder or warmer than expected. It cannot demonstrate that a ghost caused it. The image is therefore best understood as a starting point for investigation rather than a conclusion.
