## Introduction

The wall writings at Borley Rectory did more than add another strange phenomenon to an already famous haunting. They gave the case a narrative. Instead of unexplained noises, [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}) and [moving objects]({{ 'moving-objects/' | relative_url }}), the messages appeared to tell a story involving a trapped spirit, a plea for rescue, and a mysterious French nun named Marie Lairre. For believers, the writings seemed to provide direct communication from the source of the haunting. For sceptics, they became one of the strongest indications that human agency was shaping the Borley legend. The messages remain among the most debated pieces of evidence in the entire Borley case because they sit at the intersection of witness testimony, folklore, alleged paranormal activity and possible fraud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaBorley Rectory - Psi Encyclopedia18 Sept 2015 — spontaneous writing on walls and paper Marianne Foyster thrown from her b...</span></span></span>

## Messages Addressed to Marianne Foyster

Most of the famous wall writings appeared during the years when Reverend Lionel Foyster and his wife Marianne lived at [Borley]({{ 'borley/' | relative_url }}) Rectory between 1930 and 1935. Reports from that period described numerous disturbances focused on Marianne herself, including [thrown objects]({{ 'thrown-objects/' | relative_url }}), unexplained noises, alleged assaults and mysterious written communications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaBorley Rectory - Psi Encyclopedia18 Sept 2015 — spontaneous writing on walls and paper Marianne Foyster thrown from her b...</span></span></span>

What made the writings distinctive was their apparent personal focus. Several messages addressed Marianne directly, with phrases interpreted as appeals for help. The best-known examples included wording such as “Marianne, please help me get out”, apparently written on walls or other surfaces inside the rectory. Supporters of the haunting interpreted these messages as attempts by an imprisoned spirit to communicate through the living occupant most closely associated with the disturbances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

The messages altered the character of the Borley haunting in three important ways:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* They suggested intelligence rather than random paranormal activity.
* They implied a hidden history behind the manifestations.
* They created a recurring relationship between the alleged spirit and Marianne Foyster.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

</div>

Yet the writings immediately raised difficult questions. Unlike [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}) or distant apparitions, written messages leave [physical traces]({{ 'physical-traces/' | relative_url }}) that can potentially be examined. Critics therefore asked who had actually written them, when they appeared, and whether the handwriting resembled that of known residents. Later commentators and handwriting comparisons repeatedly focused on Marianne herself as a possible author, although the issue has never been universally settled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foxearth.org.uk/BorleyRectory/WallWritings.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foxearth.org.uk">[foxearth.org.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foxearth.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">No Hand Was Visible-The Wall WritingsMarianne&#x27;s name on the wall writings. ## The Marie Lairre Story

The wall writings gained even greater significance after Harry Price's later investigations linked them to a specific ghostly identity. During séances and planchette sessions associated with the Borley [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}) in the late 1930s, a spirit calling herself Marie Lairre allegedly emerged as the central figure behind the haunting narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

According to the story communicated through these sessions, Marie Lairre was a young French nun who had travelled to England after leaving her religious order in order to marry a member of the Waldegrave family. The account claimed that she was murdered on the Borley site centuries earlier and secretly buried there, either beneath the building or in a well. The wall messages were then interpreted as her desperate attempts to obtain discovery and release.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

This narrative became one of the most memorable elements of the Borley legend because it supplied what many hauntings lack: a named victim, a motive and an apparent historical tragedy. The haunting was no longer merely a collection of strange incidents. It became a mystery story centred on an identifiable individual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

However, researchers encountered major problems when attempting to verify the tale:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* No convincing historical records have been found confirming the existence of a nun named Marie Lairre connected with Borley.
* The details of the story appeared only after paranormal communications rather than in documented local history.
* Searches for physical evidence supporting the alleged murder produced no conclusive results.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.umbc.edu/specialcollections/garrett/hauntings/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: library.umbc.edu">[AOK Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">library.umbc.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AOK LibraryThe Haunting of Borley Rectory – Garrett CollectionOn January 26, 1956, the Society for Psychical Research (SPR) published a r...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 26, 1956</span></span></span>

</div>

## Why Written Appeals Changed the Haunting

Many haunted-house cases contain reports of sounds, apparitions or moving objects. Written messages are much rarer, and that rarity helps explain why the Borley writings became so influential.

A written message appears purposeful. It implies language, intention and identity. Readers naturally begin asking who wrote it and why. At Borley, the messages transformed a scattered collection of phenomena into what seemed to be a coherent account of suffering and imprisonment. The writings effectively linked the alleged haunting to a human drama.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

The messages also increased emotional engagement. An unexplained knock on a wall is mysterious; an appeal that appears to beg for rescue is personal. By addressing Marianne directly, the writings placed a living participant inside the story rather than merely making her an observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/DannyRobinsOfficial/posts/paranormal-or-extramarital-activitydid-marianne-foyster-who-lived-at-borley-rect/988764340596940/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Paranormal or extramarital activity?! ## Did the Writings Have a Natural Source?

The strongest challenge to the paranormal interpretation comes from later investigations and statements by people involved in the case. Members of the Society for [Psychical Research]({{ 'research-files/' | relative_url }}) who re-examined Borley concluded that many of the alleged phenomena could be explained by normal causes or deliberate fabrication. ## What the Wall Messages Really Added

The lasting importance of the Borley wall writings lies less in whether they were paranormal than in what they contributed to the legend. They supplied the haunting with a voice, a victim and a plot. Through the figure of Marie Lairre, Borley acquired a tragic backstory that connected scattered phenomena into a single narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

At the same time, the writings became one of the clearest battlegrounds between believers and sceptics. If they were genuine paranormal communications, they represented some of the most direct evidence ever reported at Borley. If they were written by a resident or investigator, they illustrate how stories can grow through human intervention and later become inseparable from the haunting itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2AOK Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

That tension explains why the wall writings and the Marie Lairre mystery remain central to discussions of Borley Rectory.
