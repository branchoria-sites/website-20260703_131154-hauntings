## Introduction

Reports of haunted houses often focus on sounds: [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}) overhead, unexplained knocks, or a vague sense that something is moving through the building. Less obvious is another possibility. Some buildings produce extremely low-frequency vibrations that are felt more than heard. Known as infrasound, these vibrations can originate from ventilation systems, ageing pipes, boilers, fans, traffic, or large resonant spaces. Research suggests that infrasound can influence mood, increase stress, and contribute to feelings of unease. It does not prove that ghost experiences are imaginary, nor does it explain every haunting report. However, it offers a plausible [mechanism]({{ 'mechanism/' | relative_url }}) for why certain places can feel disturbing even when no clear source of discomfort is apparent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrasound" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## What Infrasound Means in a Building

Infrasound refers to sound waves below roughly 20 hertz (Hz), a frequency range at the edge of or below normal human hearing. Although people may not consciously hear these vibrations, they can sometimes detect them through bodily sensations, [pressure]({{ 'pressure/' | relative_url }}), or subtle physiological responses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrasound" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

In buildings, infrasound can arise from several ordinary sources:

* Ventilation and air-handling systems.
* Boilers, pumps, and mechanical plant rooms.
* Vibrating pipes and ductwork.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.frontiersin.org/news/2026/04/27/vibrating-pipes-restless-spirits-increased-stress-irritability-infrasound-exposure-paranormal-experiences-frontiers-behavioral-neuroscience" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontiersin.org">[frontiersin.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontiersin.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">increased stress and irritability from infrasound exposure...27 Apr 2026 — Humans exposed to infrasound don&#x27;t detect it, but have raised...</span></span></span>
* Traffic or industrial activity transmitted through structures.
* Wind interacting with large architectural spaces, shafts, or cavities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencealert.com/hidden-phenomenon-could-explain-why-old-buildings-feel-haunted-study-finds" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencealert.com">[ScienceAlert+2The Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencealert.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Alert Hidden Phenomenon Could Explain Why Old Buildings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hidden Phenomenon Could Explain Why Old Buildings...April 28, 2026 — 29 Apr 2026 — &quot;In an old building, there is a good chan...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 28, 2026</span></span></span>

These sources are particularly relevant to older buildings, basements, tunnels, and large stone or concrete structures. Such locations frequently appear in haunting stories, making it tempting to attribute discomfort to supernatural causes when a physical source is difficult to identify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencealert.com/hidden-phenomenon-could-explain-why-old-buildings-feel-haunted-study-finds" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencealert.com">[ScienceAlert]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencealert.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Alert Hidden Phenomenon Could Explain Why Old Buildings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hidden Phenomenon Could Explain Why Old Buildings...April 28, 2026 — 29 Apr 2026 — &quot;In an old building, there is a good chan...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 28, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why Researchers Became Interested in Haunted Locations

The most famous infrasound-and-[hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) case involves British engineer Vic Tandy. In the 1990s, Tandy worked in a laboratory where staff reported strange sensations and an oppressive [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}). One evening he experienced intense unease and thought he glimpsed a grey figure in his peripheral vision. Investigating the environment, he discovered a strong low-frequency vibration generated by an extractor fan. He later proposed that infrasound near 19 Hz had contributed to the unsettling experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vic_Tandy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vic Tandy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vic Tandy</span></span></span>

Tandy's work became influential because it linked a specific environmental factor to sensations often described in haunting accounts. He later investigated other allegedly haunted sites and found unusually strong low-frequency vibrations in some of them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vic_Tandy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vic Tandy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vic Tandy</span></span></span>

Researchers did not treat this as proof that ghosts were caused by sound waves. Instead, it suggested that environmental conditions might sometimes create experiences that people interpret through a paranormal lens.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[richardwiseman.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Published in the Journal of the Society for Psychical Research. ## How Low Frequencies Can Affect Mood and Stress

The strongest modern evidence concerns mood and physiological stress rather than [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}).

A notable public experiment organised by researchers including Richard Wiseman exposed audience members to music that occasionally contained a very low-frequency 17 Hz tone. Participants did not know when the tone was present. Pieces containing the low-frequency component were more likely to be associated with feelings such as unease, sadness, chills, nervousness, or fear. Around one-fifth of participants reported unusual sensations during the infrasonic sections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrasound" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

More recent laboratory work has strengthened the case that infrasound can influence emotional state. In a controlled study published in 2026, volunteers were exposed to music with and without hidden 18 Hz infrasound. Participants could not reliably tell when the infrasound was present. Nevertheless, those exposed to it reported greater irritability, lower interest, and more negative emotional reactions. Researchers also measured elevated cortisol, a hormone associated with stress and vigilance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/apr/27/spooky-feelings-in-old-houses-may-be-caused-by-boiler-sounds-study-suggests" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian+2Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Researchers discovered that even though participants could not consciously hear infrasound, exposure led to increased irritability, eleva...</span></span></span>

## Why Expectation Still Matters

Even if infrasound contributes to discomfort, interpretation remains crucial.

Imagine entering a basement in an old building. The [pipes]({{ 'pipes/' | relative_url }}) vibrate faintly, ventilation equipment produces low-frequency energy, and the space already feels isolated. A person may experience tension, alertness, or a vague sense that something is wrong. Infrasound may therefore act less as a direct producer of ghost experiences and more as a background factor that makes paranormal interpretations seem more convincing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[richardwiseman.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Published in the Journal of the Society for Psychical Research. ## Why Infrasound Is Not a Complete Ghost Explanation

Although infrasound is a credible factor in some haunting reports, researchers generally regard it as a partial explanation rather than a universal one.

First, many allegedly haunted buildings show no unusual infrasonic levels when measured. The mechanism cannot explain cases where the environmental conditions are absent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/04/that-spooky-sensation-likely-due-to-rumbling-pipes-not-spirits/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arstechnica.com">[Ars Technica]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arstechnica.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">that spooky sensation likely due to rumbling pipes not spirits</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ars TechnicaStudy: Infrasound likely a key factor in alleged hauntingsApr 27, 2026 — Tandy thought infrasound was the culprit of an alleg...</span></span></span>

* Poor lighting and visual ambiguity.
* Sleep disruption and fatigue.
* Social suggestion and local legends.

It may help explain why some environments feel oppressive or unsettling, particularly around mechanical systems and resonant spaces, but it does not by itself account for the full range of haunting claims.

Controlled experiments show measurable effects on mood and stress, and several well-known haunting investigations have identified infrasound as a plausible contributor to eerie sensations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/apr/27/spooky-feelings-in-old-houses-may-be-caused-by-boiler-sounds-study-suggests" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian+2Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Researchers discovered that even though participants could not consciously hear infrasound, exposure led to increased irritability, eleva...</span></span></span>
