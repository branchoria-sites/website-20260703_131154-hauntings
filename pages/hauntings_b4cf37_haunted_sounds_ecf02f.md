## Introduction

Haunted [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}), knocks and other house sounds are among the most persuasive parts of haunting stories because they feel directed at the listener. A shape glimpsed in poor light may be doubted; a slow tread above an empty room, a rap that seems to answer a question, or a scrape behind a wall can feel like contact. Yet sound is also one of the easiest senses to misread in a building. Timber expands and contracts, pipes bang, heating systems click, pests move through voids, and low-frequency vibration can affect mood without being consciously heard. The result is not simply “nothing happened”; it is often a real sound with an uncertain source, made more meaningful by darkness, [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}) and the reputation of the place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/research/notes-investigators" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk+2GEICO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Notes for InvestigatorsAmong the types of phenomena most commonly reported in this type of case are thumps, raps, scratchings and scuffli...</span></span></span>

That is why haunted sounds deserve their own treatment within [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}). They sit between folklore, building physics and perception: not as proof of ghosts, but as a mechanism by which ordinary houses can start to feel personal, responsive and inhabited by someone unseen.

## What people report when a house sounds haunted

In classic haunting accounts, sound often arrives before sight. People hear footsteps crossing an upper floor, raps from a wall, a door opening or closing, furniture moving, whispers, scuffling, scratching, or a heavy thump from an empty room. The Society for Psychical Research’s investigator notes list many of these as common reports in haunting and poltergeist cases, including “thumps, raps, scratchings and scuffling noises”, footsteps, doors opening and closing, whispers and voices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/research/notes-investigators" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Notes for InvestigatorsAmong the types of phenomena most commonly reported in this type of case are thumps, raps, scratchings and scuffli...</span></span></span>

Footsteps are especially powerful because they imply a body. A creak may be just a creak; a repeated sequence can become “someone walking”. Knockings carry a different charge. They seem intentional because they resemble a human signal: one knock to attract attention, two or three to answer, a pattern repeated across nights. In spiritualist history, this quality mattered enormously. The Fox sisters’ 1848 Hydesville “rappings” were reported as knocks from a spirit and helped launch modern spiritualism, before later accounts and sceptical histories treated the case as a hoax or performance built around joint-cracking and suggestion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ghost-hoax-spiritualism-fox-sisters" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How a Hoax by Two Sisters Helped Spark the Spiritualism</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How a Hoax by Two Sisters Helped Spark the Spiritualism...October 12, 2022 — 12 Oct 2022 — Teenager Maggie Fox and her younger si...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 12, 2022</span></span></span>

Poltergeist cases show how quickly sound can become a social event. The Enfield case began in 1977 with reports from a north London house of furniture moving and knocking sounds on walls; later claims included loud noises, voices and thrown objects, while sceptics have argued that the evidence was weak, contaminated by media attention, or partly fabricated. Whether one treats Enfield as paranormal, mischief, misinterpretation or a mixture, its public life shows the narrative force of knocks: once the sounds seemed to answer the household, neighbours, reporters and investigators became part of the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enfield_poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Enfield poltergeist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Enfield poltergeist</span></span></span>

Borley Rectory, long marketed as “the most haunted house in England”, also relied heavily on auditory claims. Eyewitness reports collected around the case included mysterious knocking sounds, moving objects and other disturbances, alongside more visual legends such as the ghostly nun. Later scrutiny of Borley has raised problems about exaggeration, weak documentation and the role of investigator Harry Price, but the case remains a useful example of how noises, legends and publicity can reinforce one another until a building’s soundscape becomes inseparable from its reputation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://library.umbc.edu/specialcollections/garrett/hauntings/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: umbc.edu">[library.umbc.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">umbc.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Haunting of Borley Rectory – Garrett Collection Eyewitness reports describe visions of apparitions, moving objects, mysterious knocki</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Haunting of Borley Rectory – Garrett Collection Eyewitness reports describe visions of apparitions, moving objects, mysterious knocki</span></span></span>

## Why buildings can sound as if someone is moving

A building is never entirely still. It responds to heat, cold, moisture, air pressure, water pressure and vibration. At night those small movements become easier to notice because traffic, conversation and daytime activity fall away. The listener also tends to be still, alert and trying to locate the source, which makes intermittent sounds feel more patterned than they may be.

Several ordinary mechanisms are especially good at imitating haunted activity:

* **Timber movement:** Floorboards, joists, rafters and roof structures can creak, pop or bang as they expand, contract or shift with changes in temperature and humidity. In older houses, loosened nails and rubbing boards can make empty rooms sound occupied.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nypost.com/2024/10/29/real-estate/8-things-that-make-your-home-seem-haunted-and-whats-really-going-on/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nypost.com">[New York Post]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nypost.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New York Post8 things that make your home seem haunted-and what&#x27;s really going on</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New York Post8 things that make your home seem haunted-and what&#x27;s really going on</span></span></span>
* **Pipes and heating:** Hot water moving through pipes can make them expand and knock against walls or one another. “Water hammer” can produce sudden bangs when water flow stops abruptly and sends a shockwave through pipework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hencoplumbing.com/water-hammer-or-haunted-house/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hencoplumbing.com">[Henco Plumbing Services]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hencoplumbing.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Henco Plumbing Services Water Hammer or Haunted House?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Henco Plumbing Services Water Hammer or Haunted House?</span></span></span>

## The acoustics that make ordinary sounds feel personal

Haunted sounds are not only produced by building systems; they are shaped by architecture. A knock in a pipe chase can travel along joists and emerge as if it came from the ceiling. A sound in a loft may seem to come from directly overhead even when the source is several metres away. Large rooms, stairwells, cellars and corridors can reflect and filter sound, making it harder to judge direction.

This matters because human hearing is good at detecting change but imperfect at locating hidden sources indoors. Hard surfaces create [reflections]({{ 'reflections/' | relative_url }}); carpets and furniture absorb some frequencies but not others; walls transmit low thumps better than high detail. A small impact can therefore become a vague “footstep”, while a pipe movement can become a wall knock. The more intermittent the sound, the more the listener may link separate events into one sequence.

Low-frequency sound adds another layer. Infrasound is sound below the usual threshold of conscious hearing, often described as below 20 hertz. A 2026 study discussed in *Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience* and reported by the *Smithsonian Magazine* and *The Guardian* found that inaudible low-frequency sound could affect mood and stress responses; participants exposed to infrasound showed more irritability and raised cortisol, even though they could not consciously hear it. The researchers suggested that old pipes, boilers and ventilation systems could contribute to uneasy sensations in buildings, especially when people already expect a haunting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/worried-your-house-is-haunted-the-spooky-sensation-might-arise-from-a-surprisingly-simple-source-180988622/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine Worried Your House Is Haunted?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Spooky Sensation...28 Apr 2026 — Even though we can&#x27;t usually hear these noises, they can put us in an uneasy state of mind and indu...</span></span></span>

Participants did report unusual sensations, but the effects did not map neatly onto the manipulated environmental conditions, suggesting that suggestion and individual expectation were at least as important as the physical stimuli.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010945208001299" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sciencedirect.com.</span></span></span>

## Why knocks seem to answer back

Knocks are unusually easy to turn into dialogue. They have clear starts and stops, they can be counted, and they invite the listener to ask a question: “Do that again.” If another knock follows, coincidence begins to feel like response. This is the same structure that made nineteenth-century spirit rapping so persuasive: a simple sound became meaningful because people treated it as a code.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ghost-hoax-spiritualism-fox-sisters" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How a Hoax by Two Sisters Helped Spark the Spiritualism</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How a Hoax by Two Sisters Helped Spark the Spiritualism...October 12, 2022 — 12 Oct 2022 — Teenager Maggie Fox and her younger si...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 12, 2022</span></span></span>

The problem is that buildings already produce irregular signals. A pipe may knock after a tap is turned off; a radiator may click as it heats and cools; a joist may pop after a temperature change; an animal may pause and move again. When a listener begins testing the sound, confirmation bias can take over. The “hits” are remembered — the knock that came just after the question — while the misses fade into the background.

This does not make every witness dishonest. It means that responsiveness is a high bar. For a knock to count as more than a suggestive coincidence, an investigator would need controlled conditions: accurate timing, exclusion of plumbing and heating cycles, checks for hidden people or animals, repeatable responses to blinded questions, and recordings that capture the sound clearly enough to analyse. Many famous cases became famous before that level of control was possible or before anyone present wanted to slow the story down.

## Natural explanations to check first

A practical approach to haunted sounds begins by treating the noise as real and looking for its route through the building. Dismissing it too quickly can miss safety problems; assuming it is supernatural can do the same. Some “ghostly” noises are also maintenance warnings.

The most useful first checks are simple:

1. **Log the timing.** Note the exact time, weather, heating schedule, hot-water use, appliance cycles and where people were in the house. A pattern around heating start-up, cooling after sunset, washing machines, showers or boiler use points towards building systems rather than an agency.
2. **Map the sound path.** Stand in different rooms while another person turns taps on and off, walks upstairs, opens doors, runs heating, or moves through the loft. Sounds often travel along structures and appear to come from the wrong place.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/hauntings_b4cf37_haunted_sounds_ecf02f-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Haunted Sounds illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/hauntings_b4cf37_haunted_sounds_ecf02f-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/hauntings_b4cf37_haunted_sounds_ecf02f-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

3. **Check pipes and heating.** Repeated banging, clanking, gurgling or knocking may involve water hammer, trapped air, loose pipes, pressure issues or boiler faults. Persistent or worsening noises deserve professional attention. [Henco Plumbing Services](https://www.hencoplumbing.com/water-hammer-or-haunted-house/)

4. **Inspect lofts, basements and wall voids.** Scratching, scurrying or thumps can indicate pests, which may damage insulation, wiring or timber. [hippo.com](https://www.hippo.com/blog/house-settling-noises)

5. **Look for movement in timber and fittings.** Loose floorboards, warped doors, old hinges, poorly fixed pipes and expanding roof timbers can make sounds that resemble footsteps or knocks. [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2024/10/29/real-estate/8-things-that-make-your-home-seem-haunted-and-whats-really-going-on/)

6. **Treat dangerous signs seriously.** Hissing near gas appliances, buzzing in walls, running water when all taps are off, or electrical flickering should be handled as safety issues, not ghost evidence. [GEICO](https://www.geico.com/living/6-house-noises-you-should-never-ignore/)

This kind of checking does not remove the emotional force of the experience. It simply changes the question from “Was that a ghost?” to “What conditions would have to be ruled out before a ghost becomes the best explanation?”

## Why the story persists even after explanations

Haunted sounds persist because they combine ambiguity with intimacy. A vague cold spot may be uncomfortable, but footsteps above the bed suggest someone is there. A knock on the wall suggests attention. A scrape in an empty room suggests movement. The listener is not just observing a mystery; they feel addressed by it.

Psychology helps explain why that feeling can strengthen. Studies of paranormal belief and perception suggest that expectation affects how people interpret ambiguous stimuli. Research on auditory pareidolia — hearing meaningful words or signals in unclear sound — has shown that framing matters: when ambiguous recordings are introduced as possible [ghost voices]({{ 'ghost-voices/' | relative_url }}), people may be more likely to hear them that way. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9473424/)

Belief is not the only factor. Stress, tiredness, grief, unfamiliar surroundings and darkness can all heighten vigilance. A person alone at night in a reputedly haunted building is listening differently from someone inspecting the same house at midday with a torch and a plumber. The sound has not changed; the interpretive setting has.

This is also why haunted sounds travel so well as stories. A sound report is easy to retell: “We heard footsteps in the room above, but no one was there.” It has a beginning, a test and a contradiction. Unlike a photograph, it does not have to be shown; the absence of a visible source is part of the point. Each retelling can sharpen the pattern, remove uncertainty and attach the noise to a named former resident, a tragedy, or a room already known for strange events.

## Are haunted footsteps ever just a building?

Yes, haunted footsteps can absolutely be a building. So can many knocks, bangs, raps, scrapes and thumps. Timber movement, pipes, heating systems, pests, draughts and acoustic transmission are not weak afterthoughts; they are strong first-line explanations for many reports. The more a sound occurs at certain times, follows heating or water use, comes from walls or ceilings, or changes with weather, the more likely a building mechanism becomes.

But “just a building” should not be heard as “nothing happened”. In many cases, something did happen: a noise was produced, transmitted, mislocated and interpreted under emotionally charged conditions. That is exactly why haunted-house sounds are so persistent. They are not usually pure invention. They are often ordinary mechanisms arriving in the one form most likely to feel personal: a step, a knock, a movement, a sign that someone unseen is close.

      });
