## Introduction

When a haunting story includes physical illness, the most important question may not be whether a ghost is present but whether carbon monoxide is. Certain symptoms strongly point towards toxic exposure rather than a paranormal cause. Headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, confusion and unusual fatigue are among the most common signs of carbon monoxide poisoning, especially when they occur inside a particular building and improve after leaving it. Health authorities consistently warn that these symptoms can be subtle, easily mistaken for flu, stress or poor sleep, and therefore easy to overlook.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carbon-monoxide-poisoning/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhs.uk">[nhs.uk+2CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nhs.uk.</span></span></span>

They are evidence that should change the direction of the [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}) from supernatural speculation to immediate [safety checks]({{ 'safety-checks/' | relative_url }}).

## Ghost Story or Carbon Monoxide Warning?

A classic ghost report often centres on unusual perceptions: a sensed presence, a shadow in the corner of a room, a feeling of dread, or the impression that someone is nearby. Carbon monoxide exposure can sometimes be associated with such experiences, but what separates a possible poisoning case from a purely paranormal claim is the presence of accompanying physical symptoms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.realclearscience.com/blog/2016/10/the_ghost_story_in_a_scientific_journal.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: realclearscience.com">[RealClearScience]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">realclearscience.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Real Clear Science The Ghost Story in a Scientific Journal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Ghost Story in a Scientific JournalMarch 17, 2023 — 31 Oct 2016 — Carbon monoxide poisoning can cause all manner of h...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 17, 2023</span></span></span>

## Symptoms That Should Change the Question

The key distinction is not whether someone saw something strange. It is whether the strange experience occurred alongside signs of oxygen deprivation and poisoning.

Consider two different scenarios:

* A person occasionally hears unexplained noises but is otherwise healthy.
* A person hears noises, feels watched, develops headaches, becomes dizzy, struggles to think clearly and feels markedly better elsewhere.

The second scenario contains a cluster of recognised carbon monoxide symptoms and therefore deserves urgent practical investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carbon-monoxide-poisoning/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhs.uk">[nhs.uk+2CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nhs.uk.</span></span></span>

Confusion is especially significant. Carbon monoxide affects the brain, and health authorities list altered consciousness, impaired judgement and disorientation among important warning signs. These cognitive effects can make unusual perceptions feel convincing because the person experiencing them may also have reduced ability to evaluate what is happening.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/carbon-monoxide-properties-incident-management-and-toxicology/carbon-monoxide-general-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK+2GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">carbon monoxide general information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carbon monoxide: general information24 May 2022 — A brief exposure to small amounts of carbon monoxide may cause headache, flushing, naus...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2022</span></span></span>

Another important clue is that carbon monoxide often affects multiple people in the same environment. If several household members develop headaches, nausea or fatigue at roughly the same time, a shared environmental exposure becomes more plausible than a personal paranormal encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4922045/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Public Health Wales]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carbon Monoxide Poisoning - PMCby SR Mehta · 2011 · Cited by 51 — Initially, patients may complain of headache, dizziness, nausea, emo...</span></span></span>

## The Away-from-Home Clue

One of the strongest indicators of carbon monoxide exposure is a simple pattern: symptoms improve when people leave the building.

Energy and public-health guidance repeatedly identify this as a warning sign. If headaches, dizziness, nausea or confusion are most noticeable at home and ease when occupants spend time elsewhere, the location itself becomes part of the evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.energy-uk.org.uk/customers/carbon-monoxide/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: energy-uk.org.uk">[Energy UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">energy-uk.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Energy UKCarbon monoxideHeadaches; Dizziness; Nausea; Breathlessness; Collapse; Loss of consciousness. This pattern is especially important because many carbon monoxide symptoms are non-specific. A headache by itself could have dozens of causes. A headache that repeatedly appears in one house, affects multiple occupants and improves outdoors is far more suspicious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/carbon-monoxide-properties-incident-management-and-toxicology/carbon-monoxide-toxicological-overview" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">carbon monoxide toxicological overview</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carbon monoxide: toxicological overview24 May 2022 — The most common symptoms following acute exposure are headache, nausea, vomiting, ve...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2022</span></span></span>

From a [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) perspective, this can create a misleading narrative. Residents may conclude that a particular room feels oppressive or that a presence is attached to the property. In reality, the building may simply be where exposure is occurring. The environmental pattern is often more informative than the paranormal interpretation.

## When Fear Feels Paranormal but Illness Is the Better Explanation

Many haunting accounts focus on emotional states: dread, anxiety, unease or a feeling of [being watched]({{ 'being-watched/' | relative_url }}). These experiences can be memorable, but they are not reliable indicators of a supernatural cause.

Carbon monoxide poisoning can produce neurological effects that influence perception, mood and judgement. Historical discussions of alleged haunted houses have repeatedly highlighted cases in which reports of [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}), strange sensations or unexplained fears disappeared after faulty heating equipment was repaired. The well-known 1921 case described by physician William Wilmer remains influential because the supposedly haunted environment was ultimately linked to carbon monoxide exposure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oddathenaeum.com/haunted-houses/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oddathenaeum.com">[oddathenaeum.com+2UL Standards &amp; Engagement]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oddathenaeum.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">“Haunted” Houses</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Odd Athenaeum22 Oct 2020 — As far back as 1921 there has been a connection between “haunted” houses and carbon monoxide. The critical lesson is not that every ghost story has a toxic explanation. It is that physical symptoms provide a testable alternative explanation that must be examined before more extraordinary conclusions are considered.

## When to Seek Emergency Help

Carbon monoxide poisoning can become life-threatening quickly. Emergency medical attention is warranted if symptoms progress beyond mild headache or dizziness.

Health authorities identify the following as particularly serious warning signs:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Confusion or marked disorientation.
* Difficulty breathing.
* Chest pain.
* Severe weakness.
* Loss of consciousness.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.dbth.nhs.uk/carbon-monoxide-poisoning/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dbth.nhs.uk">[dbth.nhs.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dbth.nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DBTH HospitalsCarbon Monoxide PoisoningThe symptoms for carbon monoxide poisoning include: · Disorientation · Dizziness · Headaches · Nau...</span></span></span>

If carbon monoxide poisoning is suspected, the immediate priority is to get into fresh air and obtain medical help. Investigating the haunting can wait. Determining whether a building is safe cannot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/carbon-monoxide/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20370646" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mayoclinic.org">[Mayo Clinic+2CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mayoclinic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mayo ClinicCarbon monoxide poisoning - Diagnosis and treatment28 Mar 2025 — Symptoms can include headache, dizziness, nausea, shortness o...</span></span></span>

In the context of hauntings, the most important symptom-based distinction is straightforward: ghost stories do not reliably produce headaches, nausea, dizziness, confusion and patterned illness that improve away from the property. Carbon monoxide exposure does. Recognising that difference can turn a frightening mystery into a preventable safety emergency.
