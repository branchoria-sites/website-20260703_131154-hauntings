## Introduction

Haunted castles, hotels and tourist places turn haunting stories into experiences people can book, walk through, photograph and retell. Their appeal is not simply that visitors “believe in ghosts”. It is that a real location gives a legend weight: a locked tower, a draughty corridor, a former execution site, a bed-and-breakfast room, a ruined fort after sunset. The strongest haunted attractions usually combine three things: a memorable story, a place with visible age or drama, and a visitor format that lets people feel close to the mystery without needing proof.

That is why public haunted sites sit in a distinct corner of the wider hauntings topic. They are not private cases reported in one household; they are maintained, marketed and interpreted for visitors. English Heritage, the National Trust and Historic Royal Palaces all publish ghost stories connected to historic sites, while commercial venues such as the [Stanley Hotel]({{ 'stanley-hotel/' | relative_url }}), [Chillingham]({{ 'chillingham/' | relative_url }}) Castle and the Myrtles Plantation sell tours or overnight stays around their reputations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/members-area/members-magazine/stories-of-england-ghost-stories/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english-heritage.org.uk">[The Myrtles+5English Heritage+5National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english-heritage.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on english-heritage.org.uk.</span></span></span>

## When does a haunted place become an attraction?

A haunted place becomes an attraction when the story can be safely repeated in a visitable setting. The crucial shift is from rumour to programme: a legend is attached to a route, a room, a tour guide’s script, a museum label, a night-time event or a seasonal festival. Once that happens, the haunting becomes part of the visitor economy, not just [local folklore]({{ 'local-folklore/' | relative_url }}).

Castles are especially well suited to this transformation because their architecture already carries the imagery of imprisonment, execution, battle and aristocratic downfall. The Tower of London’s ghost stories draw on a site associated with state power, prisoners and executions, including well-known tales of [Anne Boleyn]({{ 'anne-boleyn/' | relative_url }}) and other long-dead figures; Historic Royal Palaces treats these stories as part of the Tower’s cultural afterlife rather than as verified supernatural evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hrp.org.uk/blog/famous-ghost-stories-of-the-tower-of-london/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hrp.org.uk">[Historic Royal Palaces]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hrp.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">famous ghost stories of the tower of london</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">famous ghost stories of the tower of london</span></span></span> Chillingham Castle in Northumberland goes further into the experience economy, advertising [ghost tours]({{ 'ghost-tours/' | relative_url }}) and ghost hunts throughout the year, with routes through the castle and grounds and practical instructions about clothing, torches and age restrictions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://chillingham-castle.com/ghosts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: chillingham-castle.com">[Chillingham Castle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">chillingham-castle.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on chillingham-castle.com.</span></span></span>

Hotels work differently. They turn the unsettling idea of sleeping where something unexplained might happen into the product itself. The Stanley Hotel in Colorado markets ghost tours and “Shining” tours, using both its historic identity and its connection to Stephen King’s *The Shining* as reasons to visit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stanleyhotel.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stanleyhotel.com">[Stanley Hotel Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stanleyhotel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on stanleyhotel.com.</span></span></span> [The Myrtles]({{ 'the-myrtles/' | relative_url }}) Plantation in Louisiana, now an accommodation and tour site, offers guided historical tours and evening mystery tours that explicitly focus on folklore and ghostly encounters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://themyrtles.com/pages/tours?srsltid=AfmBOoptJtOCGccUIlQ6zaQDkw8Vg0cGi1YgjmC2FLA4TCJcTn0lIsTD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: themyrtles.com">[The Myrtles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">themyrtles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on themyrtles.com.</span></span></span>

Tourist cities can scale the same idea across streets rather than inside one building. Edinburgh’s underground vault tours, for example, package stories of punishment, murder, body snatching and alleged restless spirits into guided routes beneath the city.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mercattours.com/view-tour/haunted-underground" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mercattours.com">[Mercat Tours Ltd, Edinburgh, Scotland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mercattours.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on mercattours.com.</span></span></span> Salem, Massachusetts shows the festival version: its Haunted Happenings season welcomes more than a million visitors to parades, museums, walking tours, attractions and Halloween events, with a 2025 Destination Salem release reporting 1.1 million unique downtown visitors during October and 96,600 on Halloween Day alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hauntedhappenings.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hauntedhappenings.org">[Haunted Happenings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hauntedhappenings.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hauntedhappenings.org.</span></span></span>

## What people report

Public haunted attractions tend to feature a narrower set of repeated reports than private haunting cases. The stories are memorable because they can be attached to exact spaces: a staircase, a room, a chapel, a corridor, a tower, a vault or a gate that closes at night.

Common reports include apparitions, footsteps, voices, music, sudden cold, touches, moving objects, strange photographs and the feeling of being watched. At the Tower of London, the attraction is partly the density of named ghosts: Anne Boleyn, the Princes in the Tower, a phantom bear and other figures from the site’s long history appear in modern retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hrp.org.uk/blog/famous-ghost-stories-of-the-tower-of-london/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hrp.org.uk">[Historic Royal Palaces]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hrp.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">famous ghost stories of the tower of london</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">famous ghost stories of the tower of london</span></span></span> At the Stanley Hotel, reports commonly focus on figures associated with the hotel’s founders, piano music attributed in legend to Flora Stanley, and stories around Room 217, where a chambermaid survived a 1911 gas explosion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cntraveler.com/stories/2016-04-14/by-golly-a-ghost-may-have-been-spotted-at-the-stanley-hotel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cntraveler.com">[Condé Nast Traveler]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cntraveler.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cntraveler.com.</span></span></span>

The most durable tourist hauntings usually have a “good room” or “good route”. This matters because the visitor needs a physical focus. Chillingham Castle can send people through “most haunted” areas of the castle and grounds; the Myrtles can guide visitors through a house with a folklore portfolio; Edinburgh tour operators can descend into vaults where darkness, stone, enclosed space and story all reinforce one another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://chillingham-castle.com/ghosts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: chillingham-castle.com">[Chillingham Castle+2The Myrtles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">chillingham-castle.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on chillingham-castle.com.</span></span></span>

A few sites build their haunted reputation around restrictions. Bhangarh Fort in Rajasthan is a strong example: Rajasthan Tourism describes it as a haunted place and says the fort remains out of bounds before sunrise and after sunset. The rule is practical for a fragile, isolated ruin, but the timing itself helps the legend persist because “not after dark” is exactly the sort of detail that visitors remember.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/bhangarh-fort.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tourism.rajasthan.gov.in">[tourism.rajasthan.gov.in]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tourism.rajasthan.gov.in</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rajasthan.gov.in.</span></span></span>

## Why castles and hotels make the story feel stronger

Haunted attractions work because the place does some of the storytelling before a guide says a word. A castle suggests danger, hierarchy and old violence. A hotel suggests intimacy: visitors sleep, undress, wake in the night and hear unfamiliar sounds. A tourist district suggests layers of public memory, where murder, punishment, plague, scandal or witchcraft can be mapped onto streets.

That does not mean the stories are historically clean. Many haunted-site narratives simplify the past, merge older legends with later publicity, or attach a named ghost to events that are more complicated than the tour version. English Heritage’s material on haunted monasteries is unusually useful on this point because it notes that many supernatural stories associated with its sites are modern in origin, even though some ghost and demon stories reach back to the Middle Ages and were later amplified by Gothic literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/members-area/members-magazine/haunted-monasteries/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: english-heritage.org.uk">[English Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">english-heritage.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on english-heritage.org.uk.</span></span></span>

The Winchester Mystery House shows how a building can become a tourist haunt through architecture as much as apparition. Its official history emphasises the facts: Sarah Winchester expanded an eight-room farmhouse into a 24,000-square-foot mansion with 160 rooms, 10,000 windows, 2,000 doors, 47 stairways and 47 fireplaces between 1886 and 1922.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://winchestermysteryhouse.com/history/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: winchestermysteryhouse.com">[Winchester Mystery House]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">winchestermysteryhouse.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Winchester Mystery House History</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Winchester Mystery House History</span></span></span> The supernatural legend — that she built endlessly to appease spirits — is far harder to verify, but the house’s confusing plan makes the legend feel plausible to visitors because the architecture appears to invite a story.

The Stanley Hotel’s case is different. Its haunted identity is reinforced by popular culture. Stephen King’s stay in 1974 inspired *The Shining*, but the hotel also trades on its own ghost-tour identity and historic setting in Estes Park. The visitor experience therefore blends three layers: the real hotel, the literary afterlife of the Overlook Hotel, and later paranormal stories told by guests and staff.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stanleyhotel.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stanleyhotel.com">[Stanley Hotel Portal+2People.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stanleyhotel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on stanleyhotel.com.</span></span></span>

## Natural explanations to check

A good haunted-site visit can be atmospheric without treating every odd sensation as supernatural evidence. Old public buildings are full of ordinary causes that can feel extraordinary in the right setting, especially when visitors arrive primed to notice them.

Several explanations are worth checking before treating a report as paranormal:

* **Building movement and acoustics:** Castles, hotels and old houses creak, settle, echo and transmit [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}) in surprising ways. Stone corridors, wooden floors, service passages and vaults can make sound seem to come from the wrong direction.
* **Draughts and temperature differences:** “Cold spots” may come from gaps, stairwells, chimneys, cellars, poorly insulated walls or changing outdoor conditions.
* **Low light and expectation:** Guided night tours encourage careful looking in shadows. Peripheral vision, reflective surfaces and uneven lighting can turn uncertain shapes into figures.
* **Infrasound and bodily unease:** Recent discussion of old “haunted” buildings has highlighted low-frequency sound from machinery, heating or plumbing as a possible contributor to discomfort, unease or anxiety that people may then interpret through a ghost story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.psychologytoday.com/gb/blog/time-travelling-with-apollo/202605/what-were-really-sensing-in-haunted-old-buildings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psychologytoday.com">[Psychology Today]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psychologytoday.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psychology Today What We&#x27;re Really Sensing in &quot;Haunted&quot; Old Buildings4 days ago — People often cite an &quot;uneasy feeling&quot; as evidence that</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psychology Today What We&#x27;re Really Sensing in &quot;Haunted&quot; Old Buildings4 days ago — People often cite an &quot;uneasy feeling&quot; as evidence that</span></span></span>
* **Environmental hazards:** Carbon monoxide, mould and other environmental problems can affect perception, mood and health, so serious recurring reports in accommodation should be treated as safety issues before they become entertainment. Accessible science explainers commonly stress that apparently paranormal experiences can sometimes arise from environmental and neurological causes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/five-scientific-explanations-spooky-sensations-180973436/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Smithsonian Magazine Five Scientific Explanations for Spooky Sensations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Smithsonian Magazine Five Scientific Explanations for Spooky Sensations</span></span></span>
* **Story priming:** A visitor told that a staircase is haunted is more likely to interpret an ambiguous sound or blur as meaningful. That does not make the experience fake; it shows how expectation shapes perception.

The point is not to drain the pleasure from ghost tourism. It is to separate three things that are often blended together: the history of the site, the folklore attached to it, and the physical experience of being there in darkness, silence or discomfort. Haunted attractions are strongest when they allow all three to be present without pretending they are the same kind of evidence.

## Why the story persists

Haunted tourist sites persist because they solve a problem for heritage: they make the past feel immediate. A visitor may not remember a full dynastic timeline, but they will remember the place where a queen is said to walk headless, the hotel room linked to a famous horror novel, or the vault where a guide lowered their voice before describing a murder.

Researchers often place ghost tours and haunted visits near dark tourism, meaning travel connected to death, tragedy, suffering or the macabre. Rachael Ironside’s work on death, ghosts and spiritual tourism argues that ghost tourism often draws on representations of the dead to create emotional engagement, while ghost hunting offers a more direct claimed connection with spirits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://rgu-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/2212534" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rgu-repository.worktribe.com">[OpenAIR@RGU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rgu-repository.worktribe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Open AIR@RGUDeath, ghosts, and spiritual tourism: conceptualizing a dark</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open AIR@RGUDeath, ghosts, and spiritual tourism: conceptualizing a dark</span></span></span> Other tourism research treats paranormal tourism as a visitor experience shaped by curiosity, fear, prior belief, narrative immersion and the desire for unusual emotions rather than simple credulity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.emerald.com/ihr/article/36/1/107/107212/Dark-tourism-motivations-and-visit-intentions-of" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: emerald.com">[Emerald]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">emerald.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on emerald.com.</span></span></span>

The persistence is also economic. Salem’s October tourism shows how a haunting-related identity can become a large civic event, not merely a few ghost walks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hauntedhappenings.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hauntedhappenings.org">[Haunted Happenings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hauntedhappenings.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hauntedhappenings.org.</span></span></span> At a smaller scale, castles and hotels use haunted programming to extend visiting hours, fill off-season periods, sell specialist tours, attract media attention and give returning visitors a fresh reason to come back.

There is a risk, though. Haunted tourism can flatten real suffering into entertainment. A prison, plantation, execution site or former asylum is not just a spooky backdrop; it may be connected to enslavement, punishment, illness, poverty or violence. The best haunted attractions balance atmosphere with interpretation: they let visitors enjoy suspense while making clear which parts are documented history, which parts are folklore, and which parts are commercial storytelling.

## A compact portfolio of tourist haunts

The range of haunted tourist places is broad, but a few examples show how different models work.

**The Tower of London** is the royal fortress model. Its ghost stories draw power from documented imprisonment, execution and political violence, but the attraction remains primarily a major heritage site. The hauntings add atmosphere to an already famous place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hrp.org.uk/blog/famous-ghost-stories-of-the-tower-of-london/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hrp.org.uk">[Historic Royal Palaces]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hrp.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">famous ghost stories of the tower of london</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">famous ghost stories of the tower of london</span></span></span>

**Chillingham Castle** is the specialist haunted castle model. It leans openly into ghost tours and ghost hunts while also presenting itself as a historic Northumberland fortress with gardens, tea rooms and accommodation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://chillingham-castle.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: chillingham-castle.com">[Chillingham Castle]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">chillingham-castle.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on chillingham-castle.com.</span></span></span>

**The Stanley Hotel** is the haunted hotel plus pop-culture model. Its visitor draw is not only reported ghost activity but its relationship to *The Shining*, which turns literary and film tourism into part of the haunting experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.stanleyhotel.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stanleyhotel.com">[Stanley Hotel Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stanleyhotel.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on stanleyhotel.com.</span></span></span>

**The Myrtles Plantation** is the haunted bed-and-breakfast model. It combines accommodation, historic-house tourism, folklore and evening mystery tours, while its own history page frames the site as a place with a complex past that has survived the Civil War and later reinvention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://themyrtles.com/pages/history-culture?srsltid=AfmBOopAqr7CYWu5z9LqIzFEwjMYeYA2B1VMmp2P60A64ErzDLOztGo4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: themyrtles.com">[The Myrtles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">themyrtles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on themyrtles.com.</span></span></span>

**Bhangarh Fort** is the restricted-ruin model. The story is strengthened by the rule against entry before sunrise and after sunset, a visitor-management measure that has become part of the haunting’s public identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/bhangarh-fort.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tourism.rajasthan.gov.in">[tourism.rajasthan.gov.in]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tourism.rajasthan.gov.in</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rajasthan.gov.in.</span></span></span>

**Edinburgh’s vaults and graveyard tours** are the urban-route model. Instead of one haunted room, the attraction is movement through layered city spaces where history, darkness, performance and enclosure create the experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mercattours.com/view-tour/haunted-underground" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mercattours.com">[Mercat Tours Ltd, Edinburgh, Scotland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mercattours.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on mercattours.com.</span></span></span>

## What to take seriously as a visitor

A haunted attraction does not need to prove ghosts exist to be worth visiting. The better question is what kind of experience it is offering. Some sites are heritage-first, using ghost stories as a doorway into architecture and history. Some are entertainment-first, designed for suspense, darkness and jumpy anticipation. Others sit in the middle, offering enough documented context to feel grounded while still giving visitors permission to enjoy the uncanny.

The most credible haunted tourist places are usually transparent about the difference between history and legend. They do not need to spoil the atmosphere; they simply avoid presenting every repeated story as fact. A guide can say that a queen was executed, that visitors have long reported a figure, and that no one can verify the apparition as a ghost. That distinction gives the story more, not less, staying power.

The lasting appeal of haunted castles, hotels and tourist places is that they make uncertainty visitable. People go because the setting lets them stand where the story is said to happen, test the atmosphere for themselves, and leave with a tale that is partly history, partly performance and partly personal reaction. In the broader world of hauntings, these public sites are where folklore becomes itinerary.
