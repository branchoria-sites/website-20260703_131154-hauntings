## Introduction

The question of whether Borley Rectory was truly haunted gradually became entangled with a different question: could Harry Price be trusted? By the mid-twentieth century, the focus of debate had shifted away from apparitions, mysterious writings and [poltergeist claims]({{ 'moving-objects/' | relative_url }}) toward the conduct of the investigator who had made the case famous. The Society for [Psychical Research]({{ 'research-files/' | relative_url }}) (SPR) launched a major reassessment after Price's death, and its conclusions transformed the history of Borley. Rather than settling the controversy, the investigation opened a long-running dispute over evidence, standards of inquiry and the reliability of one of Britain's most famous ghost hunters. The result was a second Borley story—one about institutional review, accusations of misconduct and attempts at rehabilitation—that became almost as influential as the haunting itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

## Did the SPR Debunk Borley or Deepen It?

When [Harry Price]({{ 'harry-price/' | relative_url }}) died in 1948, he left behind not only a celebrated haunted-house case but also a substantial archive of notes, reports and correspondence. Members of the SPR, an organisation that had often maintained an uneasy relationship with Price, began re-examining his claims. What followed was one of the most significant internal disputes in the history of psychical research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Price" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Harry Price</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harry Price</span></span></span>

The reassessment mattered because Price had become inseparable from [Borley]({{ 'borley/' | relative_url }}) Rectory itself. His books, especially *The Most Haunted House in England* (1940), shaped public understanding of the case. If his methods were unreliable, critics argued, then much of the Borley legend required reconsideration. If the criticism was unfair or exaggerated, supporters replied, then the SPR risked replacing one distortion with another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spookyisles.com/borley-rectory-haunting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spookyisles.com">[Spooky Isles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spookyisles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spooky Isles Borley Rectory: Guide To England&#x27;s Most Haunted House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spooky IslesBorley Rectory: Guide To England&#x27;s Most Haunted HouseJune 15, 2014 — 15 Jun 2014 — Here&#x27;s everything you need to know about t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2014</span></span></span>

Instead of ending debate, the SPR's intervention created a new layer of controversy. From that point onward, discussions of Borley almost always included arguments about Price's integrity as well as the alleged phenomena.

## The 1956 Critical Reassessment

The turning point came with the publication of *The Haunting of Borley Rectory* in 1956. Written by SPR investigators Eric Dingwall, Kathleen (Mollie) Goldney and Trevor Hall, the work became widely known as the "Borley Report". It represented a detailed review of witness testimony, documentary records and Price's own publications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Harry Price Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

The authors concluded that the evidence for haunting weakened substantially when examined critically. Events that had appeared mysterious in popular accounts could often be explained through ordinary causes, misunderstandings, exaggeration or deliberate fabrication. They argued that many celebrated incidents lost force when traced back to their original sources and contexts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Time]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

A particularly important feature of the report was its attention to witness reliability. The investigators revisited claims associated with different periods of the rectory's history and questioned whether some stories had been embellished as they were repeated. The report also drew attention to inconsistencies between private records and public narratives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C893376-2%2C00.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: content.time.com">[Time]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">content.time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GREAT BRITAIN: The Ghosts of Borley</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GREAT BRITAIN: The Ghosts of Borley - TIMEAt the least, say Researchers Eric Dingwall, Kathleen Goldney and Trevor Hall, Price was gu...</span></span></span>

For the SPR, the issue was larger than Borley. The case became a test of how psychical research should be conducted. The report therefore functioned not only as a critique of a famous haunting but also as a statement about evidential standards and investigative discipline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.harrypricewebsite.co.uk/Borley/PriceatBorley/HBR/hbr-contents.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: harrypricewebsite.co.uk">[Harry Price Website]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">harrypricewebsite.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harry Price WebsiteThe Haunting of Borley Rectory by Dingwall, Goldney &amp; HallThe results of their investigation dealt a tremendous blow t...</span></span></span>

## Charges Against Price's Account

The most controversial aspect of the reassessment was not its scepticism about ghosts but its scepticism about Harry Price himself.

The Borley investigators alleged that Price had, at minimum, overstated the strength of his evidence. Some critics went further, arguing that he had helped create or encourage certain phenomena. Reports circulated that during visits to Borley he may have been involved in staged incidents, including allegations that thrown stones and similar disturbances were not supernatural at all. These claims became central to later attacks on his reputation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Spooky Isles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

The report also challenged Price's presentation of the rectory [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}) as a uniquely compelling haunting. According to its authors, a number of celebrated incidents could be explained by [natural causes]({{ 'natural-causes/' | relative_url }}), practical jokes, human agency or the peculiar physical characteristics of the building. Rats, acoustics, misunderstandings and ordinary disturbances were proposed as explanations for events previously treated as paranormal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

Another significant element involved Marianne Foyster, whose years at Borley produced many of the case's most dramatic reports. Later statements attributed to her suggested that some supposed paranormal incidents had mundane origins and that certain stories had been exaggerated. Such testimony became an important foundation for critics arguing that the haunting narrative had become inflated over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

Trevor Hall emerged as the most persistent critic. His later writings expanded beyond Borley and examined Price's career more broadly. Hall argued that Price cultivated publicity and that aspects of his public image and investigative record deserved much greater scrutiny. These accusations helped transform the debate from a dispute over one haunted house into a broader argument about the credibility of a leading paranormal investigator.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/trevor-hall/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Trevor Hall</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaTrevor Hall - Psi Encyclopedia1 Dec 2021 — Harry Price. ## Robert Hastings and the Defence of Price

The SPR report did not go unanswered. Among the most important responses came from Robert Hastings, an SPR member who believed that the Borley reassessment had itself gone too far.

In 1969 Hastings published *An Examination of the Borley Report*, a lengthy critique of the 1956 investigation. Rather than arguing that every Borley phenomenon was genuine, Hastings focused on the fairness and methodology of the SPR's conclusions. He contended that some accusations against Price rested on weak foundations and that certain interpretations presented as decisive were less secure than critics claimed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

Hastings' intervention was important because it shifted the debate again. Instead of a simple choice between "haunted" and "not haunted", the argument became one about competing investigations. Supporters maintained that the Borley Report selectively interpreted evidence and judged Price too harshly. Critics replied that Hastings had failed to overturn the central findings. The result was a stalemate rather than a resolution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://secretswithinthefog.wordpress.com/2012/11/04/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: secretswithinthefog.wordpress.com">[black owl]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">secretswithinthefog.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">black owlborley rectory &#124; black owl</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WordPress.com4 Nov 2012 — Subsequently, Robert Hastings, an SPR member, discussed several of the charges of duplicity and falsification o...</span></span></span>

Other defenders joined the effort. Price's literary executor Paul Tabori and paranormal researcher Peter Underwood both challenged aspects of the case against him. Later writers sympathetic to Price continued to argue that the SPR report underestimated genuine anomalies and exaggerated the evidence for fraud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

Yet even among defenders, few attempted a complete vindication. The debate increasingly centred on proportionality: whether Price had been a deliberate deceiver, an overenthusiastic investigator, a showman who occasionally sacrificed rigour for publicity, or a researcher unfairly judged by later standards.

## Why the Dispute Became Part of the Legend

The fight over Harry Price ensured that Borley Rectory never settled into a single interpretation. In many haunted-house cases, sceptical investigation eventually pushes the supernatural claims into the background. Borley followed a different path. The critique itself became part of the story.

One reason is that the conflict mirrored broader tensions within psychical research. Price represented publicity, popular appeal and large-scale public investigations. The SPR's critics emphasised documentary analysis, witness evaluation and institutional scrutiny. The clash therefore reflected competing visions of how paranormal claims should be investigated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spookyisles.com/borley-rectory-haunting/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spookyisles.com">[Spooky Isles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spookyisles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Spooky Isles Borley Rectory: Guide To England&#x27;s Most Haunted House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Spooky IslesBorley Rectory: Guide To England&#x27;s Most Haunted HouseJune 15, 2014 — 15 Jun 2014 — Here&#x27;s everything you need to know about t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2014</span></span></span>

Another reason is that neither side achieved a universally accepted victory. The 1956 report severely damaged Price's reputation and remains influential among sceptical historians. Yet defenders continued to challenge parts of the critique, and later writers repeatedly revisited the evidence. As a result, Borley survived not because consensus emerged but because consensus failed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Spooky Isles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

The modern legacy of Borley Rectory therefore rests on two intertwined mysteries. The first concerns what, if anything, happened at the rectory. The second concerns how those events were recorded, interpreted and promoted.
