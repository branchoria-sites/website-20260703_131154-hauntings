## Introduction

The years when the Reverend Lionel Foyster and his wife Marianne lived at [Borley]({{ 'borley/' | relative_url }}) Rectory, from 1930 to 1935, produced the most dramatic haunting claims in the building’s history and later became the centre of the fiercest arguments about fraud. Reports from this period included flying objects, unexplained writing on walls, shattered windows, ringing bells, attacks on occupants and claims that Marianne herself was physically assaulted by unseen forces. Yet many of these same incidents later became evidence for sceptics who argued that the phenomena were exaggerated, staged or misunderstood. As a result, the “Foyster years” occupy a unique place in the Borley story: they are simultaneously the strongest case for believers and the strongest case for critics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaBorley Rectory - Psi Encyclopedia18 Sept 2015 — Many claims and counter-claims of fraud were made against Marianne Foyste...</span></span></span>

## Why the Foyster Years Divide Borley Readers

When the Foysters arrived at Borley Rectory in October 1930, the house already had a reputation for strange occurrences. What changed under their tenancy was the intensity and variety of the reports. Lionel Foyster kept detailed records and later supplied accounts that became a major source for [Harry Price]({{ 'harry-price/' | relative_url }})’s influential writings on Borley. Unlike many earlier stories, these reports described recurring events over several years rather than isolated sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/web.sas.upenn.edu/dist/b/160/files/2017/04/new_horizons_research_foundation_paper_19-1u2w21i.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com">[Cpb Us W2 Wpmucdn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cpb Us W2 Wpmucdn Borley Rectory: “The Most Haunted House in England”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cpb Us W2 WpmucdnBorley Rectory: “The Most Haunted House in England” - CDNApril 18, 2011 — On that first visit he accused Marianne Foyste...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2011</span></span></span>

The problem for historians is that much of the evidence depends on testimony from the Foyster household itself. Harry Price later publicised the accounts, but he was not present for most of the incidents. Critics therefore argue that the strongest haunting narratives were filtered through a chain of reporting that leaves room for error, embellishment or deliberate invention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/web.sas.upenn.edu/dist/b/160/files/2017/04/new_horizons_research_foundation_paper_19-1u2w21i.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com">[Cpb Us W2 Wpmucdn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cpb Us W2 Wpmucdn Borley Rectory: “The Most Haunted House in England”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cpb Us W2 WpmucdnBorley Rectory: “The Most Haunted House in England” - CDNApril 18, 2011 — On that first visit he accused Marianne Foyste...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2011</span></span></span>

## The Surge of Physical Phenomena

The Foyster period is remembered because the alleged disturbances moved beyond ghost sightings into what would now be called poltergeist activity. Reports included stones and bottles being thrown, bells ringing without visible cause, windows breaking, furniture disturbances and doors locking unexpectedly. Lionel Foyster also recorded incidents in which his adopted daughter Adelaide was allegedly trapped in a room and occasions when attempts at exorcism were interrupted by flying objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

The most controversial claims involved Marianne herself. According to accounts circulated by Price, she experienced repeated attacks from unseen forces and was even thrown from her bed. These stories transformed Borley from a traditional ghost tale into a dramatic case of physical haunting and became some of the most frequently quoted incidents in later books and documentaries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

Another important feature of the Foyster years was the appearance of written messages on walls. These inscriptions often addressed Marianne directly, asking for help or referring to religious themes. Because the messages appeared to communicate with a specific individual rather than merely haunting a location, they became one of the most memorable elements of the Borley legend.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.foxearth.org.uk/BorleyRectory/WallWritings.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: foxearth.org.uk">[foxearth.org.uk+2A London Inheritance]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">foxearth.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Chapter 11: No Hand Was Visible-The Wall WritingsThe &#x27;paranormal&#x27; writings first appeared in the spring of 1931 when the Foysters were li...</span></span></span>

## Why the Wall Messages Became a Flashpoint

The [wall writings]({{ 'wall-writing/' | relative_url }}) attracted unusual attention because they seemed personal. Several messages addressed Marianne by name, creating the impression that an unseen intelligence was trying to communicate with her. Harry Price later connected some of these writings to his wider narrative about a supposed murdered nun associated with the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

For believers, the writings represented one of the most difficult Borley phenomena to explain naturally. For critics, they represented one of the easiest. Later investigators argued that the messages looked as though they could have been written by a living person and questioned both their timing and their documentation. The writings therefore became a symbolic battleground in the wider dispute over Borley’s credibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaBorley Rectory - Psi Encyclopedia18 Sept 2015 — Many claims and counter-claims of fraud were made against Marianne Foyste...</span></span></span>

## Accusations Against Marianne Foyster

Suspicion fell on Marianne surprisingly early. During his involvement with the case, Harry Price reportedly suggested that she might be responsible for some of the disturbances. The accusation damaged relations between them and became the beginning of a long-running controversy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaBorley Rectory - Psi Encyclopedia18 Sept 2015 — Many claims and counter-claims of fraud were made against Marianne Foyste...</span></span></span>

After Price's death, investigators associated with the Society for [Psychical Research]({{ 'research-files/' | relative_url }}) conducted a major reassessment of the Borley evidence. Their 1956 study argued that many of the reported phenomena could be explained by ordinary causes, conscious fraud or misinterpretation. Marianne became a central figure in that reassessment. Critics suggested that some incidents may have been staged for amusement, attention or personal reasons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

Particularly damaging were later statements attributed to Marianne herself. She reportedly denied having witnessed genuine [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}) and admitted that some incidents had been produced through practical jokes or deliberate deception. She also acknowledged an extramarital relationship with a lodger and indicated that paranormal explanations could sometimes serve as a distraction from activities she wished to conceal. These admissions became powerful evidence for sceptics who viewed the Borley haunting as a mixture of folklore, misunderstanding and fraud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

## Later Claims About Lionel Foyster and Price

The controversy did not stop with Marianne. Some researchers argued that Lionel Foyster himself may have helped sustain belief in the haunting, whether consciously or unconsciously. Others suggested that his detailed record-keeping reflected sincere conviction rather than deception. Because many of the famous incidents come from his reports, assessments of Lionel's reliability have become inseparable from assessments of the haunting itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/web.sas.upenn.edu/dist/b/160/files/2017/04/new_horizons_research_foundation_paper_19-1u2w21i.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com">[Cpb Us W2 Wpmucdn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cpb Us W2 Wpmucdn Borley Rectory: “The Most Haunted House in England”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cpb Us W2 WpmucdnBorley Rectory: “The Most Haunted House in England” - CDNApril 18, 2011 — On that first visit he accused Marianne Foyste...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 2011</span></span></span>

Harry Price also became a target. The Society for Psychical Research's post-war [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}) concluded that some phenomena associated with Borley had been manipulated or exaggerated and accused Price of contributing to the mythology. Critics argued that his methods encouraged sensationalism and that he relied heavily on second-hand testimony from the Foyster years. Defenders of Price, however, challenged aspects of the SPR's conclusions and maintained that the later critique overstated the evidence for fraud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

The result is a three-way dispute. Some interpretations place responsibility mainly on Marianne. Others distribute responsibility between Marianne, Lionel and Price. A smaller group continues to argue that at least some of the phenomena remain unexplained despite evidence of trickery elsewhere in the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaBorley Rectory - Psi Encyclopedia18 Sept 2015 — Many claims and counter-claims of fraud were made against Marianne Foyste...</span></span></span>

## What the Foyster Claims Mean for the Borley Debate

The Foyster years matter because they concentrate nearly every major issue in [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) research: witness reliability, emotional stress, household dynamics, publicity, investigator influence and the possibility of fraud. Nearly all of Borley Rectory's most famous stories originate from this period, yet nearly all of the strongest sceptical arguments do as well.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaBorley Rectory - Psi Encyclopedia18 Sept 2015 — Many claims and counter-claims of fraud were made against Marianne Foyste...</span></span></span>

For that reason, Marianne Foyster remains one of the most consequential figures in the history of Borley Rectory. To believers, she stood at the centre of a remarkable outbreak of paranormal activity. To critics, she became the key to understanding how one of Britain's most famous hauntings may have been created. The enduring fascination of the Borley case lies in the fact that neither interpretation has entirely displaced the other.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/borley-rectory/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaBorley Rectory - Psi Encyclopedia18 Sept 2015 — Many claims and counter-claims of fraud were made against Marianne Foyste...</span></span></span>
