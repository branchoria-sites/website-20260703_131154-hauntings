## Introduction

Can suggestion beat the fear frequency? The best-known test of that question is the “Haunt” project, a controlled attempt to create an [artificial haunted]({{ 'haunt-room-7ad22a/' | relative_url }}) room and see whether unusual experiences were caused by environmental factors such as infrasound or by the expectations that visitors brought with them. The study became important because it challenged a simple version of the infrasound theory of hauntings. Researchers found that many participants reported strange sensations, feelings of presence, dizziness, tingling, or unease, but these reports did not reliably track whether infrasound or unusual electromagnetic fields were actually present. Instead, the findings suggested that [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}), suggestion, and the interpretation of ambiguous sensations may play a larger role than many acoustic explanations had assumed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project in which an attempt...</span></span></span>

Within the broader debate about infrasound and eerie feelings, the Haunt project is significant because it tested the theory under controlled conditions rather than relying on anecdotes from allegedly haunted buildings. Its results complicated the idea that low-frequency sound alone can explain why people feel that a place is haunted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project in which an attempt...</span></span></span>

## How the Artificial Haunted Room Was Set Up

The Haunt project, led by psychologist Christopher French and colleagues, was designed to recreate some of the environmental conditions that had been proposed as explanations for ghostly experiences. Researchers constructed a specially designed chamber and exposed participants to combinations of infrasound, complex electromagnetic fields (EMFs), both influences together, or neither. Seventy-nine volunteers spent approximately fifty minutes in the room and were asked to record any unusual sensations or experiences they noticed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project in which an attempt...</span></span></span>

The experiment did not simply place people in a neutral laboratory setting. The room was intentionally presented as an unusual environment associated with previous reports of strange experiences. This framing mattered because the study aimed to investigate not only physical influences but also how people interpret sensations when they expect something unusual to happen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/education/2003/jul/24/research.highereducation1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianScientist to create &#x27;haunted house&#x27; &#124; Research24 Jul 2003 — As a former magician, Wiseman has a few tricks up his sleeve - in...</span></span></span>

The design addressed a key weakness in many haunting claims. In reputedly haunted locations, multiple factors are mixed together: local legends, dim lighting, unusual architecture, prior beliefs, and environmental conditions. By controlling these variables, the researchers hoped to determine whether specific physical factors produced the reported effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project in which an attempt...</span></span></span>

## What Participants Reported With and Without Infrasound

Participants reported a surprisingly wide range of experiences. Common reports included feelings of dizziness, tingling sensations, temperature changes, a sense of presence, emotional shifts, and moments that some described as odd or uncanny. At first glance, these reports seemed to support the idea that a room could be made to feel haunted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project in which an attempt...</span></span></span>

The critical finding emerged when researchers compared reports across conditions. People exposed to infrasound or unusual electromagnetic fields did not consistently report more experiences than those who were not exposed. Statistical analyses failed to show the clear relationship that would be expected if those environmental factors were the primary cause of the experiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project in which an attempt...</span></span></span>

In other words, participants often experienced something unusual regardless of whether the suspected “haunting” stimuli were actually present. The room successfully generated reports of eerie experiences, but the pattern of those reports did not strongly support the idea that infrasound or EMFs alone were responsible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project in which an attempt...</span></span></span>

This result contrasted with earlier enthusiasm surrounding cases such as Vic Tandy’s famous “ghost in the machine” account, in which infrasound had been proposed as a direct cause of haunting-like sensations. The Haunt project did not show that infrasound has no effects, but it suggested that the relationship is far less straightforward than some interpretations implied.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an example, we...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why Expectation Complicates Acoustic Explanations

One of the most influential conclusions from the Haunt project was that expectation can generate reports that resemble those attributed to environmental anomalies. Participants entered a setting framed as unusual and potentially eerie. In such circumstances, ordinary bodily sensations may acquire special significance.

A brief moment of imbalance may become evidence of a presence. A normal temperature fluctuation may feel paranormal. A fleeting emotional shift may seem externally caused. The study suggested that people actively interpret ambiguous experiences rather than passively recording them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2Haque]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project in which an attempt...</span></span></span>

This interpretation fits a broader body of psychological research on [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}). When people expect a location to be haunted, they become more attentive to unusual sensations and more likely to connect unrelated events into a meaningful pattern. The resulting experience can feel entirely genuine even if no paranormal cause exists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://richardwiseman.wordpress.com/research/psychology-of-ghosts-and-hauntings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.wordpress.com">[Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman Ghosts and Hauntings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanGhosts and Hauntings - Richard Wiseman - WordPress.comSome researchers have suggested that infrasound (very low frequency...</span></span></span>

The Haunt project therefore raised a methodological problem for infrasound theories. If expectation alone can produce reports similar to those attributed to low-frequency sound, then researchers must separate the effects of acoustics from the effects of suggestion.
