## Introduction

The Artificial Haunted Room Experiment, usually known as the **Haunt Project**, was one of the most ambitious attempts to test whether a room could be made to feel haunted through controlled environmental manipulation. Rather than investigating an allegedly haunted location, researchers built a specially designed chamber and exposed volunteers to conditions that some paranormal researchers had proposed as causes of ghostly sensations, particularly low-frequency sound (infrasound) and unusual electromagnetic fields (EMFs). The central question was simple: if these factors genuinely trigger feelings of presence, dread, or paranormal encounters, could scientists create such experiences on demand? The answer turned out to be more complicated. Participants frequently reported unusual sensations, but those reports did not reliably match the presence of the experimental stimuli. Instead, the findings pointed strongly toward the role of [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}), suggestion, and individual differences in how people interpret ambiguous sensations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

## How the Room Was Set Up

The project emerged from earlier claims that certain environmental conditions might contribute to haunting reports. Some researchers had argued that fluctuating electromagnetic fields could affect perception, while others suggested that infrasound—sound below the normal range of human hearing—might create feelings of unease, anxiety, or even apparent [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}). Inspired by these ideas, psychologist Christopher French and colleagues designed a controlled experimental space intended to function as an artificial haunted room.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2haque.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

The chamber was built so that researchers could manipulate environmental conditions while keeping participants unaware of exactly what was happening. Seventy-nine volunteers spent approximately fifty minutes in the room. During different sessions they were exposed to:

* Complex electromagnetic fields alone.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.theguardian.com/education/2003/jul/24/research.highereducation1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[theguardian.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scientist to create &#x27;haunted house&#x27; &#124; Research24 Jul 2003 — Wiseman has a few tricks up his sleeve - including eerie lighting, odd electr...</span></span></span>
* Both stimuli together.
* Neither stimulus, serving as a control condition.

</div>

Participants moved around the chamber and were encouraged to note any unusual feelings or experiences. Researchers recorded reports ranging from dizziness and tingling to sensations of presence, emotional shifts, and other experiences often associated with haunted locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

What made the experiment important was its attempt to move beyond anecdotal ghost reports. Instead of asking why a particular old building felt haunted, the researchers tested specific environmental explanations under controlled conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

## Infrasound and Electromagnetic Tests

The environmental factors chosen for the experiment were not arbitrary. They reflected two influential theories in haunting research.

### Infrasound

Interest in infrasound was partly inspired by engineer Vic Tandy's proposal that low-frequency vibrations might contribute to reports of apparitions and feelings of unease in certain buildings. Because infrasound can sometimes be produced by machinery, ventilation systems, traffic, or structural vibrations, it offered a plausible natural explanation for some allegedly haunted environments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[richardwiseman.com+2richardwiseman.substack.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — When investigating a haunted building it is good practice to attempt to exclude as many possib...</span></span></span>

The Haunt Project therefore incorporated carefully controlled infrasonic exposure. If the theory were correct, participants should have reported more unusual experiences when the low-frequency sound was active.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

### Electromagnetic Fields

The second major influence came from work associated with Michael Persinger, who suggested that complex electromagnetic stimulation might sometimes contribute to sensed-presence experiences and other unusual perceptions. Such ideas attracted attention because they appeared to offer a neurological explanation for ghost encounters without invoking supernatural causes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience_of_religion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Neuroscience of religion</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Neuroscience of religion</span></span></span>

The Haunt Project exposed participants to complex EMF patterns designed to test whether such fields could reliably produce anomalous sensations. Again, if the theory was correct, reports of strange experiences should have increased under those conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

### What the Data Showed

Participants did report a surprising number of unusual experiences. However, the crucial result was that these reports were not significantly linked to whether infrasound or electromagnetic stimulation was actually present. People described sensations commonly associated with [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) across all conditions, including control sessions. The predicted environmental effects failed to appear in a consistent, statistically convincing way.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

The experiment therefore did not provide strong support for either infrasound or complex EMFs as direct causes of haunting experiences under the tested conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

## Why Expectation Mattered More

The most influential lesson from the Haunt Project was not that participants experienced nothing unusual. In fact, many did. The important finding was that these experiences seemed to depend more on psychological factors than on the environmental manipulations themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

Researchers found that individuals who were more prone to suggestion, absorption, or unusual perceptual experiences tended to report more anomalous sensations. Simply being placed in an unusual environment and encouraged to monitor one's internal state appeared sufficient to generate experiences that people often associate with hauntings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

This result fits a broader pattern seen in haunting research. When people enter a location already framed as strange, mysterious, or potentially haunted, they become more attentive to ambiguous sensations. Ordinary bodily feelings, subtle sounds, shifts in balance, or fleeting perceptions may acquire greater significance. The expectation that something unusual might happen can shape how those sensations are interpreted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.howstuffworks.com/science-vs-myth/afterlife/ghost2.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.howstuffworks.com">[HowStuffWorks+2The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.howstuffworks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How Stuff Works Explanations for Paranormal Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">He has studied locations considered to be haunted...Read more...</span></span></span>

## What the Experiment Changed in Haunting Research

The Haunt Project became influential because it tested a popular environmental explanation for hauntings while simultaneously highlighting the power of expectation. Earlier discussions often framed the issue as a choice between environmental causes and psychological causes. The experiment suggested that the relationship is more subtle. Environmental features may still contribute to unusual experiences, but interpretation, context, and suggestibility appear to play a crucial role in determining whether those experiences are understood as paranormal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2Ars Technica]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>

For the study of sensed presence in haunted places, this remains one of the project's most significant contributions. Participants could experience feelings commonly described in ghost reports even when the supposedly haunting environmental [triggers]({{ 'triggers/' | relative_url }}) were absent. The room itself did not reliably manufacture ghosts. Instead, it demonstrated how readily the human mind can generate meaningful, presence-like experiences when placed in an ambiguous setting and encouraged to look for them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18635163/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">an attempt to build a &quot;haunted&quot; room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 86 — The &quot;Haunt&quot; project: an attempt to buil...</span></span></span>
