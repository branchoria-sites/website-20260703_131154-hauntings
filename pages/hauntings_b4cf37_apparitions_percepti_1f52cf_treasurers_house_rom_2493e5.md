## Introduction

The Roman soldiers reportedly seen at Treasurer’s House in York are among the most famous apparition accounts in Britain because of one unusual detail: the figures were visible only from the knees up. The sighting, first reported by apprentice heating engineer Harry Martindale in 1953, has endured not simply because it involved “ghosts”, but because it combines several features that make apparition stories memorable—an apparently sincere witness, a vivid historical setting, highly specific visual details, and an observation that later seemed to fit archaeological discoveries beneath the building. ## What the 1953 account describes

According to the account preserved by Treasurer’s House and repeated by Martindale throughout his life, he was working alone in the cellar during boiler installation work when he heard repeated trumpet-like sounds. He initially assumed the noise came from an ordinary source, but the sound seemed to approach from beyond the cellar wall. Shortly afterwards, he reported seeing a Roman soldier emerge through the wall, followed by a horse and a column of soldiers marching in formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

Martindale described the soldiers as older, somewhat weary-looking men rather than idealised Roman legionaries. He recalled green tunics, plumed helmets, round shields and sandals strapped to the knees. The procession appeared to pass along a route beneath the building before disappearing again into the opposite wall. What most startled him was that the figures appeared only from the knees upwards. Their lower legs and feet were hidden from view.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer%27s_House%2C_York" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span></span></span>

The experience reportedly frightened him so much that he left the cellar in shock. A detail often repeated in later retellings is that a house warden supposedly recognised his reaction immediately and remarked that he looked as though he had seen the Roman soldiers. Whether every element of that exchange can be independently verified, it became an important part of the legend because it suggested that Martindale's experience was not entirely unique.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

The story gained wider attention decades later. Supporters of the account point out that Martindale consistently repeated the same description over many years and declined payment for telling the story, which has often been cited as evidence of his sincerity even by people who remain unconvinced by paranormal explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer%27s_House%2C_York" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span></span></span>

## Why partial visibility matters

The most discussed aspect of the case is not the Roman soldiers themselves but the fact that they were visible only from the knees up. In apparition reports, partial figures are surprisingly common. Witnesses often report seeing a torso, a head and shoulders, or a figure partly obscured by walls, furniture or darkness rather than a fully visible person.

In the Treasurer’s House case, the missing lower legs created a puzzle that later became central to the story. Excavations beneath the property showed that the site sits above Roman remains, including a major Roman road associated with the fortress of [Eboracum]({{ 'eboracum/' | relative_url }}), the Roman settlement that became York. The later suggestion was that the soldiers appeared complete within their own apparent environment but were marching on a road surface lower than the modern cellar floor. From Martindale’s viewpoint, only their upper bodies would therefore have been visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/history-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustHistory of Treasurer&#x27;s House &#124; YorkshireTreasurer&#x27;s House is one of the most prestigious addresses in York. This explanation is frequently cited by believers because it appears to connect an unusual visual detail with an archaeological fact discovered or better understood later. Critics, however, note that the connection does not verify the sighting itself. Instead, it demonstrates how a striking anomaly can become more persuasive when later information seems to provide a fitting explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

From a perception perspective, the partial nature of the figures may also have contributed to the experience's lasting power. Human [observers]({{ 'observers/' | relative_url }}) tend to remember and discuss incomplete images that demand interpretation. A fully visible group of soldiers might have been dismissed as an obvious mistake or hallucination; a procession cut off at the knees became a distinctive detail that separated this story from thousands of more ordinary ghost reports.

## How the historic setting strengthens the story

Treasurer’s House occupies a location unusually rich in historical associations. The building stands close to York Minster and above remains connected with Roman York. Archaeological investigations have revealed parts of the Roman fortress and a significant Roman road beneath the area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/history-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustHistory of Treasurer&#x27;s House &#124; YorkshireTreasurer&#x27;s House is one of the most prestigious addresses in York. Built upon the...</span></span></span>

That setting matters because apparition stories often become more convincing when the reported figures seem appropriate to the location. A witness seeing Roman soldiers in a modern office block would create one kind of mystery. Seeing Roman soldiers beneath a building known to stand over Roman remains creates a narrative that feels historically coherent, regardless of whether one accepts a paranormal explanation.

The story was also reinforced by reports that other individuals connected with the house claimed unusual experiences. Treasurer’s House archives record accounts from other witnesses, including a former housekeeper who later said she had seen Roman soldiers and horses in the cellar. These reports do not independently prove Martindale’s experience, but they helped transform a single witness account into part of the property's folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

York itself has long cultivated a reputation as one of Britain’s most haunted cities. Within that cultural environment, a Roman ghost story fits naturally into local identity. ## Why the case remains memorable

Many haunting stories fade because they lack a distinctive feature. The Treasurer’s House sighting endures because it combines several memorable elements in a single account:

* A named witness whose story remained broadly consistent over decades.
* A vivid historical identity for the apparition: Roman soldiers.
* An unusual visual detail—the figures appearing only from the knees up.
* A location known to contain Roman archaeological remains.
* Later discoveries that appeared to echo aspects of the witness’s description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>
