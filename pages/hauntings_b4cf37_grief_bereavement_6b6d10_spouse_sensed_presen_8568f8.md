## Introduction

After the death of a spouse, many widowed people report a striking experience: they feel that their husband or wife is still nearby. There may be no visible figure, no voice, and no obvious sensory event. Instead, there is a powerful sense that the person is present in the room, walking beside them, sitting in a familiar chair, or sharing a moment of everyday life. Within discussions of [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}), these experiences are often interpreted as ghostly visitations. Bereavement research, however, has repeatedly found that such felt-presence experiences are common, especially after the loss of a long-term partner, and do not by themselves demonstrate a literal haunting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-020-09666-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer Link]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Link Sensed presence without sensory qualities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This was...Read more...</span></span></span>

What makes these experiences noteworthy is not merely their frequency but their realism. Many widowed people describe them as vivid, emotionally significant, and deeply convincing. ## What Widowed People Commonly Report

The best-known study of widowhood experiences was conducted by Welsh physician W. Dewi Rees. Interviewing 227 widows and 66 widowers, he found that almost half had experienced some form of illusion or hallucination involving their deceased spouse. The most common report was not seeing or hearing the spouse but simply feeling that they were present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1799198/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCThe Hallucinations of Widowhood</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The proportion of men and women who had these experiences was similar.Read more...</span></span></span>

Descriptions often include:

* Feeling a spouse standing nearby while doing routine household tasks.
* Sensing someone enter a room even though nobody is there.
* Feeling a partner's presence in bed during the night.
* Experiencing a momentary certainty that the spouse is accompanying them on a walk, journey, or family occasion.
* Turning to speak to the spouse before remembering they have died.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-020-09666-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer Link]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Link Sensed presence without sensory qualities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This was...Read more...</span></span></span>

A key feature is that the experience usually feels relational rather than sensory. People often say they do not actually see or hear their spouse. Instead, they recognise a familiar personal presence. This distinction has led researchers to treat felt-presence experiences as a category of their own rather than as ordinary visual or auditory hallucinations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://philpapers.org/rec/RATSPW" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: philpapers.org">[PhilPapers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">philpapers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matthew Ratcliffe, Sensed presence without sensory qualitiesby M Ratcliffe · 2020 · Cited by 61 — This paper addresses the natu...</span></span></span>

Later studies have produced similar findings. A survey of older Danish adults conducted six to ten months after spousal bereavement found that 42% reported sensory or quasi-sensory experiences of their deceased spouse. Among those who had such experiences, a sense of presence was the most common form.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00302228211016224" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals+2Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sage Journals A Survey of Bereaved Older Adults</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsA Survey of Bereaved Older AdultsMay 21, 2021 — Sensory and quasi-sensory experiences of the deceased (SED), also called ber...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 21, 2021</span></span></span>

## Why Felt Presence Can Outlast Death

One reason these experiences attract attention is that they can continue long after the initial shock of bereavement. Rees found that reports were most common in the early years of widowhood but could persist for many years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1799198/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCThe Hallucinations of Widowhood</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The proportion of men and women who had these experiences was similar.Read more...</span></span></span>

Researchers have proposed several overlapping explanations.

### The Brain Still Expects the Spouse

Long marriages create deeply ingrained expectations. A spouse is often woven into daily routines, habits, conversations, and future plans. The surviving partner may continue to anticipate the other's presence automatically.

A person who has spent decades sharing meals, sleeping beside the same partner, or discussing daily events may continue to predict that person's involvement. The resulting mismatch between [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}) and reality can create moments in which the deceased feels present despite being absent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-025-10064-9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer Link]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Springer LinkSensing presence: deathbots and bereavement hallucinationby P Stokes · 2025 · Cited by 6 — This paper considers recent discu...</span></span></span>

### Relationships Continue After Death

Modern grief research increasingly rejects the older idea that healthy mourning requires complete emotional detachment from the deceased. Instead, many bereaved people maintain an ongoing inner relationship with the person who died.

A sensed presence may therefore reflect the continuation of an important bond rather than a failure to accept the death. The deceased spouse remains psychologically significant, and the relationship can still shape thoughts, decisions, and emotional life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707065/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2OUP Academic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sensory and Quasi-Sensory Experiences of the Deceased in...by KS Kamp · 2020 · Cited by 105 — The review concludes that the vast majo...</span></span></span>

### Presence Is More Than a Sensory Event

Philosophical and phenomenological studies have argued that human presence is not experienced only through sight or sound. People often recognise others through patterns of expectation, familiarity, and social interaction.

From this perspective, feeling a dead spouse nearby may arise because the person's characteristic way of being remains embedded in the survivor's everyday world. The experience can feel real precisely because it draws upon years of shared life rather than a fleeting sensory impression.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://philpapers.org/rec/RATSPW" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: philpapers.org">[PhilPapers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">philpapers.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Matthew Ratcliffe, Sensed presence without sensory qualitiesby M Ratcliffe · 2020 · Cited by 61 — This paper addresses the natu...</span></span></span>

## Why People Sometimes Interpret It as a Haunting

For many widowed individuals, the experience feels too vivid to dismiss as imagination. Some describe an unmistakable certainty that their spouse has returned. Others interpret the event as a sign, visitation, or communication from beyond death.

The haunting interpretation is especially likely when the sensed presence occurs during emotionally significant moments, such as anniversaries, family milestones, periods of crisis, or visits to meaningful places. The experience may seem purposeful rather than accidental.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7707065/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sensory and Quasi-Sensory Experiences of the Deceased in...by KS Kamp · 2020 · Cited by 105 — The review concludes that the vast majo...</span></span></span>

However, research has not established that felt-presence experiences provide objective evidence of survival after death. The same reports can be interpreted in multiple ways: as paranormal encounters, as manifestations of grief, as [continuing bonds]({{ 'continuing-bonds/' | relative_url }}), or as ordinary features of human cognition under conditions of loss. ## What the Evidence Suggests

The strongest evidence points to a clear conclusion: feeling the presence of a deceased spouse is one of the most common and characteristic experiences of widowhood.
