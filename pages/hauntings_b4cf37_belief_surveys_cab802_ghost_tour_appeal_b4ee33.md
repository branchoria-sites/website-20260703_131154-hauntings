## Introduction

Ghost tours succeed because they do more than tell scary stories. They turn local legends, old buildings and forgotten corners of a town into memorable experiences that connect visitors with place. In communities where ghost stories are already part of [local folklore]({{ 'local-folklore/' | relative_url }}), a haunted reputation can help historic streets, castles, inns, prisons and neighbourhoods stand out in a crowded tourism market. Importantly, [ghost tourism]({{ 'ghost-tourism/' | relative_url }}) is not driven only by people who believe in ghosts. Research consistently shows that many participants join for history, entertainment, storytelling and curiosity rather than a firm belief in the supernatural.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/d9909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsLegend-Tripping in Spooky Spaces: Ghost Tourism and...by J Holloway · 2010 · Cited by 235 — This paper investigates the inc...</span></span></span>

## How Ghost Walks Frame Local History

[Ghost walks]({{ 'ghost-walks/' | relative_url }}) are often presented as paranormal entertainment, but they also function as a form of public history. Guides use stories of [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}), unexplained events and local legends to introduce visitors to historical people, buildings and events that might otherwise receive little attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ncph.org/phc/2016-annual-meeting-topic-proposals/2016-proposal-ghosts-dark-tourism-and-public-history/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ncph.org">[National Council on Public History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ncph.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2016 proposal ghosts dark tourism and public history</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Council on Public History2016 proposal: &quot;Ghosts, Dark Tourism, and Public History&quot;This panel will collect researchers and practi...</span></span></span>

A ghost story gives structure to a walking route. Instead of simply moving from one landmark to another, visitors follow a narrative. A supposedly haunted alley may lead into discussion of crime, disease, poverty or social change. A haunted theatre may become a gateway to local cultural history. In this way, supernatural narratives help audiences remember places and historical details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gold.ac.uk/news/spectral-cities/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gold.ac.uk">[Goldsmiths, University of London]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gold.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">spectral cities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">smiths, University of LondonStudy reveals secrets behind ghost tour boom31 Oct 2019 — Their research argues that it&#x27;s the mysterious...</span></span></span>

Researchers studying ghost tourism note that these experiences often combine history, folklore, performance and [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}). Storytelling, darkness, architecture and anticipation work together to create what scholars describe as a sense of supernatural possibility, regardless of whether participants literally believe the stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/d9909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsLegend-Tripping in Spooky Spaces: Ghost Tourism and...by J Holloway · 2010 · Cited by 235 — This paper investigates the inc...</span></span></span>

The approach can be particularly effective in historic urban environments. Cities such as London, Amsterdam and many other European destinations use ghost walks to reinterpret familiar streets through tales of executions, disasters, mysterious deaths and local legends, encouraging visitors to notice details that are often overlooked during conventional sightseeing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.london-ghost-walk.co.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: london-ghost-walk.co.uk">[London Ghost Walk+2London Walks]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">london-ghost-walk.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">London&#x27;s longest established ghost walk guide (Walking The Shadows Since 1982).Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why Non-Believers Still Join In

One of the most interesting features of ghost tourism is that participation does not depend on belief. Studies of paranormal tourism repeatedly find a mix of believers, sceptics and people who simply enjoy the experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372854055_An_exploratory_study_of_the_future_paranormal_tourist_experience_on_ghost_tours" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2Helvia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) An exploratory study of the future paranormal tourist...The purpose of this study is to explore the paranormal tourist...</span></span></span>

Several factors help explain this broad appeal:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Storytelling value:** Well-told local legends can be entertaining regardless of whether they are accepted as true.
* **Historical curiosity:** Visitors often see ghost tours as an alternative form of local history.
* **Atmosphere:** Night-time walks, historic architecture and dramatic settings create a distinctive experience.
* **Social participation:** Ghost tours are commonly enjoyed as group activities among friends, families and visitors.
* **Playful uncertainty:** Participants can enjoy the possibility of the supernatural without committing to belief.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/d9909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals+2Goldsmiths, University of London]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsLegend-Tripping in Spooky Spaces: Ghost Tourism and...by J Holloway · 2010 · Cited by 235 — This paper investigates the inc...</span></span></span>

Researchers have described this attitude as a form of engagement where people willingly suspend certainty. Visitors may neither fully accept nor reject the stories. Instead, they enjoy exploring the question of whether something unusual might have happened.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1068/d9909" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsLegend-Tripping in Spooky Spaces: Ghost Tourism and...by J Holloway · 2010 · Cited by 235 — This paper investigates the inc...</span></span></span>

This flexibility makes ghost tourism unusually inclusive. A believer may attend hoping for a paranormal experience, while a sceptic may join for architecture, folklore or local history. Both can enjoy the same tour for different reasons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372854055_An_exploratory_study_of_the_future_paranormal_tourist_experience_on_ghost_tours" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) An exploratory study of the future paranormal tourist...The purpose of this study is to explore the paranormal tourist...</span></span></span>

## Haunted Sites as Heritage Attractions

Historic buildings often gain visibility when associated with a haunting legend. A ghost story can provide an accessible narrative that encourages visitors to explore a site they might otherwise ignore. Researchers have noted that ghost tourism operates both as a cultural practice and as an economic activity linked to heritage promotion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/238522/4/Mostly%20haunted%20%20a%20socio-spatial%20analysis%20of%20ghost%20tourism%20in%20England%20and%20Wales.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eprints.whiterose.ac.uk">[White Rose Research Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eprints.whiterose.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mostly haunted a socio spatial analysis of ghost tourism in England and Wales</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">White Rose Research OnlineMostly haunted: a socio-spatial analysis of ghost tourism in...by T Clark · 2026 — As highlighted by Hanks (20...</span></span></span>

Museums, preservation groups and heritage organisations have sometimes debated whether ghost stories should be included in interpretation programmes. Critics worry that hauntings can overshadow historical accuracy, while supporters argue that folklore itself forms part of a site's cultural heritage. Many institutions have ultimately adopted a middle ground, using ghost narratives as entry points into documented history while clearly distinguishing legend from established fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aaslh.org/ghost-tours-marketing-hype-or-spiritual-quest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaslh.org">[AASLH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaslh.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ghost tours marketing hype or spiritual quest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ghost Tours: Marketing Hype or Spiritual Quest?Apr 10, 2017 — Many museums and historic preservation groups have been unsure how to...</span></span></span>

The appeal can be especially strong at locations connected with dramatic historical events. Former prisons, battlefields, theatres, castles and historic homes frequently attract visitors through a combination of documented history and local supernatural traditions. The haunting narrative gives people an emotional connection to the site and can encourage repeat visits, themed events and seasonal programmes. ## Local Legends, Identity and Placemaking

Ghost stories often survive because they become attached to particular places. A town's famous apparition, haunted inn or mysterious lane can become part of local identity in the same way as a landmark, festival or traditional custom.
