## Introduction

A recurring lesson in the history of haunting investigations is that knowledge of magic tricks has often been treated as a research tool rather than a sceptic’s hobby. The most careful psychical researchers argued that a haunting file is incomplete unless it tests whether apparently paranormal events could have been produced by deception, misdirection, hidden mechanisms, witness suggestion, or deliberate fraud. This approach became especially influential through the work of [Harry Price]({{ 'harry-price/' | relative_url }}), a psychical investigator who was also an accomplished amateur conjurer. His career illustrates why many researchers believed that understanding stage magic was essential to evaluating extraordinary claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Price" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Harry Price</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harry Price</span></span></span>

Within the archives of [psychical research]({{ 'research-files/' | relative_url }}) societies, the goal was not simply to dismiss reports. Instead, investigators attempted to separate ordinary trickery from claims that remained difficult to explain. ## Why Trick Methods Matter in Haunting Files

Many classic haunting reports involve effects that closely resemble the techniques used by professional magicians: [moving objects]({{ 'moving-objects/' | relative_url }}), mysterious sounds, hidden messages, disappearing items, manipulated photographs, and performances conducted under poor lighting conditions. Investigators who lacked familiarity with such methods risked mistaking a theatrical effect for evidence of the supernatural.

This concern became particularly important during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, when séances and spiritualist demonstrations were common. Researchers discovered numerous cases in which mediums used concealed equipment, sleight of hand, prepared props, substituted photographs, or physical manoeuvres hidden from [observers]({{ 'observers/' | relative_url }}). Harry Price's investigations of figures such as spirit photographer William Hope and several physical mediums reflected the belief that paranormal research required practical knowledge of deception techniques as much as it required witness testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journal.sciencemuseum.ac.uk/article/photographic-plates-and-spirit-fakes-remembering-harry-prices-investigation-of-william-hopes-spirit-photography-at-its-centenary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencemuseum.ac.uk">[Science Museum Group Journal+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencemuseum.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">photographic plates and spirit fakes remembering harry prices investigation of william hopes spirit photography at its centenary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Science Museum Group Journalremembering Harry Price&#x27;s investigation of William Hope&#x27;s ...26 Nov 2022 — Harry Price was relatively unknown...</span></span></span>

The logic was straightforward. If an investigator could not recognise known methods of fraud, then any claim of a haunting remained vulnerable to ordinary explanations. Consequently, some psychical researchers treated conjuring manuals almost as reference works. Before proposing an unusual explanation, they wanted to know whether a magician could reproduce the reported effect by conventional means.

A useful comparison emerged repeatedly in psychical research literature:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Witnesses describe what they experienced.**
* **Scientists evaluate physical evidence.**
* **Conjuring experts examine how appearances can be manufactured.**

</div>

Each perspective addresses a different weakness in the evidence chain.

## How Suspicion Can Coexist with Unresolved Claims

The presence of fraud in some cases did not automatically convince psychical researchers that every report was fraudulent. Instead, many haunting files developed a layered approach to evidence.

A report might contain both exposed trickery and incidents that investigators believed remained unexplained. This distinction appears throughout psychical research archives. Cases were sometimes catalogued with references to cheating, [hoaxing]({{ 'hoaxing/' | relative_url }}), or practical jokes while still preserving testimony about other events that investigators felt deserved examination. The file therefore became a record of competing interpretations rather than a simple verdict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Price" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Harry Price</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harry Price</span></span></span>

The history of Borley Rectory demonstrates this tension. Harry Price's investigation generated extensive documentation and public attention, yet later researchers from the Society for Psychical Research argued that many reported phenomena could be explained by natural causes, witness errors, practical jokes, or deliberate fabrication. Critics accused both witnesses and Price himself of contributing to misleading evidence. Nevertheless, the case remained important precisely because the arguments were preserved in detail. Supporters and critics could inspect the same record and reach different conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borley_Rectory" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2library.umbc.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Borley Rectory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Borley Rectory</span></span></span>

For investigators, this produced an important methodological lesson: uncovering one deception does not necessarily resolve every claim associated with a haunting. At the same time, unresolved claims do not erase proven instances of fraud. Serious files therefore attempt to document both.

### The Value of Controlled Testing

Conjuring knowledge encouraged researchers to move beyond witness impressions and create tests.

Harry Price's National Laboratory of Psychical Research employed instruments and controlled procedures intended to detect movement, interference, or other ordinary causes behind reported phenomena. One well-known example was an electrically monitored chair designed to reveal whether a medium had moved while supposedly producing paranormal effects. The purpose was not merely to catch cheats but to create records that could withstand later scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/harry-price/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia+2Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Harry Price</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaHarry Price - Psi Encyclopedia10 Jun 2017 — Harry Price · Price founded a National Laboratory of Psychical Research and e...</span></span></span>

This approach reflected a broader principle: the more spectacular the claim, the more important it became to understand how an illusionist might imitate it.

## What Specialist Libraries Add to Investigations

One of the most revealing features of psychical research history is the importance attached to specialist collections of magical literature.

The Harry Price Library of Magical Literature, now held by Senate House Library in London, contains nearly 13,000 books, pamphlets, and periodicals covering conjuring, illusion, scientific recreations, and related subjects. The collection was assembled by Price partly because he believed that investigators needed access to the history of deception as well as the history of psychic claims. Conjuring is identified as one of the collection's particular strengths.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.london.ac.uk/about/services/senate-house-library/collections/printed-special-collections/harry-price-library-magical-literature" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: london.ac.uk">[University of London]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">london.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">University of LondonHarry Price Library of Magical LiteratureThe collection comprises nearly 13,000 books, pamphlets and periodical title...</span></span></span>

For haunting investigations, such libraries serve several functions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Historical comparison:** Researchers can determine whether a supposedly unique phenomenon resembles a known trick described decades earlier.
* **Fraud detection:** Manuals reveal practical methods for producing effects that witnesses may perceive as paranormal.
* **Context building:** Investigators learn how public expectations and popular entertainment influence reports.
* **Case review:** Older files can be re-examined using techniques that earlier investigators may not have recognised.

</div>

The existence of these collections highlights a distinctive feature of psychical research. Libraries devoted to [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) often preserve books explaining how to fake hauntings. To many researchers, this was not a contradiction but a necessity.

## Why Conjuring Knowledge Remains Relevant

The historical record suggests that the most durable haunting investigations are rarely those that begin by assuming either ghosts or fraud. Instead, they begin by asking what ordinary mechanisms could plausibly account for the reported events.

Knowledge of conjuring became valuable because it supplied investigators with a catalogue of such mechanisms. It provided practical insight into misdirection, hidden actions, manipulated evidence, staged photographs, and witness perception. Researchers such as Harry Price considered this expertise essential because a haunting file that never tests for deception cannot reliably distinguish an extraordinary claim from a skilfully constructed illusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Price" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2American Hauntings]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Harry Price</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harry Price</span></span></span>

The result is one of the enduring methodological lessons of psychical research societies: a serious investigation does not become less rigorous by studying magic. In the history of hauntings, understanding how effects can be manufactured has often been viewed as a prerequisite for deciding whether any part of a case remains genuinely unexplained.
