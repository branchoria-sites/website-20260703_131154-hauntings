## Introduction

The story of La Corriveau shows how a documented criminal case can become one of the most enduring ghost legends in North America. Marie-Josephte Corriveau was a real woman who was executed in 1763 for the murder of her second husband in the turbulent period following the British conquest of New France. Yet the event did not remain a court case. The unusual punishment imposed after her death—public display in an iron cage—transformed a legal sentence into a lasting object of fear, memory and folklore. Over time, historical facts mixed with rumour, moral warning and supernatural imagination until “La Corriveau” became a wandering spectre, a witch-like figure and a symbol of haunting injustice in Quebec culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.plainsofabraham.ca/history-and-heritage/history-of-the-park/1760-1908-civilian-and-military-occupation/marie-josephte-corriveau" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: plainsofabraham.ca">[Plains of Abraham+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">plainsofabraham.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Her name has endured through the ages, remaining embedded in collective memory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Plains of AbrahamMarie-Josephte Corriveau - From history to legendOn April 18, 1763, Marie-Josephte Corriveau was hanged on the Plains of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 1763</span></span></span>

## The Historical Case Behind the Legend

Marie-Josephte Corriveau was born in 1733 in what is now Quebec. After the death of her first husband, she married Louis Dodier in 1761. In 1763 Dodier was found dead, and a British military tribunal investigated the case during the transitional period following the conquest of New France. After a complicated legal process that initially involved her father, Corriveau was convicted of killing her husband and sentenced to death. She was hanged on 18 April 1763 on the Plains of Abraham near Quebec City.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.plainsofabraham.ca/history-and-heritage/history-of-the-park/1760-1908-civilian-and-military-occupation/marie-josephte-corriveau" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: plainsofabraham.ca">[Plains of Abraham+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">plainsofabraham.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Her name has endured through the ages, remaining embedded in collective memory</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Plains of AbrahamMarie-Josephte Corriveau - From history to legendOn April 18, 1763, Marie-Josephte Corriveau was hanged on the Plains of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 18, 1763</span></span></span>

The execution itself was not what made the case extraordinary. What distinguished it was the additional punishment. After the hanging, her body was placed in an iron gibbet—a cage-like structure used for public display—and suspended at Pointe-Lévy, where it remained visible for several weeks. ## How the Cage Became an Object of Memory

The iron cage became the most important element in the legend. Executions were public events in the eighteenth century, but the prolonged display of a body created a continuing presence. Travellers, farmers and local residents repeatedly encountered the gibbet, turning it into a landmark associated with fear and death.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ethnologiequebec.org/2017/01/la-corriveau-de-lhistoire-a-la-legende/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ethnologiequebec.org">[ethnologiequebec.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ethnologiequebec.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ethnologiequebec.org.</span></span></span>

Folklore often requires a tangible anchor, and La Corriveau's cage provided exactly that. The structure was not merely a symbol of punishment; it was a physical reminder that remained visible long after the execution. Stories accumulated around it. People reported strange sights, eerie sounds and unsettling feelings near the display. The cage itself became more memorable than the legal proceedings that had produced it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/la-corriveaus-cage-quebec-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura The Hanging Cage That Held an Infamous Québec</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraThe Hanging Cage That Held an Infamous Québec...November 2, 2017 — 2 Nov 2017 — To many, the legend of “La Corriveau” is a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2017</span></span></span>

The object continued to exert influence even after the body was removed. The gibbet entered local memory as a relic connected to an unresolved past. Its rediscovery centuries later attracted renewed public interest because it linked modern audiences to the original event behind the legend.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/la-corriveaus-cage-quebec-canada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura The Hanging Cage That Held an Infamous Québec</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas ObscuraThe Hanging Cage That Held an Infamous Québec...November 2, 2017 — 2 Nov 2017 — To many, the legend of “La Corriveau” is a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2017</span></span></span>

## Why Punishment Stories Become Hauntings

La Corriveau illustrates a broader pattern found in many ghost traditions: stories of punishment often evolve into hauntings when communities continue to revisit the emotional meaning of the event.

Several factors helped transform the case into folklore:

* **Public spectacle:** The punishment was designed to be seen. Visibility encouraged storytelling and repetition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ethnologiequebec.org/2017/01/la-corriveau-de-lhistoire-a-la-legende/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ethnologiequebec.org">[ethnologiequebec.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ethnologiequebec.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ethnologiequebec.org.</span></span></span>
