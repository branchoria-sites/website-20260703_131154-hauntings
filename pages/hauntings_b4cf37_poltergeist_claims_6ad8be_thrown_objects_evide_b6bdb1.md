## Introduction

Thrown objects are among the most frequently cited forms of alleged poltergeist activity because they appear to create physical evidence rather than relying solely on personal experience. Witnesses may report stones appearing indoors, toys crossing a room, cutlery flying from tables, or household items striking walls and people. Yet the central evidential problem is that an impact is not the same thing as a witnessed launch. A smashed ornament proves that something hit the floor; it does not, by itself, show how it got there. For investigators, the strongest cases are not those with the most dramatic damage but those in which the object's starting position, movement, trajectory, landing point and surrounding conditions can be observed with minimal gaps. Across both [psychical research]({{ 'research-files/' | relative_url }}) and sceptical investigations, the launch moment has consistently been the point at which otherwise impressive stories either gain credibility or collapse into uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/psychological-aspects-poltergeist-cases/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia+2spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Psychological Aspects of Poltergeist Cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaPsychological Aspects of Poltergeist Cases - Psi Encyclopedia24 May 2019 — Poltergeists are characterized by a relatively...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2019</span></span></span>

## What Witnesses Usually Report

Reports of thrown objects tend to follow a recognisable pattern. Rather than a single spectacular event, witnesses often describe repeated disturbances involving small, easily moved items: stones, coins, toys, kitchen utensils, bottles or ornaments. The objects may appear to emerge from another room, fall from above, strike walls, or land near particular individuals. Historical poltergeist literature contains many accounts of unexplained stone-throwing and household items apparently travelling without a visible thrower.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.encyclopedia.com/philosophy-and-religion/other-religious-beliefs-and-general-terms/miscellaneous-religion/poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.com">[Encyclopedia.com+2EBSCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PoltergeistA term that signifies some type of force to which is attributed a set of spontaneous, puzzling, and troublesome occurrences. A notable feature of these reports is that [observers]({{ 'observers/' | relative_url }}) frequently see only part of the event. They may hear a crash and then notice an object in flight, or discover an object after impact. In many cases, witnesses report certainty that nobody could have thrown the item because no thrower was visible. However, that is different from directly observing the object's entire movement from its original position to its destination. Investigators repeatedly note that the most important evidential information is often missing precisely because attention is drawn to the disturbance only after it begins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/society-psychical-research/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia+2Cambridge University Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Society for Psychical Research (SPR</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Many were...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why the Launch Moment Matters Most

The key question in a thrown-object claim is not whether an object moved but how its movement began.

An evidential chain consists of several stages:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. The object is observed at rest.
2. The beginning of movement is observed.
3. The object's flight path can be tracked.
4. The impact location is documented.
5. The surrounding conditions rule out ordinary causes.

</div>

Most reports contain only the final two stages. A witness sees the object after it is already moving or notices the aftermath. This creates a substantial evidential gap. A stone found indoors, for example, may be genuinely puzzling, but unless its origin can be established, investigators cannot distinguish between an unknown human action, an environmental cause, deliberate deception, or something genuinely anomalous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/departments/archives-modern-and-medieval-manuscripts-and-university-archives-0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.cam.ac.uk">[Cambridge University Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.cam.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">bridge University LibrarySociety for Psychical Research &#124; Cambridge University LibraryInitially the SPR focussed on investigating, exp...</span></span></span>

For this reason, investigators generally regard continuous observation as far more valuable than dramatic outcomes. An object that appears to move a short distance under direct scrutiny may be more evidential than a large amount of damage discovered after the fact.

### Trajectories and Physical Behaviour

When witnesses describe thrown objects, certain details become important:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Did the object accelerate from rest or simply appear mid-flight?
* Did it follow a normal ballistic path?
* Did it appear to change direction?
* Did it emerge from a location where a concealed throw was possible?
* Was the movement recorded from beginning to end?

</div>

Claims become stronger when multiple observers independently report the same trajectory and when the object's motion appears inconsistent with ordinary throwing. However, such cases remain uncommon, and even then investigators must consider perception errors, viewing angles and incomplete observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/psychological-aspects-poltergeist-cases/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk">[Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Psi Encyclopedia Psychological Aspects of Poltergeist Cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Psi EncyclopediaPsychological Aspects of Poltergeist Cases - Psi Encyclopedia24 May 2019 — Poltergeists are characterized by a relatively...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2019</span></span></span>

## How Investigators Separate Impacts from Causes

A common mistake in public discussions of [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) is to treat [physical traces]({{ 'physical-traces/' | relative_url }}) as proof of paranormal action. Investigators generally take the opposite approach.

A broken window may establish that an object struck the glass. It does not establish what launched the object. Likewise:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A dent in a wall proves impact.
* A bruise proves contact.
* A shattered plate proves breakage.
* None of these alone identifies a cause.

</div>

Because of this distinction, serious investigations focus heavily on reconstruction. They attempt to determine where the object originated, who was present, whether access routes existed, and whether anyone could have acted unnoticed. The emphasis is not on the damage but on the chain of events that produced it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/departments/archives-modern-and-medieval-manuscripts-and-university-archives-0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.cam.ac.uk">[Cambridge University Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.cam.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">bridge University LibrarySociety for Psychical Research &#124; Cambridge University LibraryInitially the SPR focussed on investigating, exp...</span></span></span>

Sceptical investigators have sometimes used controlled methods to test claims. In one often-cited case discussed by investigator Joe Nickell, tracer powder placed on supposedly affected objects was later found on the hands of a child associated with the disturbances, indicating ordinary handling rather than unexplained movement. ## Cases That Changed Assessment Under Observation

One recurring pattern in poltergeist investigations is that phenomena often change once observation becomes more systematic.

Historical psychical researchers and modern investigators alike have noted that dramatic claims may diminish when continuous monitoring, controlled access or close supervision is introduced. This does not automatically prove fraud in every instance, but it highlights the importance of observation conditions. Cases that appear compelling in informal household settings frequently become less clear when investigators attempt to document every stage of an event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/departments/archives-modern-and-medieval-manuscripts-and-university-archives-0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lib.cam.ac.uk">[Cambridge University Library+2Psi Encyclopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lib.cam.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">bridge University LibrarySociety for Psychical Research &#124; Cambridge University LibraryInitially the SPR focussed on investigating, exp...</span></span></span>

At the same time, a small number of reports remain controversial because witnesses claimed to observe object movement under conditions they considered difficult to explain. Such reports are often cited in psychical research literature as unresolved rather than proven. The distinction is important: unexplained does not automatically mean paranormal. ## What Would Count as Strong Evidence?

Within the specific question of thrown objects, stronger evidence would involve multiple layers of documentation rather than a larger number of anecdotes.

The most persuasive scenario would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Independent witnesses observing the object's entire movement.
* Clear knowledge of the object's starting position.
* Continuous recording before, during and after the event.
* Controlled access to the area.
* Elimination of obvious environmental causes.
* Repeatability under monitored conditions.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340508724_CONCOMITANT_OBJECT_MOVEMENTS_AND_EMF-SPIKES_AT_A_PURPORTED_HAUNT" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[researchgate.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">340508724 CONCOMITANT OBJECT MOVEMENTS AND EMF SPIKES AT A PURPORTED HAUNT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">concomitant object movements and emf-spikes at a...8 Apr 2020 — We documented 10 instances of anomalous movements (&quot;PK events&quot;) of two o...</span></span></span>

## Why Thrown Objects Remain Controversial

Thrown-object reports persist because they sit between testimony and physical evidence. They are often memorable, sometimes involve multiple witnesses, and occasionally produce visible traces. Yet the decisive question—how the object began moving—is usually the least documented part of the event.

As a result, both believers and sceptics tend to focus on the same issue. Believers point to cases in which witnesses insist no thrower was present. Sceptics point to the absence of complete observation and the long history of mischief, mistaken assumptions and incomplete reconstruction in poltergeist investigations. The debate therefore centres less on whether objects were found displaced and more on whether any case has successfully documented the entire sequence from launch to landing without a plausible ordinary explanation. On that narrower evidential question, the strongest standard remains continuous, gap-free observation of the object's movement from beginning to end.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltergeist" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Cambridge University Library+3Wikipedia+3Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>
