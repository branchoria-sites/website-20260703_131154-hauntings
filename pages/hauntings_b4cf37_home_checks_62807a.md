## Introduction

Before calling a home haunted, check it as a home. Many haunting reports begin with real experiences: knocks, [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}), cold patches, [flickering lights]({{ 'ghost-lights/' | relative_url }}), bad smells, a sensed presence, or seeing something at the edge of vision. The practical question is not whether the witness is “making it up”, but whether a building fault, health hazard, sleep event or suggestive setting could explain the pattern before the story hardens into a ghost account.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/hauntings_b4cf37_home_checks_62807a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Home Checks" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The safest first move is to look for risks that can harm people whether or not anyone believes in ghosts: carbon monoxide, faulty electrics, damp and mould, gas appliances, poor ventilation, pests, plumbing noise, structural movement and sleep-related hallucinations. Carbon monoxide is especially important because it is colourless and odourless, and poisoning can cause headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, chest pain and altered mental status. The CDC lists altered mental status among common symptoms, while UK gas-safety guidance stresses regular checking of appliances and flues.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clinical Guidance for Carbon Monoxide Poisoning...8 Jul 2024 — The most common symptoms of CO poisoning are headache, dizziness, weak...</span></span></span>

A sensible home check does not “debunk” a haunting by assumption. It slows the interpretation down. It asks: what exactly happened, where, when, to whom, and what changed in the house at the same time?

## What People Report

The experiences that make people say “this house feels haunted” are usually ordinary senses behaving in an extraordinary pattern. A noise happens in the same wall at night. A room feels colder than the rest of the house. A door opens by itself. Someone wakes unable to move and feels a presence beside the bed. Lights dim or flicker. A child hears scratching above the ceiling. A visitor smells something rotten or chemical where nobody else notices it.

Those details matter because each type of report points towards a different first check:

* **Knocks, bangs and footsteps:** plumbing, heating [pipes]({{ 'pipes/' | relative_url }}), roof movement, loose floorboards, wildlife in lofts, neighbours, shared walls or timed appliance cycles.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Flickering lights or appliances behaving oddly:** loose wiring, overloaded sockets, failing bulbs, old circuits, smart-device settings or remote controls.
* **Cold patches:** draughts, missing insulation, chimney airflow, damp walls, poor radiator balance or gaps around floors and windows.
* **Strange smells:** mould, drains, dead rodents, damp timber, gas leaks, electrical overheating or poorly vented combustion appliances.
* **Figures, voices or sensed presence around sleep:** sleep paralysis, hypnagogic or hypnopompic hallucinations, stress, sleep deprivation or medication effects.
* **Feeling ill in the house:** carbon monoxide exposure, mould, poor ventilation, chemical exposure, allergens, anxiety, infection or other medical causes.

</div>

This sorting step is useful because “haunted” is a conclusion, not a description. “A single loud bang in the kitchen after the heating clicks off” is more testable than “the house is angry”. “I wake at 3am unable to move and feel someone pressing on my chest” is more useful than “something visits me at night”. ## Check Safety First

A haunting claim should never delay a safety inspection. Some “spooky” signs overlap with hazards that deserve immediate attention.

Carbon monoxide comes first because it can be mistaken for [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}): headaches, nausea, dizziness, weakness, confusion, sleepiness or a strong sense that something is wrong. It is produced by incomplete combustion from appliances using fuels such as gas, oil, coal, wood, charcoal or petrol. UK guidance advises that gas appliances and flues are regularly checked at least every 12 months, and HSE notes that around seven people die each year in the UK from gas-related carbon monoxide poisoning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/domestic/faqownerocc.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hse.gov.uk">[HSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hse.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">HSEGas safety</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Home owners6 Nov 2024 — Before purchasing a CO alarm, always ensure it complies... A practical first check is simple:

1. **Make sure there are working carbon monoxide alarms** in rooms with fuel-burning appliances or flues, and follow the manufacturer’s instructions on placement and testing.
2. **Leave the property and seek urgent help** if several people feel unwell, symptoms improve outdoors, or an alarm sounds.
3. **Have boilers, fires, stoves, chimneys and flues checked by competent professionals**, especially if symptoms coincide with heating use, poor ventilation, storms or blocked chimneys.
4. **Do not use barbecues, camping stoves, generators or patio heaters indoors**, even in garages, conservatories or partially enclosed spaces.

### Noises in the walls, ceiling or floor

Houses make noises because they are moving systems, not sealed boxes. Water heats, pipes expand, floorboards flex, wind [pressure]({{ 'pressure/' | relative_url }}) changes, appliances cycle, animals move through cavities, and neighbours use shared structures.

A loud bang after a tap, washing machine or toilet shuts off may be water hammer. Scottish Water explains that banging in internal water pipes can be caused by waterlogged air chambers or high water pressure; air chambers are designed to soften the shock when a tap is turned off, but they can stop working properly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scottishwater.co.uk/-/media/ScottishWater/Document-Hub/Factsheets-and-Leaflets/Factsheets/240921SWNoiseinPipesv2lr.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scottishwater.co.uk">[Scottish Water]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scottishwater.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Scottish Water Your guide to noise in pipes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scottish Water Your guide to noise in pipes</span></span></span> A heating system can also make ticks, creaks and knocks as pipes expand and contract, especially where pipes rub against timber or pass tightly through holes.

Scratching, scurrying, squeaking or flapping above a bedroom is often not paranormal at all. It may be mice, rats, squirrels, birds, wasps or bats in the loft. Pest guidance from Rentokil notes that noises in lofts are commonly caused by rodents in winter, with other causes including birds, wasps, flies, bats and structural sounds from expanding or contracting materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rentokil.co.uk/noises-in-the-attic/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rentokil.co.uk">[Rentokil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rentokil.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rentokil.co.uk.</span></span></span>

A useful test is to keep a simple log: time, weather, heating status, water use, which appliances were running, and where the sound was loudest. If the “footsteps” happen after the boiler shuts off, after a toilet flush, when wind hits one side of the house, or only during dawn wildlife activity, the pattern is already pointing away from ghosts and towards maintenance.

### Lights, switches and electrical oddities

Flickering lights are one of the most easily mythologised household events because they feel responsive: a bulb dims just as a person mentions a ghost, or a lamp switches off during an argument. But electrical systems create coincidences constantly.

Check the simple causes first: loose bulbs, old dimmer switches, incompatible LED bulbs, remote-controlled sockets, smart-home routines, motion sensors, failing batteries, overloaded extension leads and appliances that draw heavy current when starting. Then look for warning signs: buzzing sockets, heat, scorch marks, repeated tripping, burning smells or visible damage. Those are not ghost-hunting clues; they are reasons to stop using the outlet or circuit and contact a qualified electrician. London Fire Brigade and UK government guidance both list flickering lights, hot plugs, scorch marks and unexplained tripping as signs that should be checked.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/safety/the-home/electrical-items/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: london-fire.gov.uk">[London Fire Brigade]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">london-fire.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">London Fire Brigade Electrical items</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">London Fire Brigade Electrical items</span></span></span>

Do not treat “it only happens in one room” as evidence of a presence. That is exactly how a localised wiring fault, loose connection, damp-affected fitting, faulty lamp or overloaded circuit would behave.

### Cold spots, draughts and “bad rooms”

A cold room can feel emotionally different from the rest of a house, especially at night. It can make people tense, alert and suggestible. Before treating it as a haunting, check the physical envelope of the room.

Look for gaps around skirting boards, windows, loft hatches, external doors, unused fireplaces, pipe penetrations, floorboards and old vents. Check whether the radiator heats fully or needs bleeding, whether furniture blocks heat flow, and whether the wall is damp to the touch. In older houses, cold patches may reflect mixed construction, blocked chimneys, poorly insulated extensions or moisture in external walls.

Humidity matters too. [Damp rooms]({{ 'damp-rooms/' | relative_url }}) often smell stale, feel colder, and worsen respiratory symptoms. UK government guidance links damp and mould to inadequate ventilation, leaks, condensation and building defects, and stresses that landlords and housing providers should act quickly rather than blaming occupants by default.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/damp-and-mould-understanding-and-addressing-the-health-risks-for-rented-housing-providers/understanding-and-addressing-the-health-risks-of-damp-and-mould-in-the-home--2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">understanding and addressing the health risks of damp and mould in the home 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">understanding and addressing the health risks of damp and mould in the home 2</span></span></span> For tenants in England, Shelter states that landlords should address damp and mould repairs and check whether the problem returns after repairs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://england.shelter.org.uk/housing_advice/repairs/damp_and_mould_in_rented_homes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: england.shelter.org.uk">[Shelter England]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">england.shelter.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">damp and mould in rented homes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">damp and mould in rented homes</span></span></span>

### Doors, movement and the house “answering back”

A door that opens by itself is unsettling because it seems intentional. The ordinary checks are alignment, hinges, latch depth, floor slope and air pressure. A house that has settled can leave a door frame slightly out of square; gravity then does the theatrical part. Open windows, extractor fans, chimneys and closing external doors can also create pressure changes that move a loose internal door.

Objects falling from shelves need the same treatment. Is the shelf level? Does a washing machine vibrate nearby? Is the wall damp or crumbling? Are fixings loose? Are there mice or squirrels in the cavity? Has a door slam or lorry vibration shaken the room? A haunting story often begins when the explanation is not visible at the exact moment the event happens.

### Smells, tastes and air quality

Smells often get folded into haunting stories because they feel intimate: perfume, smoke, decay, damp, drains, ozone or “old flowers”. They should be treated as evidence, but not automatically as evidence of a spirit.

A rotten smell may be drains, a dry trap, a dead animal, blocked ventilation or hidden damp. A fishy or hot-plastic smell may be electrical overheating. A musty smell suggests moisture, mould or old carpets. A smoky smell can come from chimneys, neighbours, previous occupants, porous materials, blocked flues or backdraughting. Because some air hazards are invisible or intermittent, smell should be logged alongside heating use, weather, open windows, appliance operation and where the smell is strongest.

Radon is not a typical cause of ghostly sensations, but it is a good example of why “invisible” should not be reserved for the supernatural. UKHSA describes radon as a colourless, odourless radioactive gas formed from uranium in rocks and soils, and advises householders to check whether a property is in a radon affected area, measure where appropriate, and act if levels are high.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ukradon.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ukradon.org">[ukradon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ukradon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U Kradon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U Kradon</span></span></span>

## When the Body Creates the Haunting

Some haunting reports are not caused by the house as a structure, but by the way the body and brain interpret conditions inside it.

Sleep paralysis is the clearest example. A person may wake in bed unable to move or speak, feel a presence in the room, feel pressure on the chest and become intensely frightened. ## A Practical Decision Path

The most useful way to investigate a supposedly haunted home is to move from urgent hazards to ordinary patterns, then to interpretation. This keeps people safe and prevents the story from outrunning the facts.

**1. Rule out immediate danger.**

Check carbon monoxide alarms, smoke alarms, gas appliances, flues, chimneys, electrical warning signs, burning smells, damp patches, mould and any symptoms affecting more than one person. If an alarm sounds, people feel acutely unwell, or there is a suspected gas or electrical hazard, leave the property and contact the appropriate emergency or qualified service.

**2. Map the report precisely.**

Write down what happened without interpretation: “three knocks behind the radiator at 11.40pm”, not “the ghost knocked”. Include date, time, room, weather, heating, water use, appliances, who was present, and whether anyone was falling asleep or waking up.

**3. Look for repeatable triggers.**

Try harmless checks: turn heating on and off, run taps, flush toilets, switch appliances, open and close windows, test doors, walk over suspected floorboards, and listen from neighbouring rooms. Do not stage dramatic “provocations”; they increase suggestion and rarely improve evidence.

**4. Separate sensory categories.**

A smell, a sound and a dream-like figure may not share one cause. Treat each as its own clue. A house can have a plumbing knock, a damp smell and one resident with sleep paralysis at the same time.

**5. Bring in the right professional.**

Use a Gas Safe registered engineer for gas appliances, a qualified electrician for electrical warning signs, a plumber for water hammer or leaks, a roofer or builder for ingress, a pest professional for wildlife or rodents, and a surveyor for structural movement. If symptoms involve confusion, hallucinations, fainting, severe headaches or breathlessness, treat them as medical rather than paranormal.

**6. Reassess after repairs.**<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://england.shelter.org.uk/housing_advice/repairs/damp_and_mould_in_rented_homes" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: england.shelter.org.uk">[england.shelter.org.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">england.shelter.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">damp and mould in rented homes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">damp and mould in rented homes</span></span></span>

The strongest mundane explanation is one that predicts change. If the banging stops after plumbing work, if the cold patch disappears after draught-proofing, if the “presence” stops after sleep improves, or if people stop feeling ill after a carbon monoxide issue is fixed, the haunting claim has weakened.

## Why the Story Persists

A home can be checked carefully and still feel haunted to the people who live there. That persistence is not always irrational. It often reflects how human memory, fear and place interact.

First, a haunting story gives scattered events a plot. Three unrelated incidents — a pipe bang, a dream-like waking experience and a flickering bulb — become more memorable when they are linked by one explanation. Once the house has a reputation, new events are sorted into the same file.

Secondly, [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}) changes attention. A visitor told that a room is haunted may notice every creak, cold draught and shadow. Psychologist Richard Wiseman’s work on suggestion is relevant here: in a fake séance study, an actor suggested that a table was moving when it was not, and about a third of participants later reported that it had moved; paranormal belief was associated with a greater likelihood of reporting movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/9078003_Belief_in_the_paranormal_and_suggestion_in_the_seance_room" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance room</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate Belief in the paranormal and suggestion in the seance room</span></span></span> That does not mean all witnesses are unreliable. It means group setting, prior belief and suggestion can shape what people remember.

Thirdly, old houses supply rich material. They have layered repairs, hidden voids, mixed wiring, old plumbing, draughts, uneven floors, former occupants and family stories. A new-build may make the same noise, but an older house gives the noise somewhere to belong.

Finally, some experiences are emotionally meaningful even after a practical explanation is found. Grief, stress, loneliness, moving house, illness and family conflict can make a place feel charged. A safety-first check should not ridicule that feeling. It should protect the people in the home, reduce uncertainty, and keep a ghost story from concealing a repair, medical issue or hazard that needs attention.

## What Counts as a Stronger Case After Checks?

After a proper home check, a haunting claim is stronger only if it survives better description, better safety control and better attempts at ordinary explanation. That does not prove a ghost. It means the account is less easily dismissed as a common household fault.

A more credible unresolved report has several features: it is documented close to the time; it is witnessed independently; it is not confined to sleep-wake transitions; it does not track heating, water, wind, pests or known appliance cycles; it continues after safety faults are repaired; and it is described without exaggeration. Even then, “unexplained” should remain the honest category unless there is positive evidence for a specific cause.

The most responsible conclusion is modest: check the home before naming the haunting. A strange house may be warning you about a boiler, a socket, a leak, a draught, a rat in the loft, a sleep problem or a story that has become more powerful than the evidence. The investigation is not less interesting because it begins with practical checks. It is more useful, because the first duty in a haunted home is still to keep the living safe.
