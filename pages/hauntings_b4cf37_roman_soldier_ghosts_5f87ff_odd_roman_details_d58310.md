## Introduction

Do odd details make a Roman soldier ghost story stronger or weaker? The famous York account at Treasurer’s House suggests the answer depends on how those details relate to history. Witness Harry Martindale described marching Roman soldiers who were not the polished legionaries familiar from films and schoolbooks. They wore green tunics, carried round shields, looked older and scruffier than expected, and appeared only from the knees upwards. Those unusual features became central to debates about the story because they can be read in two opposite ways. Believers argue that unexpected details may point to genuine historical knowledge that the witness was unlikely to possess. Sceptics argue that unusual details can arise through memory distortion, later [retelling]({{ 'retelling/' | relative_url }}), or folklore embellishment. The dispute over green tunics and other oddities has therefore become one of the most revealing aspects of Roman soldier ghost lore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireHarry&#x27;s account of the soldiers was dismissed by many academics due to hi...</span></span></span>

## The Green Tunics and Scruffy Soldiers Problem

The most famous Roman soldier apparition report in Britain comes from Harry Martindale’s 1953 experience in the cellar of Treasurer’s House in York. According to the account, he saw a column of Roman soldiers marching through the room. What made the report unusual was not merely the appearance of Roman troops but the specific way they were described. Martindale said the soldiers wore green tunics, carried round shields, had daggers on the right side, and looked somewhat shabby rather than like elite imperial troops.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer%27s_House%2C_York" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span></span></span>

These details mattered because they conflicted with the popular image of Roman legionaries. Mid-twentieth-century audiences generally expected Roman soldiers to appear in bright red clothing, neat formation, and highly standardised equipment. Green tunics sounded wrong. Round shields also seemed odd to people familiar with the rectangular shields commonly associated with the early empire. As a result, some commentators initially regarded the description as evidence against the account’s reliability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireHarry&#x27;s account of the soldiers was dismissed by many academics due to hi...</span></span></span>

The “scruffy soldiers” detail created a similar problem. Ghost stories often feature dramatic or idealised figures. Martindale instead described men who appeared older and less impressive than popular culture suggested Roman soldiers should be. The National Trust notes that critics dismissed the account partly because of these characteristics, while others later argued that such a description could fit a later Roman garrison composed of long-serving local troops rather than glamorous conquerors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireHarry&#x27;s account of the soldiers was dismissed by many academics due to hi...</span></span></span>

## When Historical Oddities Seem to Help a Ghost Story

Supporters of the York story often point to a simple argument: if someone invents a Roman ghost account, why include details that appear incorrect?

The argument rests on the claim that some of Martindale’s supposedly strange observations became less strange as historians developed a more nuanced understanding of the later Roman army. The National Trust notes that advocates linked the green tunics, older-looking soldiers, leather-strapped sandals and round shields to troops stationed in Britain during the empire’s later centuries rather than the stereotypical early imperial legionary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireHarry&#x27;s account of the soldiers was dismissed by many academics due to hi...</span></span></span>

The colour question is particularly interesting. Modern research into Roman military clothing suggests there was far more variation than the familiar “all Roman soldiers wore red” image implies. Historians and experimental researchers have pointed to evidence for undyed, white, red, blue, yellow and green garments in different periods and contexts. The idea of strict uniformity is now widely regarded as an oversimplification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tastesofhistory.co.uk/post/how-to-dress-as-a-roman-soldier-part-one" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tastesofhistory.co.uk">[Tastes Of History+2Imperium Romanum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tastesofhistory.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tastes Of HistoryHow To: Dress as a Roman soldier Part One23 Jan 2023 — The actual dyed colour of the Roman soldier&#x27;s tunic is uncertain...</span></span></span>

Because of this, believers argue that the green tunics have an unexpected ring of authenticity. If a witness in 1953 had simply invented Roman soldiers from popular imagery, they might have been expected to describe bright red uniforms rather than a less familiar colour. The apparent mismatch with popular expectations therefore becomes, for supporters, one of the story’s strongest features rather than one of its weaknesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireHarry&#x27;s account of the soldiers was dismissed by many academics due to hi...</span></span></span>

A similar argument is made about the round shields. Roman military equipment evolved considerably over centuries. The later Roman army used different shield forms and organisational structures from those of the better-known early empire. Thus, details once regarded as errors can later appear more plausible when examined against the full span of Roman military history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer%27s_House%2C_York" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span></span></span>

## Why Sceptics Remain Unconvinced

The same details that impress believers can raise caution flags for critics.

One problem is that historical accuracy is often judged retrospectively. After a famous ghost account becomes established, later researchers and enthusiasts may search for historical parallels that fit the story. This can create a selection effect in which matching details receive attention while awkward or contradictory elements fade into the background. A detail that appears unusually accurate may therefore reflect later interpretation rather than original evidential strength.

Another issue concerns the uncertainty surrounding Roman clothing itself. Historians continue to debate the prevalence of different tunic colours. While evidence supports greater variety than once assumed, there is no simple consensus that late Roman soldiers in York specifically wore green tunics as a standard uniform. The historical picture remains more complicated than many ghost-story retellings imply.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_military_clothing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2romanarmytalk.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Sceptics also note that eyewitnesses rarely observe unusual events with perfect precision. If a witness sees something unexpected, memory can fill gaps, simplify complex impressions, or reshape details over time. The very features that make a story memorable can become the features most vulnerable to unconscious alteration during repeated retellings.

From this perspective, odd details do not automatically count as evidence for authenticity. They may simply reflect the natural imperfections of human perception and recollection.

These details are vivid, unusual, and easy to repeat. As stories circulate through books, newspapers, television programmes, tours and online discussions, such elements become narrative anchors. Over time, they can acquire symbolic importance beyond their original role in the account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer%27s_House%2C_York" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span></span></span>

Folklore researchers have long observed that stories often become sharper and more coherent through retelling. Contradictions are reduced, striking details are emphasised, and uncertain elements become fixed. In a haunting narrative, a peculiar feature may gradually shift from being a casual observation into a central proof point. Once that happens, later audiences can mistakenly assume the detail always carried the same significance.

This does not mean the original witness was dishonest. Rather, it highlights how stories evolve as they pass through communities. The more famous a haunting becomes, the more likely it is that interpretation and narrative tradition become intertwined with the original testimony.

## Do Odd Details Make Ghost Stories Stronger?

The answer depends largely on what standard of evidence is being applied.

For believers, odd details such as green tunics and apparently outdated military equipment can strengthen a ghost story because they seem less likely to have been borrowed from popular stereotypes. Unexpected accuracy appears more impressive than expected accuracy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireHarry&#x27;s account of the soldiers was dismissed by many academics due to hi...</span></span></span>

For sceptics, the same details remain ambiguous. Historical knowledge changes, eyewitness memory is fallible, and famous stories accumulate layers of interpretation over time. A surprising detail may be significant, but it may also be coincidence, retrospective matching, or folklore development.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_military_clothing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Rather than settling the question of whether the soldiers were ghosts, these odd details keep the debate alive by giving both believers and sceptics something substantial to argue about.
