## Introduction

The most famous detail in York’s Roman soldier haunting is not the soldiers themselves. It is the fact that they were reportedly visible only from the knees upward. That single image transformed a local ghost story into a long-running debate about archaeology, memory and interpretation. According to the account given by apprentice plumber Harry Martindale, the marching figures he saw in the cellar of Treasurer’s House in 1953 appeared truncated below the knees, as though the lower parts of their bodies were hidden beneath the floor. Years later, when Roman remains beneath the site received renewed attention, supporters of the story argued that the soldiers seemed to be walking on a Roman road buried below the modern cellar surface. The “knees-up” detail became the central piece of evidence that distinguished the case from countless other haunting reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

## What the Witness Reportedly Saw

Harry Martindale’s account describes a column of Roman soldiers emerging from a cellar wall and marching across the room before disappearing into another wall. The soldiers were not reported as [floating]({{ 'floating/' | relative_url }}). Instead, they appeared to be walking normally, but only their upper bodies were visible at first. Martindale later stated that the men seemed to be cut off below the knees. As the procession moved, he claimed he could briefly see more of their legs and footwear, suggesting that the ground level on which they were marching was lower than the cellar floor where he stood.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer%27s_House%2C_York" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span></span></span>

This detail mattered because it was unusually specific. Ghost stories often contain vague descriptions that can be adapted to later discoveries. In contrast, the York account centred on a visual oddity that appeared difficult to explain. The soldiers were not transparent from the waist down, nor fading away gradually. They seemed physically obscured by the floor itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer%27s_House%2C_York" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Treasurer&#x27;s House, York</span></span></span>

The image became even more memorable because it matched the broader setting. Treasurer’s House stands within the area of Roman [Eboracum]({{ 'eboracum/' | relative_url }}), the fortress-city that served as one of the most important military centres in Roman Britain. Roman remains had long been known in the vicinity, making the idea of soldiers marching along an ancient route seem intuitively plausible to many listeners.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/history-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustHistory of Treasurer&#x27;s House &#124; YorkshireTreasurer&#x27;s House is one of the most prestigious addresses in York. ## How the Buried-Road Explanation Developed

The buried-road explanation emerged gradually rather than appearing immediately after the sighting. For years Martindale reportedly kept his story largely private, concerned that people would ridicule him. The account gained wider attention only after archaeological work highlighted the Roman infrastructure beneath the site. National Trust material notes that Treasurer’s House stands above one of the major roads of Roman York, the via Decumana.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

Once this connection became widely known, the haunting acquired a striking narrative logic. If a Roman road lay beneath the cellar, then the soldiers’ missing lower legs could be interpreted as evidence that they were walking on the original Roman surface rather than on the twentieth-century floor. In popular retellings, the haunting almost became a visual lesson in archaeology: modern [observers]({{ 'observers/' | relative_url }}) occupy one layer of history while the apparition occupies another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

The theory gained additional traction because it appeared to explain a feature that would otherwise seem inconvenient for believers. A witness inventing a Roman ghost story might be expected to describe complete figures. Instead, the unusual truncation became the story’s strongest selling point. Supporters argued that this was exactly the sort of detail an ordinary observer would be unlikely to fabricate because it initially made little sense.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thesimplethings.com/blog/national-trust-ghosts" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thesimplethings.com">[The Simple Things]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thesimplethings.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">national trust ghosts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Simple ThingsTop ten National Trust ghosts12 Sept 2020 — 1 The Roman Soldiers at Treasurer&#x27;s House. ## What Archaeology Can and Cannot Prove

Archaeology unquestionably strengthens one part of the story. Roman remains exist beneath Treasurer’s House, and investigations have confirmed that the site occupies ground associated with Roman York. Evidence also indicates the presence of an important Roman road in the area. These facts provide a genuine historical backdrop rather than a purely imagined one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

What archaeology cannot do is verify the apparition itself. The existence of a Roman road does not establish that ghostly soldiers were seen upon it. It only demonstrates that the setting described by later interpretations is historically plausible. A buried road can explain why believers find the account compelling, but it cannot independently confirm that Martindale witnessed an event beyond ordinary experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

There is also a timing issue. Critics note that the significance of the buried-road connection became clearer after archaeological discoveries and historical interpretation had entered public discussion. This raises the possibility that later retellings emphasised the match between sighting and archaeology more strongly than was apparent at the time of the original event. In folklore studies, such reinforcement is common: details that fit later evidence often become central to the narrative while less memorable aspects fade away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/yorkshire/treasurers-house-york/ghosts-of-treasurers-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaltrust.org.uk">[National Trust]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaltrust.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Trust The ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National TrustThe ghost stories of Treasurer&#x27;s House - YorkshireDiscover tales of ghost sightings around Treasurer&#x27;s House in York, inclu...</span></span></span>

Another limitation is precision. Even if a Roman road existed beneath the cellar, the exact relationship between the reported marching line and the archaeological remains is difficult to reconstruct decades after the event. The haunting story survives through testimony and [retelling]({{ 'retelling/' | relative_url }}), whereas archaeology records physical structures.
