## Introduction

Not every apparition report in a haunting file belongs in the same evidential category. One of the most important distinctions used by psychical researchers concerns experiences that occur while a witness is falling asleep, waking up, or lying immobile in bed. In these cases, investigators increasingly consider the possibility of sleep paralysis: a recognised sleep phenomenon in which awareness returns while the muscle paralysis associated with rapid eye movement (REM) sleep temporarily persists. The experience is often accompanied by vivid hallucinations, a strong sense of presence, and overwhelming fear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562322/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[NCBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NCBISleep Paralysis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">StatPearls - NCBI Bookshelfby M Farooq · 2023 · Cited by 28 — Sleep paralysis refers to the phenomenon in which resumption of consciousne...</span></span></span>

For societies that collect haunting reports, sleep paralysis matters because many classic “bedroom ghost” narratives contain features that closely match this [mechanism]({{ 'mechanism/' | relative_url }}). ## What Witnesses Commonly Experience

The reports most likely to raise questions about sleep paralysis share a remarkably consistent pattern across cultures and time periods.

Witnesses frequently describe:

* Waking suddenly but being unable to move or speak.
* Sensing that someone is standing beside the bed.
* Seeing a shadowy figure, intruder, ghost, demon, or human-shaped presence.
* Feeling [pressure]({{ 'pressure/' | relative_url }}) on the chest or difficulty breathing.
* Experiencing intense fear and certainty that the presence is real.
* Recovering movement abruptly, after which the figure disappears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562322/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[NCBI+2Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NCBISleep Paralysis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">StatPearls - NCBI Bookshelfby M Farooq · 2023 · Cited by 28 — Sleep paralysis refers to the phenomenon in which resumption of consciousne...</span></span></span>

Researchers studying sleep paralysis have identified recurring hallucination types. The most relevant to haunting files is the “intruder” experience: the feeling that a threatening being is in the room. Many sufferers report seeing a dark figure near the bed or in a doorway, a pattern so common that neuroscientists sometimes refer to it as the “bedroom intruder” phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5329044/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sleep Paralysis, “The Ghostly Bedroom Intruder” and Out-of...by B Jalal · 2017 · Cited by 49 — We have proposed that a functional dis...</span></span></span>

## Why Timing Around Sleep Changes the Case

In haunting files, timing is often as important as the apparition itself.

An unexplained figure seen while fully awake in a hallway, garden, or workplace raises different questions from a figure seen during a transition between sleeping and waking. Sleep researchers describe sleep paralysis as a mixed state in which elements of REM sleep continue while conscious awareness has already returned. The result can be a vivid experience that feels completely real despite originating within the sleeper's altered neurological state.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562322/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[NCBI+2cashp.columbian.gwu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NCBISleep Paralysis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">StatPearls - NCBI Bookshelfby M Farooq · 2023 · Cited by 28 — Sleep paralysis refers to the phenomenon in which resumption of consciousne...</span></span></span>

## How Files Separate Bedroom Apparitions

[Psychical research]({{ 'research-files/' | relative_url }}) organisations have long relied on classification rather than simple belief or disbelief. The practical question is how a report should be filed.

Modern reporting guidance from the Society for Psychical Research specifically asks experiencers to mention sleep paralysis when relevant. The inclusion of sleep-related information reflects an awareness that altered states around sleep can be crucial for interpreting unusual experiences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/report-an-experience" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">of-Body Experience: This refers to a sense of...Read more...</span></span></span>

When reviewing a bedroom apparition report, investigators commonly look for indicators such as:

**Position of the witness.** Was the person lying in bed or just waking up?

**Ability to move.** Was there temporary paralysis or a sensation of being pinned down?

**Duration.** Did the figure vanish as soon as movement returned?

**Witness corroboration.** Did anyone else observe the same event?

**Associated sleep factors.** Was the witness sleep-deprived, stressed, ill, or experiencing disrupted sleep? Such factors are linked to increased rates of sleep paralysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079217301120" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A systematic review of variables associated with sleep...by D Denis · 2018 · Cited by 231 — a study finding participants wh...</span></span></span>

Cases containing multiple sleep-paralysis markers are often placed in a separate analytical category from reports involving fully awake [observers]({{ 'observers/' | relative_url }}). The distinction helps preserve potentially valuable testimony while acknowledging known psychological and neurological explanations.

## Why the Experiences Feel So Convincing

One reason sleep paralysis remains important in haunting archives is that the experiences are not easily dismissed as ordinary dreams.

People commonly report certainty that they were awake. Many can describe the room accurately and recall details with unusual clarity. Researchers argue that this conviction arises because sleep paralysis combines waking awareness with dream-like imagery and bodily paralysis. The experience therefore feels less like a dream and more like an encounter occurring in the real environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6208952/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCThe neuropharmacology of sleep paralysis hallucinations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by B Jalal · 2018 · Cited by 75 — Sleep paralysis victims often complain of hypnogogic and hypnopompic hallucinations, such as seeing...</span></span></span>

Studies of presence hallucinations provide another clue. Experiments and neurological observations suggest that the human brain can generate a powerful sensation that another being is nearby even when no external person is present.
