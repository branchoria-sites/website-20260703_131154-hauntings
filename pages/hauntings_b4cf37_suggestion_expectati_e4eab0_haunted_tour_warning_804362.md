## Introduction

A ghost tour does more than provide information. It often provides a framework for interpretation before visitors enter a supposedly haunted location. A guide may describe a room as notorious for cold spots, unexplained [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}) or feelings of [being watched]({{ 'being-watched/' | relative_url }}). A sign may warn visitors that others have reported unusual sensations. By the time people step into the space, they are already looking for certain kinds of experiences.

This matters because many reports from haunted locations involve ambiguous events rather than clear observations. Visitors notice a draught, a shadow, a creak in an old building or a moment of unease and interpret it through the story they have just heard. Research into reputedly haunted sites, including [Hampton Court]({{ 'hampton-court/' | relative_url }}) Palace and Edinburgh's haunted vaults, suggests that [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}) and suggestion can influence both what people notice and how they explain it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242695933_An_investigation_into_the_alleged_haunting_of_Hampton_Court_Palace_Psychological_variables_and_magnetic_fields" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) An investigation into the alleged haunting of...December 1, 2002 — Hampton Court Palace is reputed to be one of the mo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2002</span></span></span>

## How Haunted Warnings Change Attention

[Ghost tours]({{ 'ghost-tours/' | relative_url }}) frequently use warnings and narrative cues as part of the experience. Tour descriptions invite visitors to watch for shadows, listen for footsteps or pay attention to unusual sensations. In Edinburgh's underground vault tours, visitors are often introduced to stories of restless spirits, historical tragedies and previous paranormal reports before entering the darkest spaces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mercattours.com/view-tour/haunted-underground" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mercattours.com">[Mercat Tours Ltd, Edinburgh, Scotland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mercattours.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mercat Tours Ltd, Edinburgh, ScotlandThe definitive Edinburgh underground ghost tourHear true stories of gruesome punishment, restless sp...</span></span></span>

These warnings do not necessarily tell people what they will see. Instead, they direct attention toward categories of experience:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* Changes in temperature.
* Unexpected sounds.
* Feelings of unease.
* Peripheral visual impressions.
* The sensation that someone is nearby.

</div>

Psychologists studying [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) have long noted that attention is selective. When people expect something unusual, they monitor the environment more closely. Ordinary events that might otherwise pass unnoticed become meaningful. A faint noise becomes something to investigate. A fleeting shadow becomes memorable. A moment of anxiety acquires a possible supernatural explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/BJP-hauntings.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman An investigation into alleged &#x27;hauntings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanAn investigation into alleged &#x27;hauntings&#x27; - Richard Wisemanby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 121 — The experiments took place...</span></span></span>

The Hampton Court Palace experiments provide a well-known example. Participants walked through reputedly haunted areas after receiving different information about paranormal activity. The findings suggested that reports of unusual experiences were influenced not only by prior belief in ghosts but also by the suggestions participants received before entering the location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242695933_An_investigation_into_the_alleged_haunting_of_Hampton_Court_Palace_Psychological_variables_and_magnetic_fields" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) An investigation into the alleged haunting of...December 1, 2002 — Hampton Court Palace is reputed to be one of the mo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2002</span></span></span>

### Why Stories Matter More Than Instructions

Ghost-tour warnings are rarely delivered as dry factual statements. They are embedded within stories.

A guide may describe a particular room as the place where visitors repeatedly feel a presence or hear footsteps. Research on ghost-tour storytelling shows that narratives connect visitors emotionally to the location, reducing the psychological distance between the audience and the events being described. The result is that the setting feels more immediate and personally relevant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.ut.ee/ghost-tours/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.ut.ee">[UT Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.ut.ee</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blog How uncanny sounds make ghost tours spookier</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UT BlogHow uncanny sounds make ghost tours spookierApril 23, 2021 — 23 Apr 2021 — The spoken narratives of the ghost tour are often grues...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 23, 2021</span></span></span>

This creates a powerful combination:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A dramatic story establishes expectations.
2. The environment supplies ambiguous sensory information.
3. Visitors interpret those sensations through the story.

</div>

In practice, the warning does not need to be explicit. Simply hearing that a location is one of the "most haunted" places in a city may be enough to alter what people notice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitscotland.com/info/tours/haunted-vaults-tour-989908f1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitscotland.com">[VisitScotland]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitscotland.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Haunted Vaults TourThis vaults tour takes you beneath the streets of Edinburgh&#x27;s Old Town into a dimly lit space that many c...</span></span></span>

## Why Vague Sensations Become Stronger in Groups

Many ghost tours take place in groups moving through dark, unfamiliar environments. This social setting can amplify attention and interpretation.

When one person reacts to a sound or claims to feel something unusual, others often become more alert. People begin scanning the environment for confirmation. A sensation that might have been ignored while alone becomes noteworthy because other members of the group appear to share the experience.

Research on paranormal tourism has found that visitors arrive with different expectations, beliefs and motivations, yet the shared experience of the tour can help shape how events are perceived and discussed afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372854055_An_exploratory_study_of_the_future_paranormal_tourist_experience_on_ghost_tours" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) An exploratory study of the future paranormal tourist</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) An exploratory study of the future paranormal tourist...August 2, 2023 — The purpose of this study is to explore the p...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 2, 2023</span></span></span>

Group settings also encourage a form of social validation. If several people report feeling cold in the same area, participants may interpret the experience as stronger evidence than if it occurred in isolation. This does not mean the reports are false. Rather, the group's reactions influence which sensations become memorable and which are dismissed.

### The Types of Experiences Most Often Reported

Reports emerging from haunted tours are remarkably consistent across locations. They usually involve:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-stack" markdown="1">

* Feeling watched.
* A sensed presence.
* Sudden chills.
* Brief visual impressions.
* Unexplained knocks, creaks or footsteps.
* Emotional shifts such as anxiety or dread.

</div>

Notably, these are the same kinds of experiences frequently discussed in psychological investigations of hauntings. They are often subjective, difficult to verify independently and open to multiple interpretations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://richardwiseman.wordpress.com/research/psychology-of-ghosts-and-hauntings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.wordpress.com">[Richard Wiseman+2Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman Ghosts and Hauntings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanGhosts and Hauntings - Richard Wiseman - WordPress.comDuring these tours, many people have experienced many unusual phenom...</span></span></span>

The pattern is important because it suggests that tour warnings may be especially effective at shaping ambiguous experiences rather than producing detailed sightings of fully formed [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}).

## What Tour Reports Can and Cannot Prove

Ghost-tour reports can provide valuable information about what visitors experience, but they are limited as evidence for determining whether a location is genuinely haunted.

What they can demonstrate is that certain places consistently evoke particular reactions. If many visitors independently report unease, chills or unusual perceptions in the same area, that is a real pattern worthy of investigation. Researchers have used such reports to study the relationship between belief, suggestion, environmental conditions and perception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/BJP-hauntings.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman An investigation into alleged &#x27;hauntings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanAn investigation into alleged &#x27;hauntings&#x27; - Richard Wisemanby R Wiseman · 2003 · Cited by 121 — The experiments took place...</span></span></span>

What they cannot easily demonstrate is the source of those experiences.

A visitor who hears a warning about footsteps and later notices a creaking sound may honestly report a paranormal encounter. However, the report alone cannot distinguish among several possibilities:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* An ordinary environmental source.
* Misinterpretation of an ambiguous stimulus.
* Expectation shaped by the tour narrative.
* A genuinely unexplained event.

</div>

### The Challenge of Separating Atmosphere from Evidence

Many famous ghost tours deliberately create an [atmosphere]({{ 'atmosphere/' | relative_url }}) of anticipation. Dark underground vaults, candlelit passages, stories of tragedy and warnings about previous sightings are central features of the experience. Tourism researchers note that such storytelling is not merely decoration; it is part of how ghost tours generate emotional engagement and meaning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gold.ac.uk/news/spectral-cities/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gold.ac.uk">[Goldsmiths, University of London+2UT Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gold.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">spectral cities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">smiths, University of LondonStudy reveals secrets behind ghost tour boom31 Oct 2019 — Their research argues that it&#x27;s the mysterious...</span></span></span>

From an evidential perspective, however, atmosphere complicates interpretation. If visitors are encouraged to expect strange events, then reports collected afterwards cannot be treated as neutral observations. The warning itself becomes part of the event being studied.

For that reason, haunted-tour accounts are often most useful as evidence about human perception, expectation and interpretation in allegedly haunted settings. They show how stories, environments and social experiences interact to shape what visitors notice.
