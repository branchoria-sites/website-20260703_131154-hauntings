## Introduction

Among the many reports of hauntings, few are as emotionally powerful as waking in the night and seeing a figure standing beside the bed. Witnesses often describe a dark silhouette, a person-shaped shadow, a stranger watching them, or a presence that feels unmistakably real. Yet one of the strongest scientific explanations for many of these bedroom [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}) is sleep paralysis: a state in which awareness returns before the body has fully emerged from rapid eye movement (REM) sleep. During this brief overlap between sleeping and waking, people can experience paralysis, intense fear, and vivid hallucinations that seem to occupy the real bedroom around them. Research has repeatedly shown that sensed presences, [shadow figures]({{ 'shadow-figures/' | relative_url }}) and threatening intruders are among the most common features of sleep paralysis episodes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562322/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[NCBI+2ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NCBISleep Paralysis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">StatPearls - NCBI Bookshelfby M Farooq · 2023 · Cited by 28 — [1] It is often accompanied by visual hallucinations of the intruder and in...</span></span></span>

For anyone examining apparition reports within the broader subject of [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}), sleep paralysis matters because it can produce experiences that feel indistinguishable from a supernatural encounter while occurring entirely within a known sleep-related state.

## The Figure Beside the Bed

The classic sleep paralysis story closely resembles many haunting accounts. A person wakes during the night, becomes aware of the room, and discovers that they cannot move or speak. Moments later they sense that someone—or something—is present nearby. The figure may stand in a doorway, hover beside the bed, sit on the chest, lean over the sleeper or remain motionless in a corner. The experience is usually accompanied by intense dread.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21974-sleep-paralysis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: my.clevelandclinic.org">[Cleveland Clinic+2Sleep Foundation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">my.clevelandclinic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It goes away within seconds to minutes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cleveland ClinicSleep Paralysis: What It Is, Causes, Symptoms &amp; Treatment3 Jul 2024 — Sleep paralysis happens when you can&#x27;t move your bo...</span></span></span>

Sleep researchers commonly classify these experiences into recurring patterns:

* **Intruder hallucinations:** the feeling that a threatening person or presence is in the room.
* **Incubus experiences:** [chest pressure]({{ 'chest-pressure/' | relative_url }}), suffocation sensations and the impression that a being is physically restraining the sleeper.
* **Vestibular-motor experiences:** [floating]({{ 'floating/' | relative_url }}), flying, falling or out-of-body sensations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11344621/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by V Bhalerao · 2024 · Cited by 9 — One component, known as &quot;intruder hallucination,&quot; involves feelings of panic, a sense of presence...</span></span></span>

The “intruder” pattern is especially relevant to apparition reports because it directly produces the perception of a figure in the bedroom. Studies of sleep paralysis repeatedly identify sensed presences and threatening humanoid forms as common features of these episodes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079217301120" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect+2PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A systematic review of variables associated with sleep...by D Denis · 2018 · Cited by 228 — These episodes are frequently a...</span></span></span>

## How Sleep Paralysis Creates Sensed Presences

During normal REM sleep, the brain actively suppresses most voluntary muscle movement. This temporary paralysis prevents people from physically acting out their dreams. In sleep paralysis, awareness returns while some REM-related processes are still active. The result is an unusual hybrid state: the person feels awake but remains partially trapped in REM physiology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562322/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[NCBI+2cashp.columbian.gwu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NCBISleep Paralysis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">StatPearls - NCBI Bookshelfby M Farooq · 2023 · Cited by 28 — [1] It is often accompanied by visual hallucinations of the intruder and in...</span></span></span>

Researchers believe that dream imagery can spill into waking awareness during this transition. Instead of appearing in a dream world, the imagery becomes anchored to the real bedroom. A wardrobe becomes a watcher. A dark corner becomes a hiding place. The room itself remains visible, but dream-like perceptions are layered onto it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cashp.columbian.gwu.edu/close-encounter-or-just-half-sleep-how-scientific-inquiry-can-inform-paranormal-experiences" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cashp.columbian.gwu.edu">[cashp.columbian.gwu.edu+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cashp.columbian.gwu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Close Encounter or Just Half Sleep?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">How Scientific Inquiry...Hallucinations also occur often during sleep paralysis, and might come from parts of the brain still producing...</span></span></span>

The sensation of a nearby presence appears to be particularly important. Some neuroscientists have proposed that disruptions in the brain systems responsible for constructing a sense of the body's position and boundaries may contribute to the feeling that another being is standing nearby. In this view, the brain generates a representation of a second presence even though no external person exists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00092/full" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: frontiersin.org">[Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">frontiersin.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FrontiersSleep Paralysis, “The Ghostly Bedroom Intruder” and Out...by B Jalal · 2017 · Cited by 49 — We have proposed that a functional...</span></span></span>

This helps explain a curious feature of many reports: witnesses often insist they sensed the presence before they clearly saw it. The feeling that someone is there may arise first, with the visual image arriving later as the brain attempts to explain the sensation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17574867/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sensed presence as a correlate of sleep paralysis distress...by E Solomonova · 2008 · Cited by 77 — Isolated sleep paralysis (ISP...</span></span></span>

## Why Bedroom Apparitions Feel So Real

A common misconception is that sleep paralysis hallucinations resemble ordinary dreams. People who experience them frequently report the opposite.

Unlike dreams, which are usually recognised as dreams after waking, sleep paralysis occurs while the person is aware of their actual surroundings. They can often see familiar furniture, windows, doors and lighting conditions exactly as they appear every night. The hallucinated figure therefore appears embedded within a recognisable reality rather than inside a separate dream landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.webmd.com/sleep-disorders/what-are-hypnagogic-hallucinations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: webmd.com">[WebMD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">webmd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Web MDWhat Are Hypnagogic Hallucinations?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sleep DisordersSleep paralysis is often associated with hypnopompic hallucinations. Several factors combine to make the experience exceptionally convincing:

* **Visual realism:** the bedroom itself is visible and familiar.
* **Physical sensations:** chest [pressure]({{ 'pressure/' | relative_url }}), breathing difficulty or tactile sensations can accompany the vision.
* **Emotional intensity:** fear centres in the brain are highly active during many episodes.
* **Inability to test reality:** paralysis prevents the person from turning on a light, moving closer or fleeing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sleepfoundation.org/parasomnias/sleep-paralysis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sleepfoundation.org">[Sleep Foundation+2Frontiers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sleepfoundation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">sleep paralysis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sleep FoundationSleep Paralysis: Symptoms, Causes, and Treatment25 Jul 2025 — Intruder hallucinations: These hallucinations involve the p...</span></span></span>

This last point is crucial. In ordinary circumstances, a person can quickly verify whether a figure is real. During sleep paralysis, the witness is trapped in place. The inability to investigate often reinforces the conviction that the figure is genuinely present.

Researchers studying sleep paralysis note that many sufferers remain aware that something may be wrong with their perception yet still experience overwhelming fear because the hallucination feels perceptually real.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6208952/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCThe neuropharmacology of sleep paralysis hallucinations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by B Jalal · 2018 · Cited by 72 — Moreover, like hallucinogenic drugs, sleep paralysis is commonly associated with experiencing halluc...</span></span></span>

## Why So Many People See Shadow Figures

Across cultures, centuries and belief systems, remarkably similar bedroom entities appear in reports. Witnesses describe shadow people, dark silhouettes, faceless intruders, old women, demons, witches or men wearing hats. Although cultural details differ, the underlying structure is strikingly consistent: a threatening humanoid presence appears during paralysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33573871/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PubMed&quot;Men Fear Most What They Cannot See.&quot; sleep paralysis...by B Jalal · 2021 · Cited by 8 — They often entail seeing and sensing shad...</span></span></span>

One explanation is that the brain is attempting to interpret ambiguous visual information while simultaneously experiencing fear and a sense of danger. Human perception is strongly biased toward detecting other humans, especially in uncertain conditions. A dark, incomplete shape can therefore become a person-shaped figure. ## Clues That a Sighting May Be Sleep-Related

Not every reported apparition occurs during sleep paralysis, but certain features strongly suggest a sleep-related origin.

Signs that point toward sleep paralysis include:

* The experience occurred while falling asleep or waking up.
* The witness could not move, speak or call out.
* The figure appeared in the bedroom rather than elsewhere.
* A powerful sense of dread accompanied the sighting.
* Chest pressure, suffocation sensations or buzzing sounds were present.
* The figure vanished suddenly when movement returned.
* Similar episodes occurred during periods of stress, sleep deprivation or irregular sleep schedules.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21974-sleep-paralysis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: my.clevelandclinic.org">[Cleveland Clinic+2NCBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">my.clevelandclinic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It goes away within seconds to minutes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cleveland ClinicSleep Paralysis: What It Is, Causes, Symptoms &amp; Treatment3 Jul 2024 — Sleep paralysis happens when you can&#x27;t move your bo...</span></span></span>

Haunting narratives arise from many different situations, and some reports lack the characteristic features of sleep paralysis.
