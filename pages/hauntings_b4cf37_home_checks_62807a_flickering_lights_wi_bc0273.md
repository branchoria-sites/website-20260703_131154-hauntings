## Introduction

Flickering lights are one of the most common features in haunted-house stories. A lamp blinks at the right moment, a hallway light dims when someone enters a room, or bulbs seem to fail repeatedly in one part of the house. Before treating these events as evidence of a haunting, it is worth treating them as possible electrical faults. In many cases, the explanation is ordinary: a loose bulb, a faulty switch, a voltage fluctuation, or a problem elsewhere on the circuit. More importantly, some electrical problems that create “ghostly” effects can also create overheating, arcing and fire risks. Electrical safety organisations, fire services and electricians consistently warn that persistent flickering lights, hot sockets, scorch marks and repeatedly tripping circuits should be investigated as safety issues rather than mysteries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/media-centre/press-releases/2023/10/tech-experts-debunk-haunted-house-signs/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk">[Electrical Safety First+2esfrs.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">tech experts debunk haunted house signs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electrical Safety FirstTech experts debunk haunted house signs27 Oct 2023 — Flickering lights or bulbs that keep blowing are likely cause...</span></span></span>

## Are Flickering Lights a Warning Sign?

A single flickering light is not automatically a sign of faulty wiring. The simplest explanation is often the correct one. Bulbs can loosen slightly over time, LED lamps can be incompatible with older dimmer switches, and some lights briefly dim when a high-power appliance starts up.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.realsimple.com/reasons-you-shouldnt-ignore-flickering-lights-11788339" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: realsimple.com">[Real Simple]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">realsimple.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Common causes include loose bulbs, faulty switches or outlets, overloaded circuits, loose wiring, and power surges. ## Simple Causes Before Spooky Interpretations

Many reports of “responsive” lights can be explained by common electrical issues.

**Loose bulbs and fittings.** A bulb that is not seated properly can create intermittent contact, producing flickering that appears random. This is one of the easiest causes to rule out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.realsimple.com/reasons-you-shouldnt-ignore-flickering-lights-11788339" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: realsimple.com">[Real Simple]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">realsimple.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Common causes include loose bulbs, faulty switches or outlets, overloaded circuits, loose wiring, and power surges. **Faulty switches or dimmers.** Worn contacts inside a switch can interrupt power briefly. Older dimmers are a frequent source of flickering with modern LED lamps.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.khlelectricalcontractors.co.uk/post/flickering-lights-what-it-really-means-and-when-it-s-a-serious-electrical-emergency" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: khlelectricalcontractors.co.uk">[KHL Electrical Contractor]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">khlelectricalcontractors.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">KHL Electrical Contractor Flickering Lights?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What It Really Means and When It&#x27;s a...4 Dec 2025 — 1. **Voltage fluctuations.** Large appliances such as showers, ovens and kettles can temporarily affect voltage on a circuit. While some brief dimming may occur, persistent or severe fluctuations suggest an underlying issue that should be checked.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk/guidance/your-questions-answered/questions/the-lights-flicker-when-our-new-kettle-turned-is-on-and-we-have-similar-issues-when-the-shower-is-turned-on-what-should-we-do/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk">[Electrical Safety First]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">electricalsafetyfirst.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electrical Safety FirstThe lights flicker when our new kettle turned is on and we...It sounds like you have a problem with your lighting...</span></span></span>

## Electrical Warning Signs Not to Ignore

When evaluating unusual activity in a house, some signs should move the situation out of the “curious” category and into the “safety concern” category immediately.

Several fire and electrical safety authorities identify the following as warning signs of faulty or ageing wiring:
