## Introduction

Among all the sounds that inspire haunting stories, few are more convincing than a sudden bang from inside a wall, a sharp knock beneath the floor, or a sequence of clicks that seems to move through an empty house. Plumbing and heating systems are particularly good at creating these experiences because they operate out of sight and respond to changes in water [pressure]({{ 'pressure/' | relative_url }}), temperature and airflow. What sounds like an intentional knock can be a pressure wave in a pipe; what resembles [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}) overhead may be a series of expansion clicks travelling through hidden pipework.

In many reported “haunted house” situations, the sounds are real, but their source is mechanical rather than paranormal. Water moving through pipes, metal expanding as it warms, radiators filling with hot water and air ducts changing shape can all produce noises that seem surprisingly purposeful, especially at night when other background sounds have faded. ## Is It a Ghost or the Plumbing?

The first clue is often timing. Plumbing and heating noises tend to coincide with a tap being turned off, a boiler firing up, central heating beginning a cycle, or a hot-water appliance running. Because the trigger may occur in another room—or several minutes before the sound is noticed—the connection is not always obvious.

Many of the noises associated with [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) match common plumbing and heating sounds:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A loud bang behind a wall can be water hammer.
* Repeated ticking can be pipes expanding and contracting.
* Knocking under floorboards may come from heating pipes shifting against timber.
* Gurgling sounds can indicate trapped air in radiators or pipework.
* Popping noises from ceilings or walls may originate from heating ducts responding to temperature changes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://plumbers365.uk/the-clicking-house-diagnosing-your-heating-pipe-noises" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: plumbers365.uk">[Plumbers 365 UK+2Ductworks]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">plumbers365.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Plumbers 365 UKThe &quot;Clicking&quot; House: Diagnosing Your Heating Pipe NoisesFebruary 27, 2026 — Thermal Expansion is a rhythmic ticking that...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 27, 2026</span></span></span>

</div>

The reason these sounds feel uncanny is that they often occur intermittently and without a visible cause. Unlike a washing machine spinning or a door closing, the source remains hidden behind walls, beneath floors or inside service voids.

## Water Hammer and Pipe Expansion

### Why pipes can sound like someone is knocking

Water hammer is one of the most dramatic non-paranormal sources of “haunted” noises. It occurs when moving water is forced to stop or change direction suddenly, creating a pressure surge that travels through the pipework. The resulting shock wave can produce a loud bang, thump or series of knocks that seem to come from inside walls. Engineers describe it as a hydraulic shock phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_shock" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hydraulic shock</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hydraulic shock</span></span></span>

In domestic homes, common [triggers]({{ 'triggers/' | relative_url }}) include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Rapidly closing taps or valves.
* Washing machines and dishwashers shutting off water flow.
* Toilet fill valves.
* Heating-system valves opening or closing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_shock" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Classic Plumbing &amp; Air]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hydraulic shock</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hydraulic shock</span></span></span>

</div>

Because the pressure wave travels through the plumbing network, the sound may emerge far from the appliance that caused it. A washing machine in one room can create a bang that seems to come from a completely different part of the house. This disconnect helps explain why occupants sometimes interpret the noise as unexplained knocking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hencoplumbing.com/water-hammer-or-haunted-house/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hencoplumbing.com">[Henco Plumbing Services]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hencoplumbing.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It may sound like a ghost is knocking on the walls.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Henco Plumbing ServicesWater Hammer or Haunted House?October 17, 2022 — 17 Oct 2022 — A water hammer is a loud thumping or banging noise...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 17, 2022</span></span></span>

### The quieter but more persistent culprit: thermal expansion

Not every haunted-sounding noise is a dramatic bang. Heating pipes routinely expand when hot water enters them and contract as they cool. If a pipe passes tightly through a joist, floorboard or wall opening, the movement may be released in small jumps rather than smoothly.

The result is a pattern of clicks, ticks, creaks or light knocks that often begins shortly after the heating switches on and continues as temperatures change. These sounds can seem to travel through a building because different sections of pipe expand at different times.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://plumbers365.uk/the-clicking-house-diagnosing-your-heating-pipe-noises" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: plumbers365.uk">[Plumbers 365 UK+2Trade Radiators]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">plumbers365.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Plumbers 365 UKThe &quot;Clicking&quot; House: Diagnosing Your Heating Pipe NoisesFebruary 27, 2026 — Thermal Expansion is a rhythmic ticking that...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 27, 2026</span></span></span>

This [mechanism]({{ 'mechanism/' | relative_url }}) is particularly effective at creating the impression of movement. A series of expansion noises progressing from one room to another can resemble footsteps or someone walking through an upper floor.

## Radiators, Boilers and HVAC Sounds

## Why These Noises Feel Intentional

Human perception naturally searches for patterns. A random creak may be ignored, but three knocks in succession can feel deliberate. Plumbing and heating systems often produce sounds that accidentally mimic human signalling.

Several factors reinforce this impression:

* **Repetition:** Heating cycles occur daily and often at similar times.
* **Location ambiguity:** Sound travels through pipes, ducts and structural cavities.
* **Night-time quiet:** Mechanical noises become more noticeable after dark.
* **Hidden sources:** Occupants cannot immediately verify what produced the sound.
* **[Expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}):** In a house already believed to be haunted, ordinary sounds may acquire additional meaning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://plumbers365.uk/the-clicking-house-diagnosing-your-heating-pipe-noises" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: plumbers365.uk">[Plumbers 365 UK+2Reddit]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">plumbers365.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Plumbers 365 UKThe &quot;Clicking&quot; House: Diagnosing Your Heating Pipe NoisesFebruary 27, 2026 — Thermal Expansion is a rhythmic ticking that...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 27, 2026</span></span></span>
