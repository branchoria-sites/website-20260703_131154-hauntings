## Introduction

Many rooms that earn a reputation for feeling “haunted” do not contain anything visibly unusual. People describe a sense of [pressure]({{ 'pressure/' | relative_url }}), unease, dread, or the feeling that someone else is present before they notice any sound at all. One of the most plausible physical explanations for some of these experiences is hidden building machinery. Boilers, extraction fans, ventilation systems, pumps, and associated ductwork can generate low-frequency vibrations that are difficult to identify consciously but can make a space feel oppressive or unsettling. In older buildings, where equipment may be tucked behind walls, beneath floors, or in basements, occupants often experience the sensation long before they discover its source. Research into low-frequency sound and infrasound suggests that such machinery can alter mood, increase irritation, and contribute to the atmospheric conditions that people sometimes interpret as paranormal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260502233901.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedaily.com">[ScienceDaily+2The Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The creepy feeling in old buildings might have a surprising...3 May 2026 — In an old building, there is a good chance that i...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

## Common Low-Frequency Sources in Old Buildings

The machinery most often implicated in reports of oppressive rooms is not exotic. It is the ordinary infrastructure that keeps a building functioning.

Boilers are a frequent source because they combine combustion, pumps, circulating water, metal pipework, and vibrating housings. A boiler may not sound particularly loud, yet it can transmit low-frequency energy through floors, walls, and connected pipe systems. In older buildings, worn components or poorly isolated mountings can increase vibration transmission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15273022/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Disturbing effects of low frequency sound immissions and...by H Findeis · 2004 · Cited by 35 — If mechanical vibrations in the fre...</span></span></span>

Ventilation and extraction fans are especially important because rotating blades naturally generate repeating pressure fluctuations. When those fluctuations interact with room dimensions, ducts, corridors, or cavities, they can create standing waves or resonances. Occupants may not hear a clear noise source. Instead, they may perceive a vague feeling of discomfort, pressure, or agitation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman+2IFLScience]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

Other contributors include:

* Basement air-handling units.

* Circulation pumps.
* Industrial refrigeration equipment.

## Why Hidden Machinery Can Feel Eerie

Low-frequency sound behaves differently from ordinary noise. Higher-pitched sounds are usually easy to locate because the ear can identify their direction. Low-frequency vibrations are much harder to localise and can pass through walls, floors, and doors with relatively little attenuation.

This creates a peculiar situation: a person experiences a bodily sensation without a visible cause. The room feels wrong, yet there is no identifiable source of danger. That mismatch encourages interpretation. In a mundane office, people may blame the building. In a location already associated with ghost stories, the same sensations can be interpreted as evidence of a presence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260502233901.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedaily.com">[ScienceDaily+2ScienceAlert]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedaily.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The creepy feeling in old buildings might have a surprising...3 May 2026 — In an old building, there is a good chance that i...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

Research published in 2026 found that exposure to 18 Hz infrasound increased irritability, altered emotional responses, and raised cortisol, a hormone associated with stress and vigilance. Participants generally could not reliably tell when the infrasound was present, yet measurable changes still occurred. The findings support the idea that people can react physically to low-frequency energy without consciously recognising its source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/apr/27/spooky-feelings-in-old-houses-may-be-caused-by-boiler-sounds-study-suggests" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian+2Earth.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Researchers discovered that even though participants could not consciously hear infrasound, exposure led to increased irritability, eleva...</span></span></span>

This does not mean a boiler or fan creates ghostly [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}). The evidence is much narrower. What the research suggests is that hidden machinery can contribute to feelings of tension, discomfort, alertness, or unease that make paranormal interpretations seem more plausible in the moment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/apr/27/spooky-feelings-in-old-houses-may-be-caused-by-boiler-sounds-study-suggests" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian+2ScienceDaily]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Researchers discovered that even though participants could not consciously hear infrasound, exposure led to increased irritability, eleva...</span></span></span>

## The Famous Fan That Turned a Room Into a “Haunted” Space

The best-known example comes from engineer Vic Tandy's [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}) of a laboratory that staff believed was haunted.

Tandy reported experiencing a sense of dread and the feeling of [being watched]({{ 'being-watched/' | relative_url }}) while working alone. He also described seeing a grey shape in peripheral vision. The breakthrough came when he noticed a fencing foil vibrating unexpectedly. Investigation suggested that an extraction fan, combined with the dimensions of the room, was producing a standing wave near 19 Hz. Tandy and Tony Lawrence later argued that this low-frequency environment could help explain some of the strange sensations reported in the laboratory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Damn Interesting+3Richard Wiseman+3ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

The story became influential because the suspected source was not paranormal but mechanical. A recently installed fan appeared to coincide with the onset of reports. The room felt haunted before anyone understood its acoustic conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.damninteresting.com/curio/the-original-ghostbuster/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: damninteresting.com">[Damn Interesting]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">damninteresting.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the original ghostbuster</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Damn InterestingThe Original GhostbusterAug 14, 2018 — Apparently the combination of the fan and the geometry of the room had produced a...</span></span></span>

## When Room Geometry Amplifies the Effect

Machinery alone is not always enough. The shape of a building can matter just as much.

Long corridors, basement chambers, stairwells, duct networks, and interconnected rooms can act as resonant structures. A fan or boiler may generate low-frequency energy continuously, but only certain spaces experience the strongest effects because their dimensions reinforce specific frequencies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ioa.org.uk/blogs/things-go-bump-night" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ioa.org.uk">[Institute of Acoustics]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ioa.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Institute of AcousticsThings that go bump in the night…The investigator used an impulsive source and an infrasonic/low frequency measurem...</span></span></span>

This helps explain a common pattern in haunting reports: one room feels oppressive while nearby rooms seem normal.

Instead of a supernatural hotspot, the building may contain an acoustic hotspot. Resonance can create areas where vibration intensity rises and falls dramatically over short distances.
