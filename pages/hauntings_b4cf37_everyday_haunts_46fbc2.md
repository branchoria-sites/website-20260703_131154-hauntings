## Introduction

Haunted workplaces, pubs and everyday sites show that ghost reputations do not need a ruined castle or a stately home to survive. A pub cellar, a hotel corridor, a museum office, a [fire station]({{ 'fire-station/' | relative_url }}) yard or a shop after closing can become “haunted” when repeated reports attach to a place where ordinary life already contains noise, fatigue, routine, memory and local storytelling. The reports usually involve footsteps, figures seen at the edge of vision, taps or lights behaving oddly, doors moving, cold patches, smells, voices, or the strong sense that someone unseen is nearby. The key point is not that these stories prove ghosts. It is that everyday haunted places reveal how hauntings are made socially: by staff talk, customer retellings, awkward architecture, historical fragments, environmental quirks and the value of a memorable local legend. Research on anomalous experiences has repeatedly pointed to a mix of physical setting, [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}), ambiguous perception and cultural framing rather than a single simple cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7304295/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAn Environmental Appraisal of “Haunted Houses”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PMCAn Environmental Appraisal of “Haunted Houses”</span></span></span>

## What people report

In everyday haunts, the most striking feature is often not the drama of the report but its ordinariness. A landlord hears [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}) after closing. A cleaner sees a figure in a corridor. A firefighter mistakes a shape in the yard for a person in waterproofs. A customer photographs a mirror and later notices an unexplained form behind them. These are not usually grand [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}) performing a full historical scene. They are brief, uncertain experiences that become more meaningful because they happen in a building already known by staff, regulars and visitors.

Pubs are especially fertile ground because they combine public storytelling with private corners. They have cellars, staircases, old rooms, late-night closing routines, regular customers and staff who inherit stories from predecessors. Historic England’s collection of haunted pub examples treats pubs and inns as story-bearing buildings rather than as mere backdrops: the Red Lion at Avebury, for example, is presented as a late-sixteenth- or early-seventeenth-century building later used as a coaching inn, with reported ghosts including “Florrie”, a phantom carriage, and a glass-topped well now forming a bar table.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/licence-to-chill-10-of-england-s-most-haunted-pubs/ugXByzhuRqdRKw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on google.com.</span></span></span>

The pattern is similar at London’s Grenadier in Belgravia, where modern reports focus on a named ghost, “Cedric”, said to be a young guard killed after cheating at cards. Contemporary coverage describes staff and visitors reporting taps turning on, figures appearing after closing, and customers visiting specifically to try to contact the ghost. The important detail is that the story is not just attached to the old building; it is repeated through staff memory, customer behaviour and the pub’s identity as a destination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thesun.co.uk/news/34497777/haunted-pub-stalked-ghost-london-grenadier/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thesun.co.uk">[The Sun]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thesun.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The second case involves Samantha and Rich Perry from Burton-on-Trent, who claim their Victorian house was haunted for four years by a ma...</span></span></span>

Workplaces add a different texture. A haunted workplace is often experienced by people who are not there for entertainment: cleaners, chefs, security staff, firefighters, office workers, hotel employees or museum guides. Bristol Fire Station at Temple Back is a useful example because the story is tied to a modern working building, opened in 1975, but explained through a much older site history connected in local accounts to the Knights Templar. Reports describe fire crews seeing odd figures, including one chased from a kitchen before apparently disappearing through closed doors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bristol247.com/news-and-features/features/on-the-hunt-for-ghosts-of-bristol-past/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bristol247.com">[Bristol24/7]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bristol247.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bristol24/7On the hunt for ghosts of Bristol past</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bristol24/7On the hunt for ghosts of Bristol past</span></span></span>

Reports from ordinary sites tend to cluster around a few repeatable forms:

* **Closing-time noises:** footsteps, bangs, doors, scraping chairs and voices heard when staff believe the building is empty.
* **Peripheral figures:** shapes glimpsed in corridors, behind bars, on staircases, in mirrors or on camera footage.
* **Small physical disturbances:** taps, lights, glasses, bottles, doors or electrical equipment behaving in ways that seem deliberate.
* **Atmospheric sensations:** cold patches, pressure, dizziness, unease, or the feeling of [being watched]({{ 'being-watched/' | relative_url }}).
* **Story-linked triggers:** a cellar, well, former bedroom, staff-only corridor or old staircase becomes the “known” place where later experiences are interpreted.

Those categories matter because they are also the categories most vulnerable to ordinary explanations. A single noise in a closed pub may be pipes, refrigeration, neighbours, traffic, wood movement or a staff member elsewhere in the building. A figure in a workplace may be a reflection, a shadow, a person briefly misidentified, or a camera artefact. Yet once a site has a ghost name and a story, the same ambiguous event becomes easier to classify as another sighting.

## Why pubs and workplaces become haunted

A castle can announce itself as haunted through age and spectacle. An ordinary workplace has to earn the reputation differently. The haunting usually grows from the friction between the familiar and the slightly wrong. People know how a bar, office, shop or station should sound and feel. When the familiar pattern breaks — a footstep from a locked upper floor, a smell with no source, a door that opens again after closing — the disruption is more memorable because it happens in a place of routine.

Pubs have an extra advantage: they are designed for retelling. A ghost story in a pub is not only a claim about the past; it is a social performance. It can be told by a landlord to a new staff member, by a regular to a visitor, by a ghost-tour guide to a group, or by a chain pub’s marketing team during Halloween. Greene King, for instance, openly frames its haunted pub round-up as part of the pleasure of “a pint with a fright”, showing how ghost reputation can become part of ordinary hospitality branding rather than a hidden embarrassment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.greeneking.co.uk/behind-the-bar/most-haunted-pubs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: greeneking.co.uk">[Greene King]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">greeneking.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Greene King Most Haunted Pubs &#124; 6 UK pubs with ghosts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Greene King Most Haunted Pubs &#124; 6 UK pubs with ghosts</span></span></span>

The Red Lion at Avebury shows how several layers can reinforce each other. It is not just an old pub: it sits inside one of England’s most famous prehistoric landscapes, contains a visible well, and carries a Civil War-era legend that gives the haunting a named victim, a place of death and a physical object still present in the bar. Whether or not the story is historically secure, it has all the ingredients a durable haunt needs: a memorable setting, a repeated narrative and a feature visitors can see for themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/the-red-lion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: atlasobscura.com">[Atlas Obscura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">atlasobscura.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atlas Obscura The Red Lion in Avebury</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atlas Obscura The Red Lion in Avebury</span></span></span>

Workplace hauntings often work through a different social route: staff continuity. People who work late or enter restricted areas have experiences that customers rarely have, and their accounts can feel more credible because they are not necessarily trying to be entertained. The Bristol Fire Station story depends on this point. The reported witnesses are not tourists seeking a thrill but working crews in a functional building; the “haunted” interpretation then draws force from the contrast between modern emergency service work and an older sacred-military site history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bristol247.com/news-and-features/features/on-the-hunt-for-ghosts-of-bristol-past/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bristol247.com">[Bristol24/7]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bristol247.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bristol24/7On the hunt for ghosts of Bristol past</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bristol24/7On the hunt for ghosts of Bristol past</span></span></span>

Urban folklore research also helps explain why ordinary sites matter. Adam Hay’s work on British urban haunting argues that ghosts in towns and cities are not marginal curiosities but ways in which anxieties about place, memory and social identity get played out. In that sense, the haunted pub, office or converted hospital is not a lesser version of the haunted castle. It is a more intimate version: the ghost story appears inside the places where people drink, work, clean, commute and earn a living.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.supernaturalstudies.com/previous-journal-issues/vol-6-issue-2/hay" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: supernaturalstudies.com">[supernaturalstudies.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">supernaturalstudies.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on supernaturalstudies.com.</span></span></span>

## Natural explanations to check

Natural explanations do not make a haunting story worthless. They make it more readable. A careful approach asks what else could produce the reported experience before treating the story as evidence of a ghost. This is especially important in workplaces and pubs because they contain machinery, heating systems, refrigeration, cellars, extraction fans, plumbing, shifting temperatures, long shifts, alcohol, fatigue and low light.

Environmental research on “haunted house” experiences has reviewed factors such as lighting, air quality, temperature, infrasound, electromagnetic fields and embedded cues in the physical setting. The authors do not present these as a tidy debunking machine; rather, they note that research is still limited and that haunt-type experiences often arise from interactions between person, place and expectation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7304295/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAn Environmental Appraisal of “Haunted Houses”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PMCAn Environmental Appraisal of “Haunted Houses”</span></span></span>

A practical first check is the building itself. Old pubs and workplaces often have cellars, draughts, loose fittings, uneven floors, hidden service routes and old pipework. Refrigeration compressors, boilers, extractors and pumps can create noises that seem directional or timed. Light from passing vehicles can move across mirrors or glass. Doors may shift because of air pressure changes. Even a modern workplace can feel uncanny when it has long corridors, restricted areas, [after-hours]({{ 'after-hours/' | relative_url }}) silence and unfamiliar acoustic behaviour.

Air quality deserves particular caution. Carbon monoxide is colourless and odourless, and the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention describes it as a gas that can cause sudden illness and death, with prevention depending on safe appliances and detectors. Reports linking carbon monoxide to “haunting” experiences are often oversimplified online, but the safety point is real: headaches, dizziness, confusion, nausea or repeated odd sensations in a building should be treated as a possible health issue before they become a ghost story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdc.gov">[CDC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cdc.gov.</span></span></span>

Sleep and fatigue can also shape apparently supernatural experiences. Sleep paralysis occurs when a person is awake but temporarily unable to move, and recent medical reviews describe vivid hallucinations and a sensed presence as common features. This matters for haunted hotels, live-in pubs, night shifts and staff bedrooms, where experiences may happen while waking, dozing or exhausted rather than during fully alert daytime perception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11344621/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

Another check is expectation. The “Haunt” project, reported in Wired and based on a 2009 Cortex study, attempted to build an artificially haunted room using infrasound and electromagnetic fields. Many participants reported unusual sensations, but the pattern fitted suggestibility more than the specific environmental manipulations. That result is useful for everyday haunts because it shows how people can sincerely report strange sensations in a setting presented as uncanny without those sensations mapping neatly on to a physical “ghost field”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/2009/10/scientifically-haunted-house" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Scientifically Haunted House Suggests You&#x27;re a Sucker</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Scientifically Haunted House Suggests You&#x27;re a Sucker</span></span></span>

A sensible checklist for pubs and workplaces is therefore plain rather than paranormal:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Check safety first:** gas appliances, carbon monoxide alarms, ventilation, damp, electrical faults and structural issues.
2. **Map the timing:** whether reports happen after closing, during cleaning, near machinery cycles, in bad weather, or during staff fatigue.
3. **Separate witnesses:** whether multiple people independently reported the same thing before hearing the local story.
4. **Look for repeatable triggers:** mirrors, doors, cellars, pressure changes, motion sensors, fridges, heating systems or nearby traffic.
5. **Track how the story changes:** whether a vague report becomes more detailed after being retold as part of the building’s lore.

</div>

## Why the story persists

Haunted everyday sites persist because they are useful stories. They give a pub an identity, make a workplace memorable, add texture to a walking tour, explain awkward corners of a building, and turn otherwise ordinary maintenance problems into narrative. That does not mean everyone telling the story is cynical. More often, the story survives because it is enjoyable, socially sticky and flexible enough to absorb new incidents.

Tourism and hospitality make this persistence visible. Historic England’s haunted pub feature pairs ghost narratives with archive images and listed buildings, showing how spectral stories can sit alongside architectural and heritage interest. The National Trust similarly presents ghost stories as part of the visitor experience at historic properties, not as verified supernatural evidence but as part of the atmosphere surrounding [old buildings]({{ 'old-buildings/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/licence-to-chill-10-of-england-s-most-haunted-pubs/ugXByzhuRqdRKw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on google.com.</span></span></span>

In pubs, the story can even shape behaviour. People visit to ask staff about the ghost, take photographs in known spots, request haunted rooms, join ghost walks or sit near the reputed centre of activity. A story that brings visitors through the door is likely to be retold, refined and protected. Edinburgh research on ghost tourism notes that pubs and museums can incorporate ghost stories into their public identity and marketing, especially in cities where the “haunted” atmosphere is part of the visitor economy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theses.ubn.ru.nl/bitstreams/8662e4f3-13dd-4805-a5b8-f4fa30e40bbf/download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theses.ubn.ru.nl">[Radboud Educational Repository]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theses.ubn.ru.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Radboud Educational Repository Haunting atmosphere of Edinburgh</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Radboud Educational Repository Haunting atmosphere of Edinburgh</span></span></span>

But persistence also depends on ambiguity. The best everyday haunt stories are rarely too testable. A glass moved; a door opened; a tap ran; someone felt watched; a figure vanished too quickly to identify. These events are just clear enough to be told and just unclear enough to resist final settlement. If the explanation is found immediately — a faulty sensor, a colleague’s prank, a draught — the story often dies. If the explanation remains uncertain, it can become part of the building’s personality.

There is also a democratic appeal. Castles and mansions can feel remote, but a haunted pub or workplace says that the uncanny might live inside ordinary routines. The ghost may not appear to a noble family or a famous investigator, but to the person locking up, washing glasses, mopping a corridor or checking a boiler room. That is why everyday haunts are so durable within the wider subject of hauntings: they bring the supernatural down from the battlements and into the staff corridor.

## How to read an everyday haunt fairly

A fair reading avoids two easy mistakes. The first is credulity: treating every old pub tale or workplace rumour as evidence of a spirit. The second is contempt: assuming that every witness must be foolish, dishonest or attention-seeking. Most everyday hauntings sit in the middle. They are clusters of human experience, local memory and physical setting, sometimes with a documented historical building behind them and sometimes with only a late, embroidered legend.

The strongest cases for cultural importance are not necessarily the strongest cases for ghosts. The Red Lion at Avebury matters because it shows how a pub, a visible well, a prehistoric landscape and a repeated story can fuse into a powerful local identity. The Grenadier matters because a named ghost has become part of how some visitors and staff understand a living London pub. Bristol Fire Station matters because it shows a workplace haunting attached to working routines rather than to leisure tourism alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artsandculture.google.com/story/licence-to-chill-10-of-england-s-most-haunted-pubs/ugXByzhuRqdRKw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artsandculture.google.com">[Google Arts &amp; Culture+2The Sun]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artsandculture.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on google.com.</span></span></span>

The most useful question is therefore not simply “Is it haunted?” but “What is doing the haunting?” Sometimes the answer may be pipes, sleep paralysis, carbon monoxide risk, suggestibility, low light or a known hoax. Sometimes it may be grief, workplace stress, social bonding or the appeal of a good story. Sometimes the honest answer is that the event remains unexplained but not therefore supernatural.

Ordinary haunted places endure because they give everyday buildings a second life in memory. A pub becomes more than a pub; a cellar becomes a stage; a night shift becomes a story; a workplace becomes part of [local folklore]({{ 'local-folklore/' | relative_url }}). Within the broader study of hauntings, that is their distinctive value: they show how ghost reputations are built not only from grand tragedy, but from repeated human attention to the strange edges of normal life.

    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
