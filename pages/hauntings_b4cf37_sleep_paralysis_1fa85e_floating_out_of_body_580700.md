## Introduction

Not every bedroom haunting account involves a shadow figure standing by the bed. Some of the most striking reports centre on movement that never physically occurred: floating towards the ceiling, spinning through the room, falling into darkness, being pulled from the body, or watching oneself from above. These experiences are a recognised feature of sleep paralysis and related sleepwake transition states. Researchers often classify them as vestibular-motor experiences, meaning sensations involving balance, motion, body position, or apparent movement through space.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10487786/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hypnagogic and hypnopompic hallucinations during sleep...by JA Cheyne · 1999 · Cited by 409 — Hypnagogic and hypnopompic experienc...</span></span></span>

Because these sensations often occur while a person feels awake and can see the real bedroom around them, they can be interpreted as evidence of a spirit encounter, astral projection, or a haunting. The experience may feel more convincing than an ordinary dream because the bedroom environment appears real and familiar while the sense of bodily location becomes distorted. Understanding how these sensations arise helps explain why some haunting narratives focus less on ghostly figures and more on impossible movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18621363/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2Sleep Foundation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Medvestibular-motor hallucinations and out-of-body experiences</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">vestibular-motor hallucinations and out-of-body experiences - PubMedAmong the varied hallucinations associated with sleep paralysis...</span></span></span>

## Vestibular-Motor Sensations in Sleep Paralysis

Sleep-paralysis researchers have repeatedly identified a category of experiences distinct from intruder and [chest-pressure]({{ 'chest-pressure/' | relative_url }}) reports. Instead of sensing a threatening presence, the person experiences dramatic changes in bodily orientation and movement. Common examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Floating above the bed.
* Rising towards the ceiling.
* Flying through the room.
* Falling into a void.
* Spinning or rotating.
* Feeling pulled or lifted from the body.
* Viewing one's sleeping body from an external perspective.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10487786/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hypnagogic and hypnopompic hallucinations during sleep...by JA Cheyne · 1999 · Cited by 409 — Hypnagogic and hypnopompic experienc...</span></span></span>

</div>

Research led by James Allan Cheyne and colleagues identified these vestibular-motor experiences as one of the major patterns of sleep-paralysis hallucinations. Later work specifically examining out-of-body experiences found substantial overlap between reports of sleep paralysis and reports generated by disturbances in brain systems involved in body position and balance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10487786/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Hypnagogic and hypnopompic hallucinations during sleep...by JA Cheyne · 1999 · Cited by 409 — Hypnagogic and hypnopompic experienc...</span></span></span>

The term "vestibular" refers to the inner-ear and brain systems that help determine balance and orientation in space. During the unusual state between REM sleep and wakefulness, signals involved in body position can become mismatched. A person may be consciously aware of lying in bed while simultaneously experiencing vivid sensations of movement generated by dreaming mechanisms that have not fully switched off.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18621363/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Medvestibular-motor hallucinations and out-of-body experiences</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">vestibular-motor hallucinations and out-of-body experiences - PubMedAmong the varied hallucinations associated with sleep paralysis...</span></span></span>

## Why Floating Can Seem Paranormal After Waking

A floating sensation by itself might be dismissed as a dream. The difficulty arises because many episodes occur during hypnopompic awakeningthe transition out of sleepwhen the bedroom can still be perceived accurately. The witness may recognise furniture, windows, doors, and lighting conditions while also feeling detached from the physical body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-paralysis/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhs.uk">[nhs.uk+2Sleep Foundation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nhs.uk.</span></span></span>

This combination creates a powerful impression that something objective has happened. Instead of recalling a dream later, the person experiences the event as occurring in the actual room. Researchers describe sleep paralysis as a hybrid state in which elements of REM dreaming intrude into waking awareness. The result can be a convincing blend of real surroundings and internally generated movement sensations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11344621/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Ubie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recent Insights Into Sleep Paralysis: Mechanisms and ManagementSleep paralysis (SP) is a phenomenon wherein individuals awaken from de...</span></span></span>

Out-of-body experiences are especially persuasive because they appear to solve a puzzle for the experiencer: if they could see themselves in bed, it may seem that some part of them must have left the body. Neurological research, however, points to disturbances in the brain's representation of bodily location rather than evidence that consciousness literally departed the body. Studies of vestibular-cortex and body-representation systems have produced experiences closely resembling spontaneous out-of-body reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18621363/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2Ubie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Medvestibular-motor hallucinations and out-of-body experiences</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">vestibular-motor hallucinations and out-of-body experiences - PubMedAmong the varied hallucinations associated with sleep paralysis...</span></span></span>

The emotional intensity of the event can further strengthen paranormal interpretations. A vivid sensation of being lifted from the mattress or drawn across the room often feels more significant than an ordinary dream image because it involves the entire bodily sense of self.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18621363/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Medvestibular-motor hallucinations and out-of-body experiences</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">vestibular-motor hallucinations and out-of-body experiences - PubMedAmong the varied hallucinations associated with sleep paralysis...</span></span></span>

## Why Bedroom Settings Matter

The bedroom provides a uniquely convincing stage for these experiences. The person usually awakens in the same environment they occupy every night. Familiar landmarks remain visible, reinforcing the impression that consciousness is operating normally.

When the body remains paralysed while dream-related movement sensations continue, a contradiction emerges. Physically, the person is motionless. Subjectively, they may feel as though they are accelerating, levitating, rotating, or travelling through the room. The brain must reconcile these conflicting signals, and the resulting experience can feel profoundly real.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21974-sleep-paralysis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: my.clevelandclinic.org">[Cleveland Clinic+2NCBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">my.clevelandclinic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It goes away within seconds to minutes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cleveland ClinicSleep Paralysis: What It Is, Causes, Symptoms &amp; Treatment3 Jul 2024 — Sleep paralysis happens when you can&#x27;t move your bo...</span></span></span>

## How to Compare Body Sensations with Haunting Claims

Floating and out-of-body sensations do not automatically prove or disprove a haunting claim. They do, however, provide a useful [mechanism]({{ 'mechanism/' | relative_url }}) to consider when evaluating bedroom experiences.

Several features tend to align more closely with sleep-related vestibular experiences:<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.sleepfoundation.org/parasomnias/sleep-paralysis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sleepfoundation.org">[sleepfoundation.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sleepfoundation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sleep FoundationSleep Paralysis: Symptoms, Causes, and TreatmentJul 25, 2025 — Vestibular-motor (V-M) hallucinations: V-M hallucinations...</span></span></span>


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* The event occurred while falling asleep or waking up.
* The person was lying in bed.
* Temporary paralysis was present.
* The sensation involved flying, floating, spinning, falling, or leaving the body.
* The experience ended suddenly when full wakefulness returned.
* No physical evidence accompanied the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sleep-paralysis/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nhs.uk">[nhs.uk+2Cleveland Clinic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nhs.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nhs.uk.</span></span></span>

</div>

In contrast, a haunting claim becomes harder to connect specifically to vestibular-motor sleep phenomena if the reported events occur repeatedly during full daytime wakefulness, involve independent witnesses, or include evidence that is not tied to sleepwake transitions. Those issues require separate evaluation and fall outside the mechanism described here.

The key point is that unusual movement sensations are not rare anomalies within sleep paralysis. They are a recognised category of experience documented across studies, cultures, and personal accounts.
