## Introduction

Many reports of [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) in pubs, hotels, offices and other everyday workplaces begin at the same moment: closing time. Staff lock the doors, customers leave, background noise disappears, and a familiar building suddenly seems different. Footsteps appear to echo through empty corridors, a bang sounds deliberate rather than accidental, and a brief movement at the edge of vision can become a figure. Research into anomalous experiences suggests that these reports often emerge from the interaction between ordinary building behaviour, human perception and [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}) rather than from a single mysterious cause. The change from a busy environment to a quiet one alters how sounds are noticed, interpreted and remembered, making after-hours routines a particularly fertile setting for ghost stories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7304295/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An Environmental Appraisal of “Haunted Houses” - PMC - NIHby N Dagnall · 2020 · Cited by 43 — Still, there is a paucity of laboratory...</span></span></span>

## Footsteps, Bangs and Empty-Building Assumptions

The most common closing-time haunting is not a full apparition but a sound. Staff report [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}) crossing a floor above them, a chair scraping in a distant room, a door knocking, or a sudden bang from a cellar. What changes after closing is not necessarily the building itself but the context in which those sounds are heard.

During business hours, a creak is easily dismissed because dozens of people are moving around. After closing, the same creak occurs in a building believed to be empty. The listener immediately begins searching for an explanation. If no obvious source is visible, the noise can feel purposeful.

Buildings routinely generate sounds through thermal expansion and contraction. As heating systems switch off, temperatures change and materials such as [pipes]({{ 'pipes/' | relative_url }}), ducting, timber and metal framing shift slightly. These movements can produce tapping, popping and knocking noises that resemble footsteps or deliberate impacts. Acoustics can further distort the source, causing sounds to appear closer, farther away or in a different room altogether.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/whats-sound-my-house-making-matt-steinhausen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: linkedin.com">[LinkedIn+2Reddit]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">linkedin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Linked In What&#x27;s that sound my house is making?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What&#x27;s that sound my house is making?November 24, 2020 — One of the most common noises in homes is the sound of footsteps, and th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 24, 2020</span></span></span>

Older pubs and workplaces are especially prone to these effects. Large timber structures, cellars, stairwells and service corridors can transmit vibrations over surprising distances. A sound generated in one area may emerge elsewhere with little indication of its true origin. Once a location has acquired a reputation for being haunted, staff may begin interpreting recurring noises through that existing story framework. Research on haunted-house experiences repeatedly notes that environmental ambiguity and interpretation work together rather than acting independently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7304295/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An Environmental Appraisal of “Haunted Houses” - PMC - NIHby N Dagnall · 2020 · Cited by 43 — Still, there is a paucity of laboratory...</span></span></span>

An important feature of many workplace hauntings is that the original event is often mundane: someone hears footsteps, checks the area and finds nobody there. The absence of a visible cause becomes the memorable part of the story.

## Why a Noise Becomes a Figure

A closing-time haunting frequently develops in two stages. First comes an unexplained sound. Then comes a visual interpretation.

Human perception is not a passive recording device. The brain constantly predicts what might have caused a sensory event and fills in missing information. When a person hears what sounds like footsteps in an empty building, attention shifts toward finding a walker. Under uncertain conditions, that expectation can influence what is subsequently seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282948909_Suggestion_belief_in_the_paranormal_proneness_to_reality_testing_deficits_and_perception_of_an_allegedly_haunted_building" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Suggestion, belief in the paranormal, proneness to...The present study investigated whether suggestion, level of belie...</span></span></span>

## Fatigue, Low Light and Peripheral Figures

Closing routines usually occur when staff are tired. Pub workers, cleaners, security personnel, hotel employees and office staff often perform final checks after long shifts. Fatigue affects attention, visual processing and the ability to evaluate ambiguous information.

Low-light environments add another complication. Human vision becomes less detailed and more reliant on pattern recognition. Peripheral vision is particularly vulnerable to misinterpretation because it prioritises motion detection over fine detail. A coat hanging on a door, a reflection in glass, or a shadow cast by a changing light source can briefly appear person-shaped.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/how-architecture-affects-fear-and-perception" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">how architecture affects fear and perception</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National GeographicThe science of why some spaces feel haunted—even...29 Oct 2025 — Experts reveal how architecture—through walls, light...</span></span></span>

The sequence is familiar in many haunting accounts. Someone hears a noise, turns quickly and catches a glimpse of a dark shape. The shape disappears when viewed directly. Because the event unfolds rapidly, the mind naturally constructs a coherent explanation from incomplete information.

Research into haunted environments has also identified links between anxiety, expectation and the perception of unusual experiences. People who already feel uneasy are more likely to monitor their surroundings closely, increasing the chance that ordinary sensory events will be interpreted as significant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9216229/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCCase Study of Recognition Patterns in Haunted People</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by J Houran · 2022 · Cited by 16 — Primary percipient&#x27;s anxiety–fear levels during anomalous experiences correlated to situational fac...</span></span></span>

## Why Silence Changes Everything

One overlooked aspect of closing-time hauntings is the role of contrast.

A busy pub or workplace contains constant background noise: conversations, machinery, music, traffic, ventilation systems and movement. After closing, that acoustic masking disappears. Sounds that were previously unnoticed suddenly stand out.

A faint click from a pipe may become startling. Airflow through a corridor may resemble a whisper. Structural movement that occurred all day without notice becomes remarkable because it now occupies the entire soundscape. Acoustic researchers note that [reflections]({{ 'reflections/' | relative_url }}) and reverberation can make isolated sounds seem larger and more directional than they really are.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nti-audio.com/en/support/know-how/building-acoustics-in-general" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nti-audio.com">[NTi Audio Solutions]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nti-audio.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NTi Audio SolutionsBuilding Acoustics in generalThe causes can be due to the acoustics of the environment. ## How to Check Ordinary Causes First

Many experienced building managers, maintenance staff and investigators follow a simple principle: identify the physical source before accepting an extraordinary explanation.

Useful checks include:

* **Note timing patterns.** If noises occur when heating systems shut down or temperatures change, structural movement may be responsible.
* **Trace sound paths.** Stairwells, pipe runs, ducts and cellars can transmit noises across long distances.
* **Check lighting conditions.** Reflections, exterior vehicle lights and security lights often create moving shadows.
* **Compare reports.** Independent descriptions are usually more informative than stories repeated after discussion among staff.
* **Look for environmental [triggers]({{ 'triggers/' | relative_url }}).** Drafts, loose fittings, refrigeration units and ventilation systems frequently produce intermittent sounds.

Researchers examining allegedly haunted locations consistently find that environmental, psychological and social factors can combine to create convincing experiences without requiring a paranormal cause.
