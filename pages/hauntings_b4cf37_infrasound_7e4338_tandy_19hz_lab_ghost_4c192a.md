## Introduction

Vic Tandy’s 19 Hz laboratory ghost claim is one of the most frequently cited examples in discussions of [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) and infrasound. The story became influential because it appeared to connect a classic ghost experience—a feeling of dread, a sense of presence, and a fleeting apparition—to a measurable physical cause. According to Tandy, a standing wave of very low-frequency sound generated by an extraction fan in a laboratory may have produced unusual sensations and visual distortions that he initially interpreted as a ghostly encounter. The case helped popularise the idea that some haunting experiences might arise from environmental conditions rather than supernatural forces. At the same time, the episode remains suggestive rather than definitive, because key parts of the proposed [mechanism]({{ 'mechanism/' | relative_url }}) were inferred rather than experimentally demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

## Did a Fan Create Vic Tandy's Ghost?

### What Tandy reported in the laboratory

The incident took place while Vic Tandy was working in a laboratory associated with a medical equipment manufacturer. The room already had a reputation among staff for strange experiences. Tandy, who was sceptical of paranormal claims, later described feeling cold, anxious and unusually depressed while working alone one evening. He also reported a powerful sense that another presence was in the room. At one point he saw a grey, human-shaped form in his peripheral vision. When he turned to look directly at it, the figure disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

The next day, an unrelated observation changed how he interpreted the event. Tandy was working on a fencing foil that had been clamped in a vice. He noticed the blade vibrating strongly even though nothing appeared to be touching it. As an engineer, he suspected a resonance effect. Further [investigation]({{ 'investigation/' | relative_url }}) led him to identify a low-frequency standing wave in the room, which he attributed to a recently installed extraction fan. According to his account, the timing matched the onset of the laboratory’s reported disturbances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman+2Damn Interesting]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

In 1998, Tandy and psychologist Tony Lawrence published the case in a paper titled *The Ghost in the Machine*, proposing that a standing air wave around 19 Hz could under some circumstances create sensations that people might interpret as paranormal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

## How the 19 Hz Standing Wave Explanation Works

Tandy’s explanation depended on the interaction between low-frequency sound, room acoustics and human perception.

A standing wave forms when sound reflects between surfaces and reinforces itself at specific locations. Instead of spreading evenly through a room, energy becomes concentrated in predictable zones. Tandy concluded that the dimensions of the laboratory and the operation of the extraction fan had created a standing wave near 19 Hz. The vibration of the fencing foil was taken as evidence that a resonant frequency was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman+2IFLScience]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

The more controversial part of the theory involved the human body. Tandy suggested that 19 Hz was close enough to the resonant frequency of structures within the eye to produce subtle visual disturbances. Rather than creating a detailed hallucination, the vibration might slightly distort vision, especially in peripheral sight. A shadow, reflection or ambiguous visual stimulus could then be misperceived as a figure or presence. He also argued that low-frequency vibration could contribute to feelings of discomfort, unease and apprehension.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman+2Researchers Links]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

This combination of physical discomfort and ambiguous visual perception offered a possible explanation for why someone might feel haunted without any external supernatural event occurring. The claim was especially appealing because it linked a specific environmental source—a fan—to a specific experience in a specific room.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

## Why the Case Became So Influential

The Tandy case spread far beyond [psychical research]({{ 'research-files/' | relative_url }}) journals because it provided a memorable narrative. Instead of beginning with abstract theory, it centred on an engineer who believed he had encountered a ghost and then traced the experience to a mechanical cause.

The story also fit a broader pattern reported in allegedly haunted locations. Many witnesses describe:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A sudden sense of presence.
* Anxiety or dread without a clear reason.
* Feeling watched.
* Fleeting shapes seen at the edge of vision.
* Discomfort concentrated in one particular area of a room.

</div>

These experiences are often difficult to verify objectively, making environmental explanations attractive. Tandy later investigated other reputedly haunted sites, including a cellar in Coventry, where he again reported detecting low-frequency standing waves near 19 Hz. Those follow-up investigations strengthened public interest in the idea that infrasound might contribute to haunting reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/Something-in-the-Cellar.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman Something in the Cellar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanSomething in the Cellar - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 84 — This paper describes an investigation into the phenome...</span></span></span>

The case became a staple example in books, documentaries and articles discussing scientific explanations for ghost sightings. Even today, references to haunted buildings and infrasound frequently trace back to Tandy’s original laboratory experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vic_Tandy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vic Tandy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vic Tandy</span></span></span>

## Why the Case Is Suggestive but Not Conclusive

Although influential, the Tandy case does not conclusively demonstrate that 19 Hz infrasound caused a ghost sighting.

One limitation is that the event was largely retrospective. The apparition itself was not observed under controlled conditions. Tandy experienced something unusual and later developed an explanation after identifying the standing wave. The chain of causation therefore rests on inference rather than direct experimental proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>

Another issue concerns the proposed eye-resonance mechanism. The idea that frequencies around 18–19 Hz can influence vision has often been repeated in popular accounts, but researchers have debated how strong such effects would be under real-world conditions. Demonstrating a physiological effect is not the same as demonstrating the creation of a ghost-like image. The exact pathway from vibration to apparition remains uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010945208001299" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An attempt to build a “haunted” room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 85 — Tandy and Lawrence (1998) produc...</span></span></span>

Subsequent studies of haunt-type experiences have also produced mixed results. Some researchers consider infrasound a plausible contributing factor in certain environments, while others argue that [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}), suggestion, lighting conditions, fatigue and psychological context can be equally important. Later experiments attempting to recreate haunted sensations through environmental manipulation have generally treated infrasound as one possible influence among several rather than a complete explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010945208001299" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An attempt to build a “haunted” room by manipulating...by CC French · 2009 · Cited by 85 — Tandy and Lawrence (1998) produc...</span></span></span>

## What the Tandy Case Still Contributes to Haunting Research

The enduring value of the case is not that it solved hauntings. Rather, it showed that apparently supernatural experiences can sometimes be investigated through ordinary environmental factors.

Before Tandy’s report, discussions of haunted locations rarely focused on room acoustics. Afterwards, researchers and investigators became more likely to measure low-frequency sound, vibration and airflow in places associated with reports of [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}). The case helped establish a practical question that remains relevant today: when people feel a presence in a location, are they responding solely to psychology and expectation, or could subtle physical conditions also be shaping the experience?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.richardwiseman.com/resources/ghost-in-machine.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: richardwiseman.com">[Richard Wiseman+2Richard Wiseman]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">richardwiseman.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Richard Wiseman The Ghost in the Machine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Richard WisemanThe Ghost in the Machine - Richard Wisemanby V Tandy · Cited by 146 — Using the first author&#x27;s own experience as an exampl...</span></span></span>
