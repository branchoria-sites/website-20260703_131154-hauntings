## Introduction

The Wilmer haunted house case is one of the most frequently cited examples of a “haunting” that was ultimately explained by a physical cause rather than a supernatural one. Reported by ophthalmologist William H. Wilmer in a 1921 medical paper, the case involved a family who moved into an old house and soon began experiencing [apparitions]({{ 'apparitions/' | relative_url }}), phantom [footsteps]({{ 'footsteps/' | relative_url }}), feelings of dread, exhaustion and other disturbing phenomena. What made the account remarkable was that the experiences were eventually traced to carbon monoxide leaking from a defective furnace. More than a century later, the case remains a cornerstone example in discussions of carbon monoxide and ghost sightings because it combines detailed witness testimony, documented medical symptoms and a clear environmental explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002939421908250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effects of Carbon Monoxid upon the Eyeby WH Wilmer · 1921 · Cited by 29 — In 1921 Wilmer suggested a direct toxic effect on...</span></span></span>

## What the Family Reported

According to Wilmer’s account, the family moved into a large, ageing house that had stood largely unoccupied for years. The building was heated by an old furnace and illuminated by gas lighting, both common features of early twentieth-century homes. Soon after moving in, family members and household staff began reporting a collection of experiences that sounded strikingly like a classic haunting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002939421908250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effects of Carbon Monoxid upon the Eyeby WH Wilmer · 1921 · Cited by 29 — In 1921 Wilmer suggested a direct toxic effect on...</span></span></span>

## How the Furnace Explained the Haunting

The breakthrough came when the home's heating system was investigated. The old furnace was found to be releasing combustion gases into the living space rather than safely venting them outside. This exposed the occupants to carbon monoxide, a colourless and odourless gas produced by incomplete combustion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7304295/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAn Environmental Appraisal of “Haunted Houses”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wilmer W. Carbon monoxide interferes with the body's ability to transport oxygen. The brain and visual system are particularly vulnerable because of their high oxygen requirements. Exposure can produce headaches, dizziness, weakness, visual disturbances, confusion and, in severe cases, hallucinations or altered states of consciousness. Wilmer discussed the effects partly because of his interest in vision and eye symptoms associated with poisoning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journal.opted.org/the-journal-of-optometric-education/optometric-education-vol-38-no-3-2013/carbon-monoxide-and-the-eye-a-teaching-case-report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journal.opted.org">[Journal of Optometric Education]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journal.opted.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Optometric EducationCarbon Monoxide and the Eye: A Teaching Case ReportBoth acute and chronic exposure to carbon monoxide (CO)...</span></span></span>

## Why the Case Still Matters

The Wilmer case has endured because it occupies a rare position between folklore and medicine. Many ghost stories survive only as anecdotes, making them difficult to investigate retrospectively. In contrast, this account entered the medical literature and was discussed by a physician who connected the reported experiences to a specific environmental hazard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002939421908250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Effects of Carbon Monoxid upon the Eyeby WH Wilmer · 1921 · Cited by 29 — In 1921 Wilmer suggested a direct toxic effect on...</span></span></span>

Researchers examining environmental explanations for [hauntings]({{ 'hauntings/' | relative_url }}) continue to cite the case as one of the clearest examples of a poisoning event being mistaken for supernatural activity. Reviews of haunting reports frequently note that carbon monoxide has been convincingly implicated in only a small number of cases, but the Wilmer incident remains the most famous and best documented among them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7304295/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCAn Environmental Appraisal of “Haunted Houses”</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wilmer W. The case also highlights an important practical lesson. The occupants were not fabricating their experiences. They genuinely heard, saw and felt disturbing things. The significance of the case lies in showing that sincere testimony and unusual perceptions do not automatically reveal the cause of those perceptions.
