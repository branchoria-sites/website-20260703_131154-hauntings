## Introduction

Claims of ghostly voices occupy a special place in haunting reports because they seem direct and personal. A knock or a shadow can be ambiguous, but a voice appears to carry intention. Yet disembodied voices are also among the easiest haunting phenomena to manufacture, misidentify or reinterpret. Throughout the history of séances, poltergeist investigations and modern electronic voice recordings, investigators have repeatedly encountered cases in which ordinary speech, hidden sound sources, acoustic tricks or recording artefacts created the impression of a supernatural speaker. The central problem is not merely whether a voice was heard, but whether anyone can reliably establish where it came from, how it was produced and whether the recording conditions excluded ordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voice_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electronic voice phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronic voice phenomenon</span></span></span>

## Voices from Hidden Positions

A voice does not need to originate from the location where witnesses believe it originated. Human hearing is surprisingly vulnerable to errors in sound localisation, particularly in darkness, echoing rooms, crowded environments or emotionally charged situations.

In many historical séance settings, rooms were deliberately darkened. This reduced the ability of participants to verify whether a supposed spirit voice came from an empty corner, from a medium, or from an accomplice positioned elsewhere. Investigators of fraudulent mediumship repeatedly documented situations in which darkness, poor sightlines and audience [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}) allowed ordinary voices to be perceived as independent entities. The long history of [psychical research]({{ 'research-files/' | relative_url }}) includes numerous exposures of mediums who relied on concealed methods rather than paranormal phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.spr.ac.uk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spr.ac.uk">[spr.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spr.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Society for Psychical ResearchFounded in 1882, the SPR was the first organisation to conduct scholarly research into human experiences th...</span></span></span>

Haunting narratives can produce similar conditions. A witness may hear a whisper from another room, an outdoor voice entering through an open window, or speech reflected from a corridor. Once the expectation of a haunting is established, later retellings may transform an uncertain sound into a clearly remembered “ghost voice”. The evidential value of the voice then depends heavily on whether anyone independently verified the source at the time.

The problem becomes even more severe when only a single witness hears the voice or when reports are reconstructed from memory long after the event. In such cases there is often no way to separate an external sound, a misunderstood conversation, a perceptual error or deliberate deception from a genuinely unexplained event.

## Ventriloquism, Tubes and Acoustic Misdirection

One of the oldest methods of producing apparently disembodied voices is ventriloquism. Contrary to popular belief, effective ventriloquism does not require extraordinary skill. It relies on a combination of altered speech production, audience expectation and the tendency of listeners to misattribute a sound source. Research on the ventriloquism effect demonstrates that people can be led to perceive sounds as coming from locations other than their true origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15321" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Analysis of the Ventriloquism Aftereffect Using Network Theory Techniques</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Analysis of the Ventriloquism Aftereffect Using Network Theory TechniquesJanuary 16, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 16, 2026</span></span></span>

Historically, investigators of séance fraud often paid close attention to vocal performance because a skilled speaker could create the illusion that multiple personalities or spirits were communicating independently. In dim conditions, listeners frequently lacked the visual information needed to verify who was speaking.

Acoustic devices can amplify the deception:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Speaking tubes** can transmit speech from one location to another while obscuring the true source.
* **Hidden openings or ducts** can carry voices between rooms.
* **Reflective architecture** can make sounds appear to emerge from walls, ceilings or distant corners.
* **Concealed assistants** can speak from positions inaccessible to the audience.

</div>

These techniques are not merely theoretical. Architectural speaking tubes were common technologies before electronic intercom systems, and sound transmission through unexpected pathways has long complicated investigations of reported voices. The possibility of hidden sound routes means that a reported voice is strongest as evidence only when investigators can rule out concealed pathways and verify the physical environment.

Modern technology introduces additional forms of acoustic misdirection. Loudspeakers, wireless transmitters, hidden earpieces and remotely controlled audio devices allow voices to be projected without an obvious source. Because such equipment can be small and inexpensive, investigators who ignore environmental controls risk attributing technologically produced sounds to paranormal causes.

## Why Recordings Are Especially Vulnerable

Many contemporary ghost-voice claims involve electronic voice phenomena (EVP), in which faint sounds on recordings are interpreted as messages from spirits. The challenge is that recordings preserve sound but often do not preserve context.

Researchers and sceptical investigators have identified several ordinary mechanisms that can generate apparent voices:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Radio-frequency interference from broadcasts and communication devices.
* Cross-modulation between electronic components.
* Recording artefacts created during capture or playback.
* Residual sounds from previous recordings.
* Distortion introduced by noise reduction or enhancement software.
* Environmental speech that was not noticed during recording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voice_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electronic voice phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronic voice phenomenon</span></span></span>

</div>

A recurring issue is that many reported [EVP recordings]({{ 'evp-recordings/' | relative_url }}) contain heavy background noise. Under such conditions, listeners often disagree about what words are supposedly present. Studies of auditory perception have shown that expectation strongly influences interpretation of ambiguous sounds. This creates a subtle pathway from ambiguity to certainty. An indistinct sound may initially be described as “possibly a voice”. After repeated listening and suggestion from others, it becomes remembered as a clear sentence. The recording itself has not changed, but the interpretation has.

Experiments attempting to evaluate EVP under controlled conditions have generally struggled to demonstrate clear evidence that anomalous voices originate from discarnate entities. Researchers have reported hearing voice-like sounds but have also noted that such sounds remain highly open to interpretation and difficult to distinguish from ordinary recording anomalies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voice_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electronic voice phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronic voice phenomenon</span></span></span>

## What Makes a Voice Claim More Credible?

Because voices are so vulnerable to hidden production, strong investigations focus on eliminating opportunities for deception and contamination rather than simply collecting more recordings.

Useful controls include:

**Independent audio capture.** Multiple synchronised recorders placed in different locations can help determine whether a sound originated in the room, on a particular device or from an external source.

**Environmental monitoring.** Investigators should document nearby roads, neighbouring buildings, radio transmitters, public-address systems and other potential sound sources.

**Chain of custody.** Original recordings should be preserved and analysed before any enhancement. Edited files can unintentionally introduce artefacts or remove context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voice_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electronic voice phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronic voice phenomenon</span></span></span>

**Blind listening tests.** Independent listeners who are not told what words are expected provide a better measure of whether a recording genuinely contains intelligible speech. Studies of EVP interpretation have repeatedly highlighted the importance of reducing suggestion effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://michaelanees.com/s/Nees-Phillips-2015-Applied-Cognitive-Psychology-author-preprint.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: michaelanees.com">[michaelanees.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">michaelanees.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AUDITORY PAREIDOLIAby MA Nees · Cited by 51 — One type of ostensibly supernatural occurrence commonly featured in these shows is electron...</span></span></span>

**Physical inspection of the location.** Hidden speakers, acoustic pathways, ducts, tubes and concealed access points should be considered before extraordinary explanations.

**Simultaneous witness verification.** Reports become stronger when multiple [observers]({{ 'observers/' | relative_url }}) independently hear the same thing under controlled conditions and provide matching descriptions before discussing the event.

## The Core Fraud Risk

The central fraud risk associated with ghost voices is not that every reported voice is necessarily fabricated. Rather, voices can be produced, redirected, contaminated or reinterpreted with unusual ease. Sound travels invisibly, recordings can conceal their origins, and human listeners are highly susceptible to expectation and suggestion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_voice_phenomenon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2michaelanees.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Electronic voice phenomenon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Electronic voice phenomenon</span></span></span>

For that reason, disembodied voices occupy a category where ordinary explanations and deliberate trickery must be excluded with exceptional care.
